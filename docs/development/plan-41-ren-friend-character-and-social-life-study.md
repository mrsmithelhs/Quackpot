## Goal and owner direction

The owner selects **Ren as a friend to develop**, drawing on the Plan36 Opus proposal. Build a distinctive person and reciprocal relationship, not a reward dispenser or a spokesperson telling the player to quit. Social contact with friends/family may restore HP as a catch-all for the fictional character's well-being; frequently ignoring connections can endanger relationships. Some possession sales may affect relationships when a concrete shared context makes that consequence meaningful. Exact gains, thresholds, schedules and triggers are not selected.

Appearance direction: ambiguously gendered/androgynous presentation, a complexion and character heritage reflecting a Black/Latino mixed background, with possible tattoo and earrings. These are flexible directions, not a final model sheet. Treat heritage as characterization rather than something a face or skin tone must prove. Compare original designs with a small range of complexion/undertone, hair, dress and accessory choices without caricature or relying on stereotyped identity signals. Pronouns, exact age, background, occupation and personal history are owner choices if load-bearing; do not infer them from appearance. Friendship is platonic unless the owner separately chooses otherwise.

## Work plan and authorized latitude

1. **Short owner conversation and character brief.** Establish how Ren knows the character, what Ren enjoys/wants, speech and humor, reciprocal help, boundaries and presence channels. Offer recommendations with concrete examples and ramifications; let the owner decide genuinely consequential forks. Avoid a long identity questionnaire before showing useful ideas. Ren can enjoy ducks or games without endorsing every choice; disagreement need not make Ren morally superior.
2. **Reference research and visual concepts.** Research illustration/character approaches and relevant visual references, recording creator/source/license or usage limits and how each informs the design. Reference gathering is authorized; real students, private photos, scraped personal profiles and impersonation of a real person are not inputs. Do not copy a recognizable commercial character. Owner authorizes image generation as report-local concept inspiration; it is not production art. Read the actual harness image-generation workflow when using it. Start with a small curated comparison (for example, three directions with limited variations), not a combinatorial grid. Explain what differs. A small refinement pass within the selected brief is authorized; ask if quota/cost or a substantially new art direction is needed. If generation is unavailable, use researched visual briefs or original SVG studies rather than claiming images were produced.
3. **Compare at game scale.** Show the face/half-body or whole-body as appropriate, a few expressions and one apartment-context composition. Check contrast, readable gesture, fit with existing metagame art and wide/close framing. Distinguish phone portrait, message avatar and in-room presence needs. No full multi-angle rig or animation required. Preserve source prompts, model/tool metadata actually exposed and concept filenames. Final game assets must be authored as SVG in a later bounded adoption/art task; generated raster concepts stay in reports and out of runtime.
4. **Interaction menu with a small coherent arc.** Develop a few concrete opportunities: casual contact, a shared activity, practical mutual help, a declined/rescheduled invitation and a repair/reconnection. Bookshelf help/picnic are starting proposals, not required scenes. Include genuine enjoyment and quiet low-effort contact, not only expensive sacrifices. Compare optional activity with a gacha opportunity and ordinary work without auto-skipping commitments. A friend has their own availability, not an infinitely repeatable HP machine.
5. **Consequence alternatives.** Compare a small qualitative relationship state/flags with a minimal opportunity-based model; no affection meter or score is presumed. Explore HP restoration as a bounded action outcome, not simply opening a message. Separate not reading, declining, rescheduling, repeatedly breaking an agreed commitment and communicating a boundary. One reasonable refusal must not automatically damage the friendship. Recovery/reconnection should be possible; no real-time response deadlines or hidden cost for reading/accessibility.
6. **Possession-sale example.** Show one situation where a real sale has a concrete social consequence (shared seating/hosting equipment or a previously established joint plan) and one ordinary sale with none. Do not automatically punish every sale, equate financial distress with being a bad friend, imply Ren owns the player's possessions, or fabricate co-ownership/history just to attach a penalty. Explain material consequences before a commitment when selected action-time rules require disclosure; character reactions can remain nuanced.
7. **Owner review and delivery.** Work through ordinary drafts/research/concept revisions directly with the owner to a coherent recommendation. A labelled report-local HTML storyboard may use selected concepts and synthetic states, with no game storage/runtime imports that mutate state; no new renderer/framework/dependency. Ask for approval of significant new behavioral scope. Return exact asset/module/state/event needs as a future integration proposal; never write production merely because a concept is liked.

For each interaction record eligibility, phase/maintenance-or-anchor classification, commitment, displayed information, effect (symbolic until selected), once-per-opportunity limits, save/reload, Uninstall/static equivalent and Receipt mapping. Separate depicted character habits, actual player actions and displayed narration. No inferred loneliness, addiction, intent or influence in factual records. If relationship changes affect future invitation eligibility, surface the proposed rule clearly before adoption.
