## Goal and why this exists

Make a race collision noticeable and identify its participants, then distinguish an actual injury from ordinary collision recovery. The owner reports that the current impact/recover motion is too subtle during playback. An attractive close-up alone is insufficient: the effects must read with six moving ducks, upright labels, drama lighting and the existing race camera. Injury mechanics remain under Plan55 investigation; this study must not turn a visual candidate into a selected game rule.

### 3. Small controlled gallery

Start with two collision silhouettes: an angular comic/paper burst and a compact splash. Include dust as a third contrast only if it helps the owner choose; label it as a candidate despite the water setting. Compare shape alone, participant whitening alone and their combination. The recommended initial whitening is one quick onset followed by a smooth roughly 0.5-second return; compare a roughly 1-second return as the owner's suggested longer accent. Timings are adjustable candidates, not a new injury/recovery duration.

Whiten only the actual involved digital duck artwork, keeping the silhouette/outline legible; never flash the entire scene or labels. Do not introduce automatic repeated blinking or repeated collision playback by default. Any replay/loop is deliberate and stoppable. Keep accepted recovery/layer separation visible; if emphasizing it is useful, compare current versus report-local amplification without overwriting accepted motion.

Compare two persistent injury marker families: a paper bandage attached to the duck and a compact contrasting bandage/status tab beside its label. Avoid relying only on color or continual blinking. Include a textual `Injured` equivalent in status/details and narrative. Marker placement must work across colors, accessories, facing changes and Y-axis turns; define whether it follows the artwork or stays upright in screen space. Do not invent limp/blood or alter physical companion art.

Provide independent controls for collision style, whitening duration and injury marker, with a few named recommended combinations rather than a sprawling mandatory matrix. Use the same event timing and duck arrangement across comparisons. Show:

- close-up and actual production race-size views with six ducks and labels;
- normal lighting and current drama/podium dimming context, preserving visual hierarchy;
- collision with neither injured, only either participant injured, both injured, and an already-injured duck colliding again;

- an accessible static/reduced-motion equivalent and keyboard-operated playback controls.

Use explicit authored event fixtures first. Keep `collision participants`, `injury onset`, and `current injury state` separate. The gallery must not load or write owner game storage.
