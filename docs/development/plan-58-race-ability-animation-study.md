## Goal and why this exists

Develop a coherent visual language for automatic race abilities: the owner should be able to recognize what a duck is doing, who is affected and when an ability is active or has ended. Use duck motion plus local water/wake/air effects as appropriate. Combinations must remain readable at ordinary six-duck race size. The owner explicitly asks for Sonnet and Flash to contribute creative ideas, followed by Haiku and/or Sonnet drawing help and a rendered gallery. Art should inform future implementation without selecting untested mechanical advantages by accident.

## Work plan and gates

Map each candidate's intended action, trigger, affected duck(s), activation/active/end states, motion/effect needs, unresolved semantics and source authority. Cover Burst and Guard from the first study, plus Wide Flap, Dive, Flap Ahead, Draft, Straight Shooter, Bounce, Quick Recovery, Crowd Surge (working name), Steady Nerves and Wake Break. These are review candidates; include overlap/merge/defer recommendations rather than implying twelve final abilities. In particular, the original whole-race Burst rating proxy is not automatically the visual flight ability Flap Ahead.

Return exact files/reuse pins, synthetic event fixture structure, gallery composition, consultancy transport/input/output limits and serial resource envelope. Root clears this before provider calls or executable gallery work. After clearance, perform routine implementation and bounded revisions autonomously; meaningful owner visual/semantic decisions can occur in the implementer chat. No approval request per SVG or ordinary code edit.

### 2. Sonnet and Flash creative consultation

Give both consultants the same accurate concept brief, rendered digital rig/race references, ordinary race display size, paper aesthetic, multi-contact context, Plan57 collision/injury boundary and unresolved mechanic flags. Ask each for an initial independent treatment before sharing the other consultant's suggestions. This is creative diversity, not a formal blind evaluation. Then the Codex lead synthesizes and may reuse each chat for focused follow-up/context-rich critique. Verify actual image capability and transport from current Plan50 evidence and installed tools; do not assume that a filename in prompt text transmits an image.

Ask for a coordinated visual vocabulary across the whole candidate set, a few memorable treatments per family, collisions with existing cues, combined-ability readability, camera/facing/lighting limitations, end/cancel transitions and static/text alternatives. Invite alternatives, consolidations and warnings that a proposed animation promises more than the candidate mechanic does. Require rationale at race scale, not just attractive close-up imagery. No final numeric mechanic selection, provider claim treated as proof, or gratuitous research/subagent fan-out.

Keep context reuse purposeful: broad batch ideation is efficient; bounded subsequent questions are useful when the same consultant remembers the style and trade-offs. Use proportionate calls mindful of teacher subscription limits without a fixed subagent-count quota. Root reviews the concrete transport/model/permissions, staged inputs, bounded time/memory/output/spend and revision plan. No uncontrolled tools, credentials, private owner data, uploads or raw provider transcripts in the public repo. Record requested/observed identity, delivered image evidence and limitations honestly. Do not silently substitute providers if unavailable.

### 3. Original drawings and early gallery checkpoint

Use Haiku for bounded motifs such as wake strokes, splash/ripple shapes, wing accents and badges; Sonnet for complex compositions or coherent larger drawing families. The Codex lead chooses tasks based on the concept needs and returned quality. Give drawing helpers selected style guidance and explicit original-SVG deliverables, then reuse successful helper context for revisions. Helpers own disjoint report-local outputs or structured responses; Codex remains the single gallery integrator. Inspect scripts/handlers/external references/IDs and actual rendered quality. Mere code validity is not acceptance.

Create a first small animated batch for Bounce and crowd acceleration, plus a contrasting protection/positioning cue where useful. Show two visibly different treatments for the priority pair and one combination scene. Bring this early batch to the owner before expanding polished artwork across every candidate. Provide brief storyboards for remaining concepts at that checkpoint, so owner feedback can steer the shared style economically. Concept/decorative alternatives may be tested before Plan55 settles physics, but their fixture assumptions must stay explicit.

Possible directions to evaluate, not mandated selections:

- Airborne actions: readable lift, separation from a water shadow, wing/body paper-layer motion, takeoff/landing accents. Distinguish threat-responsive Bounce from deliberate passing in Flap Ahead; airborne contact rules are not decided by their appearance. An actual impact remains visible if it happens; anticipated contact alone gets no collision burst or injury marker.
- Dive: entry ripple, persistent visible underwater location and resurfacing cue; never make the player's duck impossible to locate. Bubble/trail style can communicate state without requiring full disappearance.
- Draft, crowd acceleration and Wake Break: distinct water-flow/acceleration motifs identifying source and affected entrants; avoid a false tether or guaranteed path when the mechanic has neither. A proximity cue is not proof that an avoided collision would have happened.
- Guard, Straight Shooter, Quick Recovery and Steady Nerves: protection, maintained heading, recovering heading and moderated self-wobble should have distinguishable cues or an explicit recommendation to merge redundant visuals. Guard protection is not an injury marker or healing promise.
- Wide Flap: wing movement and an expanding directional paper/water accent, with affected ducks identified only when the authored event includes them; do not make a non-injuring push look like a damaging impact.

### 4. Expand the selected language into the comparison gallery

After the early owner checkpoint, develop a base treatment for each retained candidate and alternatives where a meaningful uncertainty remains. Avoid a combinatorial collection of every color, timing and pairing. Group comparisons by useful families and present a few recommended combinations for judging clarity.

Show close-up and actual production race-scale six-duck views, crowded and dispersed situations, left/right facing and Y turns, upright labels, ordinary/drama lighting and narrow layout. Use the same duck arrangement and fixture across alternatives. Include activation, active state, end/cancel, unavailable/no-trigger control, post-finish cancellation where applicable, and distinct collision/injury context from Plan57. Test both events separated in time and a small set of simultaneous/overlapping combinations, especially Bounce plus crowd acceleration. No always-on full-scene light, automatic repeated flashing, color-only distinction or clutter masking the player's duck.

Owner direction2026-11-03: enlarged ability demonstrations are also useful for players inspecting a duck in the Duck-Dex or choosing a racer. Include report-local examples of those two explanation contexts with deliberate replay and concise player-facing descriptions. An enlarged demonstration must actually enlarge the duck/effect, not merely crop a race-size sprite. It may clarify source/target and activation/active/end states more fully than the race cue, while preserving the same visual identity and event meaning. Do not imply a guaranteed outcome or require the player to watch an animation to understand an ability. Duck-Dex/racer-selection production mounting remains separately scoped.

The prototype takes explicit time-coded presentation events and state. It does not roll ability chance, invent target effects or compute race rewards. Synthetic fixtures may depict proposed water/air paths, but reviewer-only captions must identify invented motion and unresolved landing/contact details. Expose useful tuning controls without disguising them as production UI. Default to deliberate playback, with pause/replay/seek and no autoplay audio or endless unattended effects.

Provide static/reduced-motion alternatives carrying the same ability/participant/state information. Text identifies actual activation and end, not inferred benefit. Keep markers legible across rarity colors/accessories. Pause/seek must reconstruct state without duplicate pulses; teardown removes nodes, timers/listeners and animation handles; instances do not share IDs or clock state. No game storage access.
