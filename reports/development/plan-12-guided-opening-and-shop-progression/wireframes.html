Pedagogical Analysis: Misunderstanding Risk Matrix Where learners stumble and how Quackpot protects them

1. Conflating Physical Companion with Digital Gacha Collectibles

Learner Risk: A student might think their desk duck "Bubbles" is their starting racer, or that pawning Bubbles deletes their gacha inventory.
UI Protection: Strict diegetic separation. Bubbles stays in the physical apartment on the desk blotter. Racing ducks live inside the smartphone's digital cards. The race roster strictly prohibits selecting the physical companion.

2. Believing Down Feathers Enable an Infinite Pull Loop

Learner Risk: Students accustomed to RPG recycling systems might believe duplicates fund continuous free pulls.
UI Protection: Mathematical transparency. The Down Exchange prominently displays: 10 Down per duplicate vs 1,000 Down for 1 pull ticket (a 100:1 loss ratio). The helper box explains that getting one ticket back requires sacrificing 100 duplicate ducks (worth 16,000 Gems).

3. Falling for Leftover Gem Traps (Currency Obfuscation)

Learner Risk: Believing a bundle that yields 1,750 gems is "extra generous" because of the 150 extra gems.
UI Protection: The neutral helper box explicitly states: "Leaves 150 Gems — exactly 10 Gems short of another pull!" In classroom debriefs, students realize that unspendable balances exist solely to provoke a second purchase.

4. Believing "400% Value" is an Objective Retail Calculation

Learner Risk: Assuming the publisher discounted a real $4.99 asset down to $0.99.
UI Protection: Disclosures clarify that the base price was arbitrarily set by the fictional publisher. The Receipt asks: "Did the '400% value' badge make this transaction feel low-risk, even though the regular price was arbitrary?"
