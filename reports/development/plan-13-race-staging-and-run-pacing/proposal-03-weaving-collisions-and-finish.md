# Plan 13 Proposal 03: rank-weighted weaving, contact recovery, and line finish

**Date:** 2026-10-26. **Disposition requested:** QP Orch review before any preview mechanic is changed. This is a proposal and hand-worked pacing analysis, not implementation evidence or approval to repair the preview.

## Recommendation

First compare a **tangent-only no-weave baseline** with a **lane-recentered no-weave control**. The current preview targets the local course tangent and has no cross-track correction; when a collision leaves a duck displaced from its assigned lane, tangent alignment alone does not return it to that lane. Only after measuring this controller change should the comparison proceed to **no weaving**, **symmetric weaving**, and **rank-weighted weaving**, all using the same lane-recenter controller. All six entrants are eligible in both weaving treatments. The symmetric treatment is the no-rank-weighting control; the rank-weighted treatment changes event cadence using rank frozen at the due checkpoint. Use the same racer statistics, course, choice, trial state, starting rows, contact rules, seed, and race ID in each comparison. Do not enable the existing catch-up sprint or leader-wobble assists; they would confound weaving. Do not add variant-specific speed or handling stats.

Use a bounded three-phase event: **acquire**, **hold**, then **recover and recenter**. Draw a path-relative target magnitude of 12–16 degrees. Acquisition ends only when actual heading is within 1 degree of that target and at least 10 degrees from the current lane tangent. Then hold for 0.5 seconds (10 ticks). If the duck cannot acquire the target within 1.0 second because the turn cap or a corner conflict prevents it, abort the hold and record the attained maximum; do not claim a 10-degree held deviation. At 10 m/s, 12 degrees held for 0.5 seconds corresponds to about 1.04 m of lateral travel before subsequent recentering.

The lane-recentered control uses curvature-aware tangent feedforward plus bounded cross-track correction. Let `e = lateral − assignedLaneOffset`, `θt = current local lane tangent`, and `θc = clamp(atan2(−e, 10 m), −20°, +20°)`. The desired heading equilibrium is `θeq = θt + θc`, recomputed each tick; the existing speed-dependent steering cap limits how quickly actual heading follows it. At zero cross-track error `θc = 0`, so the equilibrium is exactly the current tangent on straights and curves. This avoids a future-point chord that would be about 14.3 degrees off tangent on a 20 m radius bend with a 10 m look-ahead, even at the lane center.

Hand checks for the correction sign and zero-error behavior:

| Course point | Local tangent | Cross-track error `e` | Correction | Desired equilibrium |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Right bend, rightmost point | 90° | 0 m | 0° | 90° |
| Right bend, rightmost point | 90° | +0.5 m | −2.862° | 87.138° |
| Right bend, rightmost point | 90° | −0.5 m | +2.862° | 92.862° |
| Left bend, leftmost point | 270° | 0 m | 0° | 270° |
| Left bend, leftmost point | 270° | +0.5 m | −2.862° | 267.138° |
| Left bend, leftmost point | 270° | −0.5 m | +2.862° | 272.862° |

Positive error therefore turns toward the assigned lane center on both bends; negative error turns the opposite way. Define **yaw-recovered** as actual heading within 1 degree of the current controller equilibrium `θeq` for three consecutive ticks. Define **recentered** as `|e| ≤ 0.25 m` and actual heading within 1 degree of `θeq` for three consecutive ticks. Record both times; only recentering completes a recovery. These checks validate the sign and zero-error geometry, not closed-loop stability; lane overshoot, wall response, and recovery time remain for the controller-only headless comparison after approval. This is a mechanical controller proposal, not an animation.

Retain the current steering cap in the first collision comparison. Compare the existing fixed 6-degree response against one seeded 15–20 degree contact-response candidate. The impact normal determines each participant's outward sign; during its 0.5-second hold, the target is recalculated each tick as the **current local lane tangent plus that signed outward offset**, not a fixed world-space normal. Then recover and recenter under the same curvature-aware controller. Record immediate post-impulse heading, actual tangent-relative error, hold start/end, yaw-recovered time, and recentered time. Any added collision speed loss is a later gated comparison, excluded from the initial build; do not bundle it with yaw/recovery changes.

Adopt the owner-selected **first real forward finish-line crossing** as the proposed race result boundary. Interpolate crossing time within the 0.05-second tick; at that instant stop the shared simulation and rank every entrant by unwrapped progress, using the existing deterministic tie order. Do not move any duck to the line. An exact 20-second finish is not guaranteed; the two current-course headless examples fall within the owner's 17–23 second acceptance band, but they do not establish a broader finish-time distribution.

## Weaving contract candidate

| Rule | Candidate contract for QP Orch review |
|---|---|
| Comparison A | No weave events. Existing course, performance draws, collisions, and race choices remain the baseline. |
| Comparison B | Every entrant uses the same seeded 3.0-second event cadence, regardless of rank. This is the symmetric-weaving control. |
| Comparison C | Every entrant remains eligible. While in the frozen front half, use a 3.0-second cadence; while in the frozen rear half, use a 6.0-second cadence. Rank affects frequency only, not event size, speed, reward, or base stats. |
| Rank snapshot | At each due-time checkpoint, sort by unwrapped progress using the existing stable tie order and freeze the order before selecting any entrant's event. A rank change during movement affects a later due-time checkpoint, never the event already selected. |
| Schedule | Compute a shared keyed initial phase from `(seed, raceId, 'weave-phase', entrantId)` as `1 + floor(U × 60)` ticks (ticks 1–60, or 0.05–3.00 seconds) per entrant. Symmetric weaving sets `nextDue = dueTick + 60` every time. Rank-weighted weaving freezes rank at each due tick, then sets `nextDue = dueTick + 60` for the front half or `dueTick + 120` for the rear half. A rank change after that snapshot does not revise the pending due tick; for example, a rear-to-front change can wait the already scheduled 6 seconds. No backfill. |
| Common latent draws | Both weaving treatments use the same keyed phase, sign, and magnitude values for the same entrant/episode. Sign and magnitude keys are based on `(seed, raceId, 'weave', entrantId, opportunityIndex)`; omit treatment/model name and due tick so scheduling differences do not alter the next latent turn. Increment the opportunity index at every due check, including skipped checks, so paired comparisons keep the same latent sequence. Use an equiprobable sign and 12–16 degree magnitude. Performance draws remain unchanged. Rendering, zoom, lighting, slow motion, and narration consume no race RNG. |
| Due checks | At each due tick, freeze ranks once before resolving any entrant. Lane recentering is considered active when cross-track error exceeds 0.25 m after a disturbance; the controller remains present outside that state to aim along the assigned lane. If the entrant is in collision response, acquisition, hold, recovery/recentering, or another weave, record the opportunity as skipped and advance `nextDue` from this due tick using the cadence selected from the frozen rank. A collision cancels an active weave and takes steering priority; record cancellation. Do not overlap episodes. |
| Heading | During acquisition and hold, target `current local lane tangent + signed offset`, recalculated from the current progress at every substep. Hold 0.5 seconds only after actual heading is within 1 degree of its target and at least 10 degrees from tangent. Acquisition times out at 1 second; log timeout and peak attained error with no hold. Then recover to the curvature-aware equilibrium `local tangent + bounded atan2 cross-track correction` and record separate yaw-recovered and recentered times. |
| Limits and priority | Bound the line-race watchdog at 800 ticks / 40.0 seconds. No crossing by then is unresolved with no winner or payout. Allow at most 128 mechanical event records per race; reaching the cap makes that run unresolved rather than silently dropping events. Store scheduled-opportunity diagnostics separately, with a cap of 84 rows (six entrants × ceiling(800 / 60)); exceeding it also marks the run unresolved. This separates event-budget accounting from diagnostic eligibility logging. |
| Finish proximity | Do not force a reversal or schedule a special event near the finish. Only already-due seeded events can occur. A race terminates on the interpolated first forward crossing. |

The cadence is a candidate, not an accepted balance. Three and six seconds are due-opportunity intervals, not guaranteed activation rates: a due check skipped during acquisition, impact recovery, or lane recentering is not retried, so measured activation intervals can be longer. The same entrant can be front-half eligible in one interval and rear-half eligible in another. A rank-weighted outcome is not presumed to help or hurt the player; report results across seeds.

## Contact response candidate

The current model has a 0.05-second step, current steering cap `max(30°, 90° − 5° × speed_m/s)`, a fixed 6-degree opposite post-impulse heading rotation, and at most one duck-contact episode per race. At 10 m/s that cap is 40°/s, so the current 6-degree offset can yaw-align in about 0.15 seconds if no other event intervenes; it does not guarantee lane recentering. Preserve that cap in the initial comparison: a 20 m turn at 10 m/s needs about 28.6°/s, leaving only about 11.4°/s of steering margin. Lowering the global cap could prevent ordinary turn negotiation and would mix a course-feasibility change into the contact test.

1. **Control:** current restitution and positional correction, fixed 6-degree paired response, no additional speed penalty.
2. **Heading treatment:** keep restitution/position correction; use an independent keyed 15–20 degree outward offset for each actual participant. After the impact impulse, use the contact normal to choose each outward sign and target `current local lane tangent + signed offset`, recomputed as progress changes. Acquire that target under the existing steering cap, with the same maximum 1.0-second acquisition. Start the 0.5-second hold only after actual heading is within 1 degree of target and at least 10 degrees from tangent; otherwise record acquisition timeout and no held deviation. Then recover and recenter with the curvature-aware controller. Log immediate heading, actual attained offset, hold interval, yaw-recovered and recentered times, speed delta, and wall strike.
3. Any extra speed-loss treatment is deferred to a later gate; it is not part of this initial comparison.

The existing one-contact-per-race cap means this first proof cannot demonstrate chain reactions or a second duck contact. It can show a collision leading to a wall response if the resulting trajectory reaches a wall. If multiple contacts or cascades are later desired, they need their own eligibility, ordering, per-tick/event caps, and tests; do not describe the current cap as chain-reaction mechanics. The collision event must identify both actual participants, and the renderer should orient each duck from its simulated heading plus the oval-facing transform. The accessible narrative should describe the two participants and the measured recovery; never claim both lost speed because restitution can accelerate one while slowing the other.

## Finish-line rule and pacing fit

The owner selected a real forward line crossing. The candidate rule is:

1. Keep each entrant's shared world progress and the three existing staggered rows (0, −4, −8 m). A completed lap is the first tick interval where an entrant moves from progress below course length to progress at or above it.
2. For every entrant crossing in the current tick, interpolate `f_i = (L − progressBefore_i) / (progressAfter_i − progressBefore_i)`. The earliest crossing is the minimum valid `f_i`, not the first entrant encountered in array order. Treat crossing times within `1e-9` seconds as simultaneous; then apply the deterministic progress/lateral/player/ID tie rule at that same instant.
3. Interpolate every entrant's state at the winner's crossing instant and classify by unwrapped progress then the existing lateral/player/ID tie order. Only the first entrant has crossed at that instant unless another has equal crossing time; describe the rest as classification at winner time, not their crossing order. Apply unique-place payouts from this classification. Do not teleport followers. A finish that never crosses before the watchdog is unresolved with no winner/payout.
4. The start rows are 0, −4, and −8 m. Therefore, the rear rows intentionally travel 4 or 8 m farther to cross the same line. Keep those exact row starts identical across matched treatments and report this positional advantage; do not describe each entrant's distance as an equal one-lap distance.

The current course length is `2 × 60 + 2π × 20 = 245.664 m`. I ran the unchanged headless physics module in memory for the named seed `Quackpot Plan 13 named-seed example`, steady choice, 11.7 m/s player base, 11.5 m/s field base, no trial, baseline model, and R01/R03. I scanned the 30-second trajectories for forward crossings and interpolated within the first crossing tick. With the existing 60 m straights and 20 m radius, R01's A crosses first at 21.625 seconds; classification at that instant is A, B, P, C, E, D. R03's P crosses first at 21.822 seconds; classification is P, A, B, E, C, D. Both fall inside the owner's 17–23 second acceptance band for a 20-second target. These are two fixed seed examples, not a finish-time distribution. Each example produced a contact (R01 C/P; R03 C/E), so neither is a no-contact control for that exact run.

**Initial course candidate: retain the existing 60 m straights and 20 m turn radius, frozen across all treatments.** The two measured crossings already fit the accepted ±15% race-time band; shortening or rescaling the course is not justified by this small sample and could change contact eligibility. Do not open a tuning sweep. After approval, broaden seed coverage with this fixed course; report the actual distribution and return to QP Orch only if the band or handling fails. The previous constant-average extrapolation was too rough and is superseded by these interpolated headless results.

For the run-level floor, include displayed playback wall time as well as simulated race time. With three races, the two-seed crossing range above gives about 64.9–65.5 seconds of simulation total; adding the current maximum 2-second highlight on each race gives about 70.9–71.5 seconds of playback. This is an illustrative transfer of the two control times to three races, not a player timing result. Keep the 900-second floor a budget target until broad race-time and day-pacing evidence includes these highlight extensions.

## Matched-seed evidence plan

First use a no-weave, fixed-seed comparison to measure tangent-only steering against the bounded cross-track controller; hand checks above cover both bends and both correction signs. Then run the same named seeds and race IDs, opening choice, field speed inputs, trial state, frozen course, start rows, and contact response through no-weave, symmetric-weave, and rank-weighted-weave arms using the recenter controller. Preserve shared phase/sign/magnitude keys across the two weave arms; performance draws remain identical across every arm. Select no-contact and contact examples only after verifying the frozen course's actual event records. No per-variant stats, payouts, awards, or concealed player advantage.

Report, per seed and treatment: due tick/rank bucket; shared phase and latent sign/magnitude key; skipped, canceled, or activated opportunity; acquisition duration and attained error; held deviation/duration; yaw-recovered and recentered times; time/distance off lane center; duck and wall contacts; participants and speed deltas; interpolated first-crossing time; same-instant classification (and which entrants actually crossed); tie/payout; player place/reward; and diagnostic/mechanical event counts. Summaries should include median and range, plus how often rank or reward changed against the no-weave control. Include verified no-contact seeds and no-effect controls. Do not claim random weaving guarantees sign alternation, a pass, a close finish, or an upset.

Presentation evidence is separate from mechanical evidence: simulated heading changes affect duck orientation; camera framing eases around the recorded event; background dimming, participant glow, and scene-frame accent are cosmetic cues; reduced-motion and accessible narrative expose the same committed event facts. Report the full playback duration distribution as finish time plus configured highlight wall time, separately from simulated finish-time distribution. None of these cues may draw from the mechanical PRNG or alter the world trajectory.
