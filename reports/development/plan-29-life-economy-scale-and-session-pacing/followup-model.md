# Follow-up model and pre-run contract — 2026-10-31

This is a finite sensitivity investigation, not selected prices. Three configurations, six deterministic strategies, no seeds, seven days maximum; Day4 deadline explicitly branched. Money in cents, initial HP 100, no interest or new bills in seven days; this omission limits recovery conclusions. Fictional take-home pay is for an illustrative four-hour afternoon shift, not a legal or tax claim. Three scheduled shifts D1–3 and D4–6 are an experimental calendar, not an adopted schedule. No overtime assumed. Full rent and amount previously paid are unspecified: the listed remaining balance was partially paid before the run, but no amount or in-run earlier payment is invented.

Breakfast and dinner coexist with anchors. Seven meals are considered through D4 breakfast. HP skips −5, late play −10 at next wake, free leisure +10 (cap 100), illustrative bedrest to 25 once on collapse. Collapse cannot erase the deadline: record deadline cash both on arrival and after breakfast; pay/hold branches are both shown. When an unaffordable meal is encountered the experiment skips it; actual forced choice/order remains open. Work follows any morning bedrest, as an explicit optimistic ordering; no attendance strike inferred from morning rest. Three misses terminate scheduled work, no invented odd jobs, no overtime after termination. Credit is an optional debt principal sufficient to cover a shortfall, no interest simulated; sale is one fictional $40 item with nonnumeric social consequence. These cannot establish an indefinitely available escape. No inferred player motives.

Six strategies: no purchases; repeat small purchases (one $0.99 starter D1, then $4.99 packs D2/D3); mixed (one starter, D2 leisure, D3 gacha); missed work (skip D2 shift, starter plus D2/D3 packs); fatigue/recovery (three late nights, D2/D3 skip meals and D4–7 leisure); sale/credit (miss D2/D3, buy starter/one pack, sell once D3, if short consider separate credit branch). Misses and strategy events are specified choices, not simulated player decisions or outcomes. Days 4–7 are an illustrative continuation with available D4–6 shifts unless terminated, voluntary rent paid when affordable after breakfast for the main trace, and no new purchases; hold and credit branches are shown separately, no interest/new rent. The no-sale routes do not silently sell. Formula before payment: initial + completed wages + sale receipts − meals − purchases − other modeled fixed obligations (zero); reserve after unpaid rent = cash − remaining balance. A negative reserve is a shortfall, not negative cash.

Coupled deterministic cash/Gem ledger: initial promotional ten-pull grants 10 pulls directly, consumes zero purchased Gems. One starter is $0.99/240 Gems, bundle is $4.99/330 Gems; single costs 160 Gems, ten costs 1600; no Gem-to-cash conversion. Run D1–3 gacha sessions, each with a parameter of up to two paid single pulls; this is a computational cap, not selected session policy. Two aggregate race rewards are independently sensitized at 0, 120 or 240 Gems each (low/base/high; illustrative, not source-verified payouts); award after D1 and D2 single-pull actions. Track purchased cash, granted pulls, each session's paid pulls, Gems earned/spent/remaining, and remaining affordable singles/ten-pulls. No pity, rarity, Down, duplicate, individual race results or self-funding probability modeled. Restrained may still use the free introductory ten-pull and receives no simulated race Gem income. Display actual action counts rather than infer purchases imply draws.

Reserve threshold is arithmetic sensitivity: with all seven meals affordable, no sale, no other charges, no starter, count n=$4.99 purchases from cash after meal and rent; compare one missed $W shift. This independent capacity calculation permits hypothetical repeated purchases beyond the three-session sample; it does not claim such a purchase schedule or pull usage is authorized by a future session contract. Wage-only sensitivity holds all other control values unchanged and substitutes 60 or 90 for 10 on three completed shifts, adding $150/$240 to the reserve; it is not a coherent full-scale budget.
