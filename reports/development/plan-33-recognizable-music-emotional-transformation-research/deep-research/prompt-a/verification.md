## 3. Summary of Dispositions Across Prompt A Reports

1. **Kuusi (2009) Stimulus Count, Manipulation, and Confounds (`ChatGPT-A` vs. `Claude-A` / `Gemini-A`; Review 01 Item 1):**
   - `ChatGPT-A` reported 14 analyzed tunes; `Claude-A` and `Gemini-A` reported 19 tunes. Full-text verification (`https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstreams/37dba1fe-eff1-44a1-9d0d-78e7548cedef/download`) confirms **19 familiar tunes were selected and 14 were retained for the statistical analysis**.
   - Kuusi never replaced original harmony with clashing chords, and `Gemini-A`'s claim that the rhythm+harmony condition featured an "incorrect melodic contour" is unsupported (the melody was omitted).
   - **Review 01 confound repair:** The ~15–33 percentage-point gap between the intact tune (95.9% / 93.6% / 86.5%) and the altered isochronous melody conditions (76.1% / 67.4% / 53.3% melody-only; 81.3% / 68.0% / 53.9% melody+harmony) is **descriptive and confounded** by (a) simultaneous presence of original harmony + rhythm in the intact version, (b) presentation order (the intact version was always presented *after* the altered versions), and (c) conditional trial selection (altered-condition scores excluded tunes the participant failed to recognize in the intact version). We report these descriptive differences and confounds rather than claiming an isolated causal rhythm penalty.
2. **Liu (2018, 2022) Provenance & Scope (`ChatGPT-A`; Review 01 Items 2 & 3):**
   - `argon-f1` marked Liu (2018) as `full text`, whereas SAGE's full text (`10.1177/0022429418776936`) is behind a subscription paywall and no open full-text witness was identified. We reconcile Liu (2018) to **`[E-abs]`** (verified official abstract: $N=80$, 34 pairs, 3 harmonic tasks, conservation above chance) plus **`[E-sec]`** (secondary-reported result that primary→diatonic substitutions `ii, iii, vi` preserved melody conservation better than primary→secondary chords).
   - Furthermore, Liu's diatonic substitution comparison does **not** directly validate modal mixture (`iv`, $\flat\text{VI}$), chromatic clusters, or frightening arrangements; those are explicitly marked as `[H]` / craft choices in our synthesis.
3. **Jimenez, Kuusi, & Ojala (2022) Task Misrepresentation (`Gemini-A`):**
   - `Gemini-A` claimed listeners judged melodies as identical despite radical re-accompaniment. In the actual paper (`10.1525/mp.2022.40.2.89`, $N = 120$), the vocal melody was fixed and participants rated whether the block chords matched the **original song's harmony** (Target vs. Lure vs. Clash). Rejected for melody identity.
4. **Authorship of the 2018 *Music Perception* Drone Paper (`ChatGPT-A` vs. `Claude-A`):**
   - **J. Jeffrey Bostwick, George A. Seror III, and W. Trammell Neill (2018)** is the true author list for `10.1525/mp.2018.36.2.243`; `ChatGPT-A` misattributed it to Ronald S. Friedman, who authored the follow-up paper **Friedman (2019)** (`10.1037/pmu0000237`).
5. **Trevor, Renner, & Frühholz (2023) DOI & Correlational Design (`Claude-A` vs. `Gemini-A`; Review 01 Item 2):**
   - **`10.1121/10.0016857`** (`https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36732275/`) is the correct DOI (`Gemini-A`'s `10.0016847` is a typo). The study used listener ratings/classification of 100 existing horror-film excerpts followed by correlational acoustic analysis—not isolated experimental manipulation of single musical parameters.
6. **Farbood (2012) & Farbood & Price (2017) Paradigm Scope (`Gemini-A`; Review 01 Item 2):**
   - `Gemini-A`'s claim that loudness failed in 65% of Farbood (2012) trials misrepresents stationary-pitch control trials. Farbood & Price (2017) (`https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/Farbood_Price_2017.pdf`) tested **isolated 2-second synthesized sound pairs**, showing roughness, inharmonicity, and spectral flatness increase tension in paired tone judgments, which we extrapolate (`[H]`) to accompaniment voices without making discomfort a listening target.

## 4. Remaining Evidence Gaps After Prompt A Integration

1. **No parametric "identity-loss threshold" for a newly composed looping game hook:** No study tests a newly learned 8-bar game loop across repeated play while its accompaniment steps from diatonic (`Base`) to borrowed minor/modal (`Uneasy`) to unresolved/rough (`Dire`).
2. **Dissonant/non-tonic pedals and modal mixture under a fixed melody remain `[H]`:** Bostwick et al. (2018) and Friedman (2019) tested modal/tonic drones, and Liu (2018) tested primary-to-diatonic and secondary chords rather than minor modal mixture or dissonant pedals.
3. **Accompaniment-only bass register drop vs. whole-passage transposition:** Huron et al. (2006), Granot & Eitan (2011), and Eerola et al. (2013) transposed entire passages downward rather than dropping only the bass under a fixed-register melody, and laptop speaker audibility requires direct hardware checking rather than relying on a frequency formula.
