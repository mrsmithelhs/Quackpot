## 1. Explicit Resolution of the Gemini B Opening Claim on Physiological Arousal

**The claim to resolve:** `gemini-research-b.md` opens with:
> *"Empirical research demonstrates that adaptive game audio significantly modulates subjective tension and physiological arousal compared to linear soundtracks, even when musical tension is mapped inversely to game events [1]."*

Later in `gemini-research-b.md` (Source Table row 2, Empirical Study table row 3, and §"Experienced Tension and Arousal"), Gemini B asserts that **Klimmt et al. (2019, Study 2)** in *Alien: Isolation* measured *"Physiological: Salivary cortisol assays across three intervals (baseline, gameplay, recovery)"* and found that *"Music condition generated significantly higher cortisol production."*

**Verification result (`REJECTED — CONTRADICTED BY PRIMARY FULL TEXTS`):**
1. **Plut & Pasquier (2019, *IEEE CoG*, `10.1109/CIG.2019.8847951`, `[E-full]` read in full by Sonnet from the paper PDF and verified by Argon & `argon-f3`) collected zero physiological measures.** It used 13 post-condition self-report sliders (0–1) with $N = 30$ participants:
   - Univariate self-reported **arousal** was **not statistically significant**. Only *tension* ($F(3,87)=3.662, p=.015, \eta^2_p=.112$), *soundtrack matching* ($F(3,87)=6.967, p<.001, \eta^2_p=.194$), and *soundtrack experience* ($F(3,87)=11.227, p<.001, \eta^2_p=.279$) were significant.
   - Pairwise tension contrasts were significant **only against the Neutral (static) music condition** ($p=.048$ for Inverse-Adaptive vs. Neutral; $p=.036$ for Congruent-Adaptive vs. Neutral), **not against Silence/None** (mean tension: $.55$ None, $.49$ Neutral static, $.61$ Inverse-Adaptive, $.62$ Congruent-Adaptive).
   - Contrary to Gemini B's claim that *"participants were aware of the presence of adaptive music"*, Plut & Pasquier explicitly noted that free-text responses did **not** contain any discussion of the music's adaptivity.
2. **Klimmt, Possler, May, Auge, Wanjek, & Wolf (2019, *Media Psychology* 22(5), 689–713, `10.1080/15213269.2018.1507827`, `[E-full]` verified by Argon & `argon-f3`) conducted zero physiological or salivary cortisol assays.**
   - Study 1 ($N = 68$ young men, *Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag*) and Study 2 ($N = 59$ young men, *Alien: Isolation*) used **self-report psychometric questionnaires only** (a 4-item horror experience scale, an 8-item game enjoyment scale, PANAS positive/negative affect, MEC Spatial Presence, and avatar identification), comparing **linear soundtrack present vs. absent (SFX only)**.
3. **Conclusion:** Neither Plut & Pasquier (2019) nor Klimmt et al. (2019) measured physiological arousal or salivary cortisol, and the systematic review by **Heikkilä, Danso, & Luck (2026, *PLOS ONE* 21(10):e0361519)** (`[E-full]`) confirms that physiological arousal data across the game-music literature are sparse and mixed.

*(Review 01 distinction on rejected claims: Where a primary source's full text was directly inspected and lacks the claimed measurement—such as Plut & Pasquier 2019, Klimmt et al. 2019, and Rogers & Weber 2019—the claim is rejected as **contradicted by the primary text**. Where a practitioner interview or blog post simply does not contain a specific numerical range—such as Gemini B's `0.5–2.0s` or `3.0–5.0s` crossfade figures—we reject the numbers as **unsupported by the cited source** rather than claiming to have proved no other interview ever mentioned them.)*

## 3. Summary of Contradicted vs. Unsupported Claims Across Prompt B Reports

1. **Physiological Arousal & Salivary Cortisol Claims — Contradicted by Primary Full Texts (`Gemini-B`):**
   - `Gemini-B` claimed Plut & Pasquier (2019) demonstrated physiological arousal modulation and claimed Klimmt et al. (2019, Study 2) measured salivary cortisol across three intervals in *Alien: Isolation*. Both `ChatGPT-B` and `Claude-B` reported that Plut & Pasquier used self-report only ($N=30$) and that Klimmt et al. measured self-reported horror/enjoyment/presence in a music-present vs. music-absent design. Full-text verification of both papers confirms neither collected physiological or cortisol data.
2. **Loop-Duration Annoyance Table — Contradicted by Primary Full Text (`Gemini-B`):**
   - `Gemini-B` attributed a quantitative table of loop durations (`<30s` → annoyance in 5–15 min; `1–2 min` → 30–60 min; `>3–5 min` → multiple hours) and a "<60 seconds causes acute annoyance within 15–30 minutes" rule to **Rogers & Weber (2019)**. Full-text inspection of Rogers & Weber (2019, $N = 737$) confirms it is a survey of general listening/muting habits containing zero loop-length experiments or duration tables.
3. **Shipped Crossfade Second Ranges — Unsupported by Cited Practitioner Sources (`Gemini-B`):**
   - `Gemini-B`'s table claimed `0.5–2.0s` equal-power crossfades for *NieR: Automata* (citing Okabe 2018) and `3.0–5.0s` unquantized crossfades for *Genshin Impact* (citing Chen 2020), while `ChatGPT-B` and `Claude-B` reported that no first-person composer source gave numerical crossfade durations for those titles. Neither Okabe (2018), Ueda (2017), nor Chen & Cai (2020) contains those numbers, so they are rejected as unsupported by the cited sources.

## 4. Remaining Evidence Gaps After Prompt B Integration

1. **No empirical audience study of a recurring theme degrading across authored story states:** Every empirical game-music study found (**Plut & Pasquier 2019**, **Klimmt et al. 2019**, **Gasselseder 2014**, and the 14 studies in **Heikkilä et al. 2026**) tested either music presence vs. absence or performance-driven combat/hazard adaptivity. The specific expressive device proposed for Quackpot—one recognizable gacha hook whose arrangement shifts from Base → Uneasy → Dire across authored story chapters—is supported by composer practice (`[P]`: *Genshin Impact*, *Hades*, *Celeste*, *NieR: Automata*) and music-perception studies (Prompt A), but remains `[H]` at the whole-session game level.
2. **No empirical optimum for crossfade duration or bar-quantized vs. brief-silence transitions:** Middleware docs (FMOD, Wwise) and composer accounts (`[P]`: Korb, O'Donnell) treat beat/bar quantization and short crossfades or pad bridges as standard craft, while the exploratory lab project (**McNamara et al. 2022**) found brief silence between areas scored slightly higher on flow than crossfades without strong statistical significance. Crossfade duration and transition style remain art-direction choices.
3. **No parametric loop-length tolerance curve:** While **Rogers & Weber (2019)** (`[E-full]`), **Dutta (2020)** (`[E-abs]`), **Worrall (2025)** (`[E-abs]`), and **McNamara et al. (2022)** (`[E-full]`) indicate that verbatim short-loop repetition can cause overfamiliarity and muting, any specific loop-fatigue benefit in Quackpot is `[H]` and must be checked by ear.
