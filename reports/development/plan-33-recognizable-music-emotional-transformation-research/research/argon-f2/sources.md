## Claim-to-Source Map

- **Claim 1 (Trevor et al. 2023 DOI, sample, stimuli, and metrics):** Supported by Source [1]. Resolves DOI conflict (`10.1121/10.0016857` correct; `10.1121/10.0016847` was a typo from another paper in the same JASA issue). Verifies N=99, naturalistic commercial horror excerpts (FEARMUS), and exact acoustic numbers for event density (3.190 vs 2.176), RMS loudness (0.194 vs 0.172), and loudness variability (0.561 vs 0.513).
- **Claim 2 (Acoustic profiles of Anxiety vs. Terror):** Supported by Source [1]. Uneasy/anxious fear is characterized by high loudness variability and sparse texture; dire/terrifying fear is characterized by higher event density, harsh roughness, bright spectral energy, and higher mean loudness.
- **Claim 3 (Drone authorship, modal shift, and emotional valence):** Supported by Source [2] (Bostwick, Seror, & Neill 2018, correcting ChatGPT-A's misattribution to Friedman et al.) and Source [3] (Friedman 2019). Demonstrates that drones define perceived modal center and minor-mode drones heighten sadness, but neither demonstrates threat induction from static tonic pedals alone.
- **Claim 4 (Hudson & Wang 2025 pedal classification):** Supported by Source [4]. Confirms this is music-theory taxonomy (`[T]`), not an empirical listener study.
- **Claim 5 (Register and Pitch Height as Threat/Tension signals):** Supported by Source [5] (Granot & Eitan 2011) and Source [6] (Huron et al. 2006). Transposition to lower octaves systematically increases perceived threat, dominance, and tension across trained and untrained listeners.
- **Claim 6 (Timbral roughness and inharmonicity driving tension):** Supported by Source [7] (Farbood 2012), Source [8] (Farbood & Price 2017), and Source [9] (Pressnitzer et al. 2000). Roughness and inharmonicity reliably increase musical tension independent of tonal syntax.
- **Claim 7 (Refutation of Gemini-A's 65% loudness claim):** Supported by Source [7] (Farbood 2012) and Source [5] (Granot & Eitan 2011). The claim that "loudness shifts alone fail to drive perceived tension in 65% of test trials" misrepresents Farbood's discussion of stationary pitch controls. Both sources show loudness change is a primary driver of perceived tension.
- **Claim 8 (Orchestration and expertise divergence):** Supported by Source [10] (Silvey 2011). Timbre shifts affect listener tension, but nonmusicians and musicians exhibit an inverse response to sparse piano versus dense ensemble textures.
- **Claim 9 (Irrelevance of 18 Hz infrasound to student browser audio):** Supported by Source [11] (Scatterty et al. 2026). While 18 Hz infrasound produces endocrine stress in laboratory settings, it is an inaudible acoustic wave that cannot be reproduced by standard laptop/mobile speakers (which cut off at 100–150 Hz).
- **Claim 10 (Cadential withholding suspends closure but does not drive dire arousal):** Supported by Source [12] (Smit et al. 2020), Source [13] (Sears et al. 2020), and Source [14] (Choi & Fujioka 2025). Deceptive and withheld cadences reliably manipulate expectancy and closure, but isolated cadence types do not drive threat or high arousal without accompanying timbral or dynamic disruption.
- **Claim 11 (Syncopation does not drive negative tension):** Supported by Source [15] (Keller & Schubert 2011). Syncopation increases positive enjoyment and happiness rather than negative tension or anxiety.

## Gaps

1. **Friedman (2019) Participant Breakdown ($N$):** Accessed via comprehensive abstract and methodological/results summaries on APA PsycNet and ResearchGate; exact demographic sample size $N$ for Experiment 1 was behind APA PsycNet paywall. However, experimental design, independent variables (mode $\times$ tempo $\times$ accompaniment), and key emotional outcomes (sadness increase restricted to minor mode, no effect on peacefulness) were fully verified.
2. **Scatterty et al. (2026) Direct Infrasound browser test:** Full text verified in *Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience* (Vol. 20), but browser Web Audio rendering of infrasound was confirmed to be physically unfeasible on classroom hardware due to mechanical low-frequency transducer limits.
