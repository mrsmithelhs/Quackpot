# Task T2 — Creating tension while preserving a theme

Read `C:/AI/Quackpot/reports/development/plan-33-recognizable-music-emotional-transformation-research/research/common-brief.md` first; its limits and output format bind this task.

**Write only:** `C:/AI/Quackpot/reports/development/plan-33-recognizable-music-emotional-transformation-research/research/t2-tension/sources.md` and `.../findings.md`.

## Question

Which arrangement-level techniques make an unchanged (or lightly changed) melody sound more tense, uneasy, ominous or dire, and how strong is the evidence for each? Distinguish **music-theory description** [T], **composer/orchestration practice** [P], and **empirical listener findings** [E].

## Techniques to cover (one finding each where evidence exists)

- Harmony/voicing: reharmonization, minor/modal mixture, diminished/tritone and cluster voicings, added dissonance (b2, #4, b5), pedal points, sustained non-resolving chords. Mode (major/minor) treated as **one variable among many**, not a universal mood switch.
- Bass/drone and low register: sub-bass, drones, pedal tones, pitch-register lowering.
- Register/octave displacement of the melody; narrow vs. wide range.
- Density/texture: thinning vs. thickening; added layers; silence/rests; sparse versus saturated texture.
- Articulation/timbre: staccato/legato, attack/decay, filtering/brightness (spectral centroid), roughness/inharmonicity, detuning/beating, distortion.
- Rhythm/meter: tempo, syncopation, swing/straight, metric ambiguity, irregular meter, pulse irregularity, ritardando/stalling.
- Expectation and unresolved phrases: withholding the cadence, deceptive cadences, interrupted phrases, delayed resolution (e.g., work on musical expectancy such as Huron; Egermann et al.).
- Dynamics/loudness — and why loudness must be **matched** when comparing arrangements.
- Selective dissonance: dissonance perception being partly culture- and training-dependent (e.g., findings with non-Western or Amazonian listeners vs. Western listeners).
- Combining cues: additivity/linearity of cues (e.g., Eerola, Friberg & Bresin 2013 on primary musical cues).

## Look for (leads only — verify by opening each; ignore any you cannot find)

- Gabrielsson & Lindström on structure and expressed emotion; Juslin & Västfjäll on emotion mechanisms; Eerola et al. on cue contributions; Hevner-style adjective-circle research; Huron's expectation work; Egermann et al. 2013/2015 on expectation violation and physiological response; McDermott et al. (2016, Nature) on dissonance and culture; Bowling et al. on chord/vocal similarity; Fritz et al. (2009) on cross-cultural basic emotion recognition in music; film/horror-music studies (e.g., Lipscomb & Tolchinsky; Bullerjahn & Güldenring; Tan/Cohen on film music); meta-analyses of tempo/mode effects.
- Studies using melodies held constant while arrangement varies (manipulation of accompaniment/harmonization) — these are the most relevant; note if you find few.

## `findings.md` extra requirements

1. A compact **technique → expected impression → evidence type → strength/limits** list (plain text, not a table wider than ~6 columns).
2. A short list of **"subtle" vs "clear" shifts**: which parameters plausibly produce a subtle unease and which a clearly dire sound, with evidence labels. Mark inferences [H].
3. Explicit caution list: where results are context-dependent (culture, training, genre expectations, loudness/tempo confounds, short lab excerpts).
4. Recommendations for how a later small paired-listening comparison (same hook, three arrangements, loudness-matched) could vary one parameter group at a time.
