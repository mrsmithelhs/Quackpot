| **Hook pitches & rhythm skeleton** | Identical across states in Approaches 1 and 2; modified only in the deferred Approach 3 option | Melodic contour/intervals (`[E-abs]`: Dowling & Fujitani 1971) and surface rhythm/interval motifs (`[E-abs]`: Mélen & Deliège 1995) anchor recognition. | **Tempo & tonal center ("same key")** | Identical tempo and **identical tonic / tonal center** across states in Approaches 1 and 2; varied only in Approach 3 | Key ($\pm 6$ semitones) and tempo ($\pm 64$ bpm) shifts impair short-term recognition (10 min, 1 day) of unfamiliar melodies (`[E-full]`: Schellenberg & Habashi 2015; `[E-abs]`: Schellenberg et al. 2014). Defining "same key" as **shared tonal center** allows diatonic substitutions (`[E-abs]`/`[E-sec]`: Liu 2018) and modal mixture (`[H]`) without transposing the melody's tonic. |
| **Loop length & seam** | Identical bar count (8 bars of 4/4, $19.2\text{ s}$ at 100 bpm) and identical bar-line seam | Compares arrangement rather than form; enables sample-locked or bar-quantized transitions (`[P]`: Korb 2020; Okabe 2018). |

Separating low-pass filtering from detuning ensures a clean single-factor comparison (`[H]`). |

## 2. Approach 1 — Additive Shadow Layers (Baseline Reference Layout)

*Concept:* The calm Base arrangement continues playing unchanged; Uneasy and Dire fade in additional "shadow" stems underneath or around it. Preserves identity by construction because the exact Base stem never leaves, and provides the shared-stem engineering layout adapted by Approach 2.

| State | Anchors Retained | Parameters Altered (`[H]` Craft Proposal) | Hypothesized Impression `[H]` | Supporting Evidence & Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Base** | Everything (hook pitches, rhythm, tempo, tonal center, lead timbre, consonant chords, light bounce) | None | Bright, inviting, lightly bouncy, readable | Baseline reference; hook designed to pass HOYO-MiX's V0 bare-melody test (`[P]`: Chen & Cai 2020). |
| **Uneasy** | All Base stems intact | Fade in a low sustained tonic/minor-third pedal (`[H]` proposed 55–110 Hz + overtones) and a quiet high-register pad | Loses some comfort underneath a still-bright tune | Low drones induce modal centers where minor-3rd intervals sound more tense/sadder (`[E-full]`: Bostwick et al. 2018, $N=32$; `[E-abs]`: Friedman 2019); lower register increases tension/threat in transposed passages (`[E-full]`: Granot & Eitan 2011; Huron et al. 2006). *Limit:* Adding a drone under an unchanged major triad can sound muddy rather than modal. |
| **Dire** | Base lead and rhythm skeleton intact (optionally mute bright percussion) | Add a restrained dissonant/rough backing pad at matched integrated LUFS | Crowding or eerie irony ("the bright machine keeps playing over pressure") | In isolated 2-s tone/chord pairs, psychoacoustic roughness and inharmonicity increase perceived tension (`[E-full]`: Farbood & Price 2017, $N=24$; Pressnitzer et al. 2000, $N=17$). Additive stem stacking matches *Dead Space* / *Hades* craft (`[P]`: Graves 2011; Korb 2020). *Limit:* Because the bright Base chords remain audible, Dire may sound cluttered rather than sparse or bleak. |

## 3. Approach 2 — Accompaniment-First Reharmonization, Register Shift, and Single-Factor Lead Check (Recommended First Pass)

*Concept:* Share the **hook pitches, rhythm skeleton, tempo, and tonal center** across states, while **replacing** the bass and harmony accompaniment in Uneasy and Dire (`[H]` state progression). Aim **Base** for bright, inviting, read-friendly bounce; let **Uneasy** lose comfort without becoming horror; aim **Dire** first toward **restrained, sparse, unresolved pressure** (keeping denser/rougher backing as an optional backup if contrast is too weak). Compare **`Dire-Clean` (`2A`, untouched Base lead)** against **`Dire-Filtered` (`2B`, gentle low-pass filtering only on the same lead patch)**.

| State | Anchors Retained | Parameters Altered (`[H]` Craft Recipe) | Hypothesized Impression `[H]` | Supporting Evidence & Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Base** | Everything | Primary diatonic major triads (`I, IV, V`); warm mid-bass register; lightly bouncy, read-friendly accompaniment; resolved authentic cadence at bar 8 | Bright, inviting, comfortable during reading | Reference state (`[H]` art direction; V0 bare-melody check `[P]`: Chen & Cai 2020). |
| **Uneasy** | Hook pitches, rhythm, tempo, tonal center, lead patch, rhythm skeleton | **Diatonic substitutions (`ii, iii, vi`)** plus at most **one unexpected or borrowed minor-mixture chord (`iv` or $\flat\text{VI}$, `[H]`)**; **thinned, sparser mid-texture** over a **low tonic/minor-third pedal**; moderate dynamic swells (e.g., $\pm 6\text{ dB}$ proposed starting range `[H]` at matched `-16 LUFS`); **withheld half or deceptive cadence** at bar 8; **no groove syncopation** | Loses comfort without becoming horror; familiar tune over slightly unsettled ground | - **Identity under reharmonization:** Adults conserve melody sameness under changed harmony (`[E-abs]`: Liu 2018, $N=80$; Liu 2022, $N=192$), with higher accuracy reported for primary→diatonic (`ii, iii, vi`) than primary→secondary chords (`[E-sec]`: Liu 2018 Task 2). *Boundary:* Borrowed minor mixture (`iv`, $\flat\text{VI}$) is an `[H]` craft step beyond Liu's diatonic test.<br>- **Tension & texture:** One unexpected chord raises felt tension (`[E-abs]`: Steinbeis et al. 2006, $N=24$); low minor-3rd drones raise tension/sadness (`[E-full]`: Bostwick et al. 2018; `[E-abs]`: Friedman 2019); correlational analysis of horror excerpts links anxious cues with sparser density ($2.18$ events/s), hollow registers, and loudness variability (`[E-full]`: Trevor et al. 2023, $N=99$).<br>- **Cadence & rhythm:** Half/deceptive cadences delay closure (`[E-full]`: Sears et al. 2020, $N=40$; Choi & Fujioka 2025, $N=38$), while syncopation increased happiness rather than tension (`[E-full]`: Keller & Schubert 2011, $N=35$). |
| **Dire-Clean (`2A`: Clean Lead)** | Hook pitches, rhythm, tempo, tonal center, **exact Base lead patch** | **Restrained, sparse, unresolved backing (`[H]`):** exposed low pedal (proposed ~55–110 Hz + ~110–240 Hz overtones `[H]`), sparse dissonant/suspended accompaniment voicings (seconds/tritones or controlled roughness without harsh ear fatigue), and **unresolved loop seam** at bar 8 *(denser/rougher backing held as backup option if needed)* | Stark, cold, unresolved pressure; the untouched lead sounds isolated over sparse, tense backing | - **Sparse/restrained tension:** In **Silvey (2011)** (`[E-full]`, $N=40$), non-musicians rated a sparse solo piano arrangement *more* tense than full orchestra; lower whole-passage register increases threat/tension (`[E-full]`: Huron et al. 2006, $N=41$; Granot & Eitan 2011, $N=132$; Eerola et al. 2013, $N=46$); tone/chord-pair studies show roughness/inharmonicity increase tension (`[E-full]`: Farbood & Price 2017; Pressnitzer et al. 2000).<br>- **Limits:** Applying register drop and dissonance to accompaniment only under a fixed-register melody is an `[H]` recipe; secondary/chromatic chords carry higher false-negative risk than diatonic chords (`[E-sec]`: Liu 2018), mitigated by prior exposure to Base (`[E-abs]`: Pollard-Gott 1983). |
| **Dire-Filtered (`2B`: Low-Pass Filtered Lead Only)** | Hook pitches, rhythm, tempo, tonal center, **same lead synth patch (no detuning)** | **Exact same backing as `Dire-Clean` (`2A`)**, plus **gentle within-patch low-pass filtering only** on the lead voice (detuning is held separate for later if needed) | Cohesive, slightly dimmed, worn down; tests whether gentle filtering integrates the lead better than `Dire-Clean` | Within-family timbre similarity preserved recognition (`[E-abs]`/`[E-sec]`: Lim & Goh 2012, cited in Schellenberg & Habashi 2015), and game composers use low-pass filtering on active voices across state transitions (`[P]`: Korb 2020; Ueda 2017). Keeping detuning out of `2B` preserves a clean single-factor comparison (`Dire-Clean` vs. `Dire-Filtered`). |

## 4. Approach 3 — Re-Register, Modal Inflection, and Pulse Hesitation (Deferred Later Option)

*Concept:* Held in reserve unless the owner hears too little contrast in the 5-rendering first pass. Relaxes strict melody/pulse invariance in Dire by dropping the lead an octave, inflecting melodic 3rds/6ths toward minor while keeping the contour/rhythm pattern, and adding subtle beat hesitation.

| State | Anchors Retained | Parameters Altered (`[H]` Craft Recipe) | Hypothesized Impression `[H]` | Supporting Evidence & Limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Base / Uneasy** | Same as Approach 2 (or optional octave/minor shift in Uneasy if expanded later) | See Approach 2 | See Approach 2 | See Approach 2. |
| **Dire (`Dire-3`, Deferred)** | Hook contour shape and rhythm onset pattern | Octave-dropped + filtered lead; minor/Phrygian pitch inflection ($\flat 3, \flat 6$); slight tempo drag or beat-4 micro-hesitation; unresolved seam | Heavy, stalling, "the tune itself is wearing down" | Lowering the *melody's* register increased "scary" (`[E-full]`: Eerola et al. 2013, $\beta=-0.23, sr^2=.15$) and threat (`[E-full]`: Huron et al. 2006); minor mode is a strong Western cue (`[E-full]`: Eerola et al. 2013; Athanasopoulos et al. 2021 UK cohort, $p=.0074$) and matches *Celeste*'s Madeline→Badeline motif inversion (`[P]`: Raine 2018). *Limit:* Combining pitch/mode, register, timbre, and tempo changes increases recognition risk (`[E-full]`: Schellenberg & Habashi 2015; `[E-abs]`: Schellenberg et al. 2014). |

## 6. What This Matrix Deliberately Does Not Claim

No cell asserts that every student will feel a specific emotion, that any arrangement causes physiological stress, dopamine release, or compulsive behavior, or that roughness/dread should be made uncomfortable to listen to. Listener training (`[E-full]`: Silvey 2011; Kuusi 2009) and cultural enculturation (`[E-full]`: Athanasopoulos et al. 2021; `[E-abs]`: Liu 2022) moderate every cue. The Receipt records only which music state was *displayed*, never what the player felt.
