# Plan 33 — Base → uneasy → dire transformation matrix

Three bounded approaches for transforming one original hook. Cells give the **anchors retained**, the **parameters altered**, the **hypothesized impression**, and the **supporting evidence and its limits**. "Impression" cells are hypotheses about how an imagined listener might hear it **[H]**; none is a measured result for this hook. Evidence labels as in `recommendation.md`: **[E-full]** measured, read in full; **[E-abs]** measured, abstract/snippet only; **[P]** composer account; **[T]** theory; **[H]** hypothesis.

## Controls that apply to every comparison

| Control | Setting | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Loudness | Match integrated loudness (e.g., LUFS/RMS) and peak headroom across base/uneasy/dire of the same approach; check by ear at a fixed playback level | Louder dynamics push "scary" ratings up [E-full: Eerola 2013]; unmatched level would be mistaken for composition |
| Tempo | Identical across states in Approaches 1–2; varied only in Approach 3 and only in a separate pair | Tempo changes cost short-term recognition and add to other surface changes [E-full: Schellenberg & Habashi 2015; E-abs: Schellenberg 2014] |
| Key and hook pitches | Identical in all states | Contour and intervals carry identity [E-abs: Dowling & Fujitani 1971] |
| Loop length and seam | Identical length; same loop boundary position | Compare composition, not form; also required for any later crossfade |
| Lead timbre | Identical in Approaches 1–2 | Timbre change lowered recognition after all tested delays [E-full: Schellenberg & Habashi 2015] |
| Exposure | Same number of loop repeats per version in a test | Repetition fatigue is a [P]-reported risk |

## Approach 1 — Additive shadow layers (base stem untouched)

*Idea:* the calm arrangement is never altered; later states add layers on top of it. Cheapest to build as stems. Identity is preserved by construction because the base is still playing.

| State | Anchors retained | Parameters altered | Hypothesized impression [H] | Evidence and limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Everything | None | Inviting, bright, steady | Starting point; owner should confirm the base reads as pleasant/"enticing" rather than already tense |
| Uneasy | Melody, rhythm, tempo, key, lead timbre, all base stems | Add a low sustained drone; add a quiet thin pad with a mildly clashing interval (e.g., a minor second or tritone above the bass) at low level | Something is "off" underneath a familiar tune | Low register contributes to "scary" [E-full: Eerola 2013, melody register manipulated, not accompaniment]; dissonant intervals rated more negative/tense [E-abs: Costa et al. 2000]. **Drone/pedal untested empirically**; clash level and dissonance tolerance vary by culture and training [E-full: Athanasopoulos 2021]. |
| Dire | All of the above, plus the base melody and rhythm | Louder-in-mix is *not* used; instead add a second dissonant layer and a rough/detuned layer; optionally mute the bright percussion stem | Crowding, menace, "the same tune is still there but something is wrong" | Roughness distinguishes scream-like horror excerpts and tracks lower valence [E-full: Trevor 2020, 800 ms clips]; rough harmonizations convey energy/dominance/anger, effects small, one group reversed [E-full: Athanasopoulos 2021]. Density as a cue: stated only in a news summary, unmeasured [lead]. Risk: may read as "noisy" rather than "dire"; the unchanged cheerful base may sound ironic (a possible feature, to be judged). |

**Strengths:** lowest identity risk; trivially reversible; matches the composers' vertical-layering practice [P: Phillips]. **Weaknesses:** the ceiling for "dire" may be low because the cheerful base keeps playing; masking/headroom needs care.

## Approach 2 — Reharmonize and revoice the accompaniment (recommended starting set)

*Idea:* same melody, same rhythm skeleton, same lead; replace the chords, bass and voicing under it. Rhythm skeleton and lead stems are shared across states; bass/harmony stems are state-specific.

| State | Anchors retained | Parameters altered | Hypothesized impression [H] | Evidence and limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Everything | Consonant, bright-voiced chords; bass in a normal register; clear cadence at phrase end | Inviting, tidy | Reference state. |
| Uneasy | Melody, rhythm, tempo, key, lead timbre, rhythm skeleton | Modal-mixture or minor-leaning chords; bass moved lower; one *unexpected* chord at a phrase point; softer, wider-spaced voicings; the phrase end left slightly unresolved | Familiar but slightly wrong; "expectation not quite met" | Unexpected chord raised felt tension [E-abs: Steinbeis 2006, N=24, one-chord differences in Bach chorales]; different harmonizations of one melody changed ratings, culture-dependent [E-full: Athanasopoulos 2021]; mode a strong but not sole cue [E-full: Eerola 2013]. **Withheld cadence: theory only [T], no empirical test found.** Harmony as identity carrier: weak [E-abs: Dowling & Fujitani 1971; Kuusi 2009], but a *replacement* is not directly tested [H]. |
| Dire | Melody, rhythm, tempo, key, lead timbre | Low bass plus a pedal tone; cluster/tritone-heavy or mildly detuned voicings; no cadence (the loop restarts unresolved); sparse upper harmony so the melody is exposed | Bare, cold, menacing; the tune sounds "alone and wrong" | Low register and minor mode contributed to "scary" [E-full: Eerola 2013]; roughness [E-full: Trevor 2020; Athanasopoulos 2021]. Pedal/sub-bass/detune untested [T/H]; the stacked effect of several cues is assumed from approximately additive cue effects in a synthesized-excerpt study [E-full: Eerola 2013] but stacking arrangements was **not** tested there. |

**Strengths:** most direct use of the evidence; fewest identity-risking changes; stems are cheap (shared lead and rhythm). **Weaknesses:** the evidence that harmony changes preserve identity is indirect; requires a competent reharmonization — Plan 32 will need to confirm that the same hook still "fits" each harmonic language.

## Approach 3 — Revoice, re-register and destabilize pulse (highest risk, highest range)

*Idea:* accept some surface change to reach a more clearly "dire" sound: darker lead, lower melody register, slower and less steady pulse, thinner texture, unresolved ends.

| State | Anchors retained | Parameters altered | Hypothesized impression [H] | Evidence and limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Everything | None | As above | Reference. |
| Uneasy | Melody contour and intervals, rhythm skeleton, loop length | Melody dropped by an octave or lead filtered darker; slightly slower tempo step; sparser texture | "Tired," "dimmed" | Melody-register lowering raised "scary" [E-full: Eerola 2013]; but timbre change lowers recognition at all delays [E-full: Schellenberg & Habashi 2015] and tempo/key changes add to the cost [E-abs: Schellenberg 2014]. Octave displacement and lead darkening are not directly tested for identity **[H]**. |
| Dire | Contour and intervals only (rhythm skeleton loosened) | Further tempo drop or irregular pulse, rubato, wide silences, held unresolved notes, melody-off stem for some bars | Fragmented, stalling, "falling apart" | Composer accounts describe tempo and layer steps as urgency devices and melody-off stems as variants [P: Phillips; Kondo; Martins]; **no measured audience effect**. Irregular pulse/meter and rhythm: theory-level only [T]. Highest chance listeners say "that's a different tune." |

**Strengths:** widest expressive range; mirrors common composer practice [P]. **Weaknesses:** stacks several surface changes, which the identity evidence suggests accumulate [E-abs: Schellenberg 2014 additivity]; harder to compare fairly (tempo/loudness/timbre confounded).

## Quick comparison

| | Approach 1 Additive | Approach 2 Reharmonize | Approach 3 Revoice/destabilize |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity risk (hypothesized) | Lowest | Low–medium | Highest |
| Expressive ceiling ("dire") | Medium-low | Medium-high | Highest |
| Evidence directness | Medium (layer practice [P], dissonance [E-abs]) | Medium (harmonization [E-full], expectation [E-abs]) | Low–medium (register [E-full], rest [P]/[T]) |
| Build cost (stems) | Low | Low–medium | Medium (extra stems and tempo variants) |
| Pairs well with matched-loudness test | Yes | Yes | Needs tempo-matching option |

## Subtle vs clear shifts (hypotheses to test, not findings)

- *Subtle uneasy:* one unexpected chord at a phrase point; bass lowered by an octave; an unresolved last bar. 
- *Clear dire:* stacked cues in the same direction (low bass + pedal + roughness + no cadence) — additive stacking was seen for synthesized cue manipulations [E-full: Eerola 2013] but not for arrangement stacking.
- Mixed-state sanity check: play "uneasy" after "base" and "dire" after "uneasy" to hear whether the *steps* feel ordered, not just the endpoints. No evidence exists for gradual morphs.

## What the matrix deliberately does not claim

No row says a listener *will* feel a particular emotion; cultural background, training, genre expectation and listening context change the answer. No row implies anything about an actual player's motives or addiction. The Receipt may report which music state was *displayed*, not what a player felt.
