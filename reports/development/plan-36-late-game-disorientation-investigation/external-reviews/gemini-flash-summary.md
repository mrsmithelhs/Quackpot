## 1. Strongest Recommendation: Grounded Cognitive Spillover Over Control Sabotage

Our core recommendation is to anchor late-game disorientation entirely in **diegetic perception, attentional narrowing, and character rationalization**, while strictly preserving 100% mechanical reliability and responsiveness of player controls.

Earlier design seeds raised the prospect of erratic returns, bouncebacks, or obfuscated click targets to simulate cognitive fatigue. We strongly push back against any mechanism that makes real user interface controls lag, misclick, or secretly fail. For high school students in CourseVGD, control sabotage does not teach microtransaction design; it provokes input frustration and conflates software bugs with psychological manipulation. Furthermore, it directly undermines accessibility standards (`prefers-reduced-motion`, screen-reader navigation) and compromises the core pedagogical contract: *the game’s systems are transparently honest, and only the commercial framing is deceptive.*

Instead, the strongest disorientation vehicle is **Cognitive Spillover**:
1. **Rarity Grammar Intrusion:** The visual and linguistic taxonomy of the gacha (Common / Rare / Legendary frames, pity thresholds, countdown urgency) bleeds into the character’s perception of mundane apartment life—such as grocery items labeled like loot tiers, utility bills styled with "limited-time banner" borders, and medical or social obligations weighed strictly in "equivalent pulls."
2. **Audio Pareidolia:** Inanimate apartment sounds (a kettle whistle, street traffic, radiator clanks) fleetingly resolve into synthesized gacha chimes or duck quacks in the character's internal thoughts before fading back to reality.
3. **Cinematic Duck Estrangement:** The physical rubber duck remains completely inanimate and non-emissive, but the authored camera composition shifts across days—from warm, centered, eye-level framing on Day 1 to awkward, high-angle, shadowed, or half-obscured framing behind unopened mail in later days, reflecting the character’s growing avoidance of their original anchor.

## 2. Proposed Experience Arc: The Three-Phase Friction Loop

To comfortably sustain an engaged playtime of **30 to 45 minutes** across 5–7 in-game days without filler waiting or forced retention, we propose an arc built on **The Gradual Displacement of Fictional Life**:

```
[Phase 1: Novelty & Alignment] (Days 1–2, ~8–12 min)
  - Opening companion styling, free 10-pull, starter win, tutorial race.
  - Life metagame is manageable: breakfast costs $5, shift pays $10, rent is $15.
  - The phone is an exciting novelty on the desk; gacha is a delightful diversion.
  - Grounded Baseline: Full clear room, physical duck warmly framed, clean bills.

[Phase 2: The Squeeze & Cognitive Creep] (Days 3–4, ~12–18 min)
  - Rest mechanics (Plan 22) require roster rotation; Down upgrades tempt gems.
  - The life budget tightens: rent jumps, food choices emerge, shift fatigue begins.
  - Disorientation begins subtly: return transitions linger slightly; background ambient
    glow from the phone illuminates the darkened living room; bills adopt gacha urgency.
  - Unreliable Narration: Character rationalizes small $1.99 packs as "cheaper than eating out."

[Phase 3: Crisis, Divergence, & The Mirror] (Days 5–7+, ~10–15 min)
  - Divergent paths activate: System Optimizer vs. Quiet Recovery vs. Bankruptcy.
  - Disorientation peaks: Audio pareidolia in apartment; physical duck framed in corners;
    gacha music bleeds across room navigation until dismissed.
  - Terminal Horizon: The player chooses between pawn escalation, debt, or Uninstall.
  - The Receipt Debrief: Unpacks displayed cues vs. actual player actions without preaching.
```

## 3. Three Strategic Priorities

1. **Strict Separation of Player Agency and Character Rationalization:**  
   The human student must never be accused of being "addicted" or "confused." When the narrative describes the character thinking *"Just one more race before eating,"* the Receipt must only record the objective fact: *"A 2-minute decision window was displayed; you chose 'Enter Race' while Lunch was pending. Did this cue affect your choice?"*
2. **Action-Based Life Granularity (Plan 37 / 29 Coordination):**  
   Implement 3–4 discrete action slots per day (Morning, Midday, Afternoon, Evening). Gacha activities consume slots just like work shifts and grocery runs, forcing explicit opportunity costs (e.g. playing gacha during the morning slot causes breakfast to be skipped) without penalizing slow reading speeds or accessibility tools.
3. **Multi-Branch Narrative Dignity (Contrasting Arcs):**  
   Reject a single, moralizing trajectory of inevitable ruin. Provide at least two contrasting paths: the **System Optimizer** (who treats the game as a financial spreadsheet and sells possessions with chilling calculation) and the **Grounded Recovery** (who steps outside, engages with neighbors, and reaches a quiet, conscious Uninstall).

## 4. Main Pushback & Critical Challenges to Project Assumptions

- **Pushback on Sabotaged Controls / "Erratic Returns":**  
  We push back against owner seeds suggesting bounceback controls, simulated input lag, or horizontal phone orientation locks. Intentional navigation must always succeed on the first click. Disorientation should be conveyed through narrative text, camera framing, and transient visual overlays, never by degrading the browser software's input fidelity.
- **Pushback on Equating Engagement with Spending:**  
  A student who refuses to spend a single dollar and meticulously grinds races is engaging just as deeply with microtransaction design as a spender. The narrative and Receipt must validate F2P friction and completionist burnout with equal nuance.
- **Pushback on Real-Time Pressure in Fictional Life:**  
  Real-world elapsed timers must never drive apartment life consequences. All missed opportunities must stem from deliberate slot commitments under the action-based time model.
