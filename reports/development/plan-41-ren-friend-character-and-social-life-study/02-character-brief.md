# Ren — character brief (draft 1)

Labels: **[SELECTED]** owner-chosen (see `01-owner-selections-and-proposals.md`), **[DIRECTION]** owner-stated flexible direction, **[PROPOSED]** lead suggestion for the owner to accept, change or drop, **[OPEN]** needs an owner choice if load-bearing. Nothing here is a game rule, schema or asset.

## 1. Who Ren is

- **Neighbor down the hall** [SELECTED]. Ren lives in the same building. They notice the player character's door, the recycling, whether the lights are on.
- **Tinkerer and maker** [SELECTED]: repairs and builds things (shelves, bikes, a balcony herb box, fixing other people's lamps). Pencil behind the ear is a visual shorthand, not a uniform.
- **Pronouns: they/them** [SELECTED]. First-person message copy needs no pronouns.
- **Ambiguously gendered presentation; mixed Black/Latino heritage; possible tattoo and earrings** [DIRECTION]. Heritage is characterization, not something a face must prove. See §4.
- **Platonic friendship** [SELECTED default].
- **Adult, roughly late 20s** [PROPOSED, OPEN]: old enough to have a lease and a trade habit, young enough to share the player character's world. Exact age is not load-bearing; do not state it unless the owner wants to.
- **Occupation** [PROPOSED, OPEN]: part-time at a bike/repair co-op, fixing things on the side. Deliberately not the player's workplace, so Ren never sounds like a manager (Plan37's attendance warnings stay with the employer).

## 2. Voice and humor [PROPOSED]

- Short, lowercase-ish texts, concrete and practical: "got a drill if u need it" or "your hallway bulb's out again, i have spares".
- Dry, observational humor; teases the player character about the physical rubber duck ("is professor quacks supervising") without mocking the player. The duck stays an inanimate object (Hard constraint): Ren talks to or about it as a joke; the duck never responds.
- Ren likes games and ducks in a small way (a pocket puzzle game; a mug with a duck on it) and may play Quackpot's fictional world lightly. Ren **does not endorse or condemn** the player's pulls: they might say "nice pull?" or ask what the banner is. Disagreement, if any, is mild and about time ("i'll be at the shelf at 6, no pressure") and never moral superiority.
- Ren never lectures about money, spending, or leaving the game, and never narrates the player's feelings. No "are you okay?" diagnoses.

## 3. What Ren wants and gives [PROPOSED]

- Wants: finish the balcony herb box; get the second-hand bookshelf upstairs; a Saturday afternoon of not working.
- Gives: practical help (a tool, a fix), company without agenda, an invitation that is easy to say yes to.
- Needs: a second pair of hands now and then; someone who shows up, or says so when they can't. Reciprocity is about **showing up and communicating**, not about transaction counts.
- Boundaries: Ren has plans, a job, and tired days. They can say "not tonight," and sometimes the answer is "not this week." They will state a boundary once, plainly, instead of fading into hurt silence.
- Ren is not owed the player's money or possessions and does not own anything of the player's. A shared context (for example, the couch-and-foldout setup for a hosted evening) must be *established in a scene first* before it can matter; it is never fabricated to punish a sale (see the Possession-sale study).

## 4. Heritage as characterization [PROPOSED, OPEN]

The research warns against heritage as a single template or a face-only signal (see `research/visual-reference-provenance.md`). Recommended approach for the owner to accept or reshape:

- Show heritage in **specific, optional details of life**, not catchphrases or costume: a family-owned repair counter that taught Ren their craft; a chipped enamel mug from a grandparent that holds screws; a particular home dish Ren brings to a picnic; one or two phrases in Spanish used the way a real bilingual person would, rarely and naturally, with no gloss every time.
- Do not tie tastes, speech or work to a stereotype, and do not make heritage the character's only characterization.
- Offer **optional** specifics rather than making them canon: the owner picks which, if any, are written. Nothing about Ren's family or neighborhood is selected today.

## 5. Presence channels [SELECTED: phone + door/hallway]

- **Phone**: message avatar plus short text; a call log entry ("4 min"). Avatar must read at roughly 32–64 px (see concept page).
- **Door/hallway**: a half-body figure at the door with a short exchange. No new location art is assumed; the apartment kit's existing door/room framing is the reference.
- Not selected: outside scenes (park/rooftop), a multi-room Ren home, animation. Any move there is a separate owner decision.

## 6. What a first meeting could look like [PROPOSED sketch, not selected]

Early in Day 1 evening or Day 2 (timing is Plan37's call): a knock; Ren holds a small box ("welcome-to-the-floor spare bulbs?"), notices the duck on the desk, makes one joke, says "the shelf's still in my hallway if you ever want to help carry it." No obligation, no meter, no reward. The first impression is "someone who lives here," not "an HP source."
