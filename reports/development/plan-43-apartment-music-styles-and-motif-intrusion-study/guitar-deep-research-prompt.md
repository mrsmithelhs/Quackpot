# Optional deep research prompt — browser guitar synthesis

> **Question:** What is the most promising practical route to a recognizably guitar-like instrument in an offline, locally bundled browser music laboratory, with editable note/strum scores and finite rendering resources?
>
> **Context.** Our first dry same-score comparison used (A) a custom procedural renderer with worker-reconstructed GM-inspired recipes and (B) genuine upstream `g200kg/webaudio-tinysynth` at revision `3d75aee4b3f43cbd932265e7d60201fd5b770397`, including a disclosed offline-initialization adapter. The listener preferred A for a nylon guitar note and staggered chord but said **neither sounded like guitar**. Both engine and bank changed; this does not isolate patch data. No single engine has been selected for all instruments. We already tested a four-note chord with onsets 60 ms apart; do not recommend “add strumming” as though the baseline had simultaneous attacks. Guitar has separate timbre and performance problems.
>
> **Constraints.** Research only; no proposed runtime network, CDN, telemetry or account. Assets and dependencies must be locally bundled, documented and compatible with redistribution; art/audio promotion to the real game is a separate owner gate. No large soundfonts or sampled libraries may be acquired without a separate review. Report a range of feasible CPU/memory/voice costs as estimates unless actually measured. Do not assume exact live and OfflineAudioContext parity or that a Web Audio feedback graph works across the full guitar register. Keep the current two baselines unchanged.
>
> **Find and compare:**
>
> 1. Actual public **TinySynth instrument extensions or applications** that improve guitar timbre or playing, with direct links to implementation code, immutable revision, exact license and demonstrable audio if available. Distinguish verified guitar-specific implementation from a generic `setTimbre` example, GM program label, untested suggestion or unrelated synth. If no credible examples are found, say so and describe the search.
> 2. Concrete **other MIDI/browser synthesizers**: procedural/FM, Karplus–Strong/digital waveguide, and compact sample-based instruments. Trace each useful candidate to an exact repository/release and file-level license/source; note samples' independent provenance and modification/redistribution terms, size, browser execution and offline rendering support. Separate code and sound-license questions. Do not assert license compatibility from a repository label alone.
> 3. For acoustic and electric guitar separately, identify first-order audible cues and practical approximations: excitation/pick versus finger, note-dependent attack/decay and string/body resonances, string selection/fretboard voicing, strum direction/order and timing, note overlap/damping and muting, velocity, slides/bends/vibrato. For electric guitar distinguish clean pickup coloration from drive/amp/cabinet processing. Use primary papers, instrument-makers or performers and actual synthesizer code. Avoid unsupported exact parameter recipes and universal claims.
> 4. Propose the smallest useful owner-listening experiment that separates **timbre**, **performance**, and **effects**, preserving identical notes when evaluating timbre and explicitly labeling note/voicing changes when evaluating performance. Include a clean baseline, dry first, short single-note/chord/phrase excerpts, fair adjustable listening level, no silent padding, finite tails, and a stop/pivot rule. Explain what a positive or negative result would and would not establish.
> 5. For a procedural plucked-string candidate, compare precomputed PCM buffers, Web Audio native-node feedback and AudioWorklet against current Web Audio specifications and actual browser constraints; note event scheduling, polyphony, pitch accuracy, cleanup, memory and live/offline feasibility. Verify rather than assume numerical latency/quantum restrictions. Distinguish physical-model concepts from copying or adding a dependency.
>
> **Evidence format:** provide direct links, exact version/date and reading depth for every implementation or paper; separate measured/demonstrated evidence from inference, untested configuration and subjective listening. Include a concise comparison table, two or three specific experiments in priority order, named gaps and any license or provenance uncertainty. Do not invent proof of sound quality, compatibility, licensing or a TinySynth extension. No implementation, download, installation or production decision is implied.
