## B. Explicit, narrow offline initialization adapter — recommended feasibility candidate

Keep the exact upstream source and its actual engine/patches untouched. In the disposable iframe, after closing the constructor-created context, temporarily shadow **only that particular OfflineAudioContext instance's** `resume` method while synchronously calling upstream `setAudioContext`. During this call the shadow returns an already fulfilled promise and records each invocation; restore the original method in `finally` immediately after the call. Require exactly the two expected initialization calls, the context's state to remain suspended, and no other call while the seam is active; fail closed otherwise. This acknowledges that resuming a not-yet-rendering offline context has no useful scheduling role here; it substitutes behavior for a failing call, so it is an **explicit compatibility adapter**, not unmodified upstream API behavior or an upstream-supported offline guarantee. It does not suppress `unhandledrejection` globally and must not wrap later note scheduling, rewrite upstream, mask other errors, or treat failures as successes. A browser may disallow safely shadowing the method; in that case stop.

A new tiny one-note witness would first prove descriptor/restore identity, exact invocation count, no other errors, context states, timing, release, bounded resource and teardown, and compare it with the failed baseline. The initialization's own tiny note-on/note-off remains part of the upstream call; investigate its audible effect, do not silently remove it. Then, only if root clears the method and the witness passes, verify shared-score mapping and no voice stealing for at most 16 **total** notes per render; `notetab` includes future notes, so concurrent voice count alone does not justify prescheduling 128. Tail bounds, gain/compressor differences, source randomness and real/live versus offline parity remain distinct tests. If its controlled workaround passes, the result is genuine upstream engine and bank **through a modified context interface**; it is not a bit-identical reproduction or proof of upstream native offline support.

Cost: one isolated browser feasibility turn, one short offline render and small report-local adapter; no package, extra dependency, sample, service or long buffer. Browser memory remains outside Node's heap cap; retain one actual render lock until settlement. Full comparison only follows the previously cleared 8-file/40-second ceiling and separate finite browser coordination. The existing 300 ms callback observation and one closed context are limited evidence, not a general leak proof.
