## Event, tempo and voice boundaries

`comparison.js` uses seconds and MIDI integers, maps program 12/24/32 to zero-based channels 0/1/2, calls `setTsMode(0)`, `setProgram`, then `noteOn(channel,note,velocity,time)` and `noteOff(channel,note,time)`; Plan39's custom renderer receives velocity / 127. The 30s report-local example `melody-pilots-render.mjs` converts beat to seconds as beat × 60 / BPM at 100 BPM, rounds symbolic velocity ×127 for upstream, and preserves note durations as scheduled note-off times. A transcription importer must independently handle its file's actual tempo map, ticks, program/channel conventions, note ordering, overlaps, sustain and other MIDI events; these cases were not validated by the fixed-tempo pilot and cannot be inferred from it. For simultaneous same-pitch notes on one upstream channel, note-off matches active entries by channel/note: do not assume independent MIDI note identities; investigate and test a policy before relying on overlaps. Percussion has its own upstream source-stop rule and does not use ordinary pitched-note-off semantics.

Upstream `_limitVoices` considers future-scheduled pitched notes in `notetab`; a short concurrent-note estimate alone can silently prune future notes. First comparison bounded total scheduled notes to 15 with 16 voices; two 30s pilots used 96 voices and checked every scheduled pitch/onset/off marker before and after adding drums. Their rhythm notes are not ordinary pitched table entries. This does not certify arbitrary transcriptions, sustains, bends, controllers, dense MIDI or the general upstream polyphony model. Bound total scheduled events, operators, concurrent voices, graph nodes, per-node lifetime and memory **before** allocation; validate actual scheduling and prevent stealing. `guitar-pluck.mjs` accepts <=16 events, <=9s segment-local timeline, <=8 concurrent and <=3s notes. The 30s pilot segments it into three local batches and separately validates global offsets/concurrency; this is a bounded report adapter, not a general MIDI importer.

## Mix, export and known limitations

`selected-drum-arrangement-page.mjs` and `melody-pilots-render.mjs` combine upstream master .22 with guitar master .12 and Plan39 bass graph master .5 / bass layer .75; notes, velocity responses and signal paths differ by engine. TinySynth's output compressor and level-dependent mixing mean pre-compressor and post-render gains are not interchangeable. These are experimental balances, not calibrated loudness, LUFS, true peak, or normalized tracks. A transcription renderer must check resulting mix and seek owner listening rather than silently copying gains as universal defaults.

The two preserved 30s pilot WAVs/manifests and implementation are `melody-pilot-auditions/` and `melody-pilots-{score,render,runner}.mjs`; `melody-pilots-score.test.mjs` checks the limited score identity/bounds. Last-second peaks were nonzero even though final PCM samples were zero; neither establishes natural tail completion nor click-free looping. No seam was selected or audibly validated. Upstream noise uses `Math.random`, so drum-containing exports are not promised bit-identical; preserve actual files and hashes. No claim that normalized signals, frame counts or model feedback establish musical quality or fidelity. Earlier Flash described 14s clips as 1–2s despite later-window audio; transport vs perception unresolved (`flash-observations.md`). No usable subsequent Flash assessment is implied. A preview-page attempt to rewind paused players was not fully verified (`comparison-evidence.md`, `progress.md`).
