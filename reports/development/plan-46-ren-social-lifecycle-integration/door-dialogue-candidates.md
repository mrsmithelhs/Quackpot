# Ren dialogue direction — owner-review candidates

These are draft examples combining the same-session Sonnet consultation with Codex writing. They do not add mechanics, guarantee objects or adopt a line. Actual branches need review against the active life state and selected tone. Player responses are meaningful conversational flavors; where branches rejoin, a response changes Ren's immediate reply rather than secretly changing relationship points.

## First meeting

Ren: “oh. hi. i’m ren, down the hall. we keep passing each other, figured i’d say hi before it got weird.”

- **Nice to meet you** → Ren: “you too. i make and fix things. mostly i just wanted to say hello.”
- **What brings you by?** → Ren: “mostly saying hello. i make and fix things, and figured it’d be nice to know someone nearby.”
- **Not a great time** → Ren: “no problem. another time.” Close for now; return behavior is subject to the technical design.

After either opening response: **What do you like making?** → Ren: “depends what catches my attention. i like figuring out how things fit together.” Or **Glad you stopped by** → Ren: “me too. hallway introductions are surprisingly difficult to schedule.”

Ren: “here, write down my number. it’s good to know someone in the building. no pressure to use it.” Responses: **Thanks, Ren** → “sure. see you around.” Or **I’ll keep that in mind** → “sounds good. see you around.” No digits displayed, no plan reserved.

## Invitation and response

Ren: “i could come by {relative_evening} for {engine_activity}. no pressure.”
- **Sounds good** → Ren: “cool. {relative_evening}. i’ll knock.” Only after the existing acceptance succeeds.
- **Another evening?** → Ren: “how about {engine_available_evening}?” Use existing proposal and confirmation semantics; do not present a proposed time as accepted.
- **Not this time** → Ren: “all good. another time.” No claim that a particular future offer is guaranteed.

## Visit: door, then appropriate seating

Ren: “hey. thanks for having me.” **Come on in** → Ren: “glad we could make it work.” **Good to see you** → Ren: “you too.” Continue to the selected context. On a game visit: “i remember the rules being simpler than this. possibly because i was winning.” Responses can be friendly or competitive, with a distinct short reply before the common closing. Only mention the duck when the engine establishes its presence; it never responds. No unprovided props, controller or room details. Apply +5 HP only on actual completion, capped by the existing engine.

## Communicated cancellation and competing activity

When a player communicates: “okay. thanks for telling me. another time.” The existing conflict notice must also plainly distinguish telling Ren from going ahead without telling them, before the player makes the choice. Do not turn declining or an excused inability into a broken commitment. Rescheduling retains its existing actual confirmation boundary.

## Missed commitment and repair

Ren: “didn’t see you. let me know if another time works.” Possible replies: **I’m sorry I missed it** → “thanks for saying so.” **Can we talk?** → “yeah. we can make time.” Distinct words must not silently promise restored friendship. For a repair scene: “hey. thanks for asking me over.” **I’d like to talk** → “okay. i’m listening.” **Thanks for coming** → “of course.” Continue with a human conversation grounded in the existing repair state; don't imply an unnamed object gets repaired. Completion supplies the existing +5 HP, not immediate friendship restoration. Reconnection still requires the selected honored visit; breaking it without notice returns to repair under the chosen policy.

## Context changes and farewell

A couch sale affecting a scheduled game night receives factual advance disclosure and later floor reconfirmation; Ren should not claim the couch remains. A sold TV must stay absent from the floor scene. Farewells may vary by the previous response but cannot promise a future date, alter the plan or award health before completed contact. Later phone messages and physical visits may need different wording even where they share choices; do not imply that the physical door scene and a phone message are the same medium.
