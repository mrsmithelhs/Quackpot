## Owner-directed shelf and target adjustments

The owner said the shelf obscured the bed, requested moving the shelf and contents to the left edge, then requested its left edge extend out of frame while the robot's left edge remain visible. The owner said the farther placement looked better, and requested that the interaction target encompass the visible bed. The report-local composition shifts shelf, toys, surface and static duck together by 160 scene units and expands the bed target to x40–93% of the scene for turn-in and recovery. The original before captures remain in `captures/`; the first 100-unit move is in `shelf-move-captures/`; the farther version is in `shelf-offset-captures/`. The shelf's bottom remains cropped by the scene; narrow art remains small. No production art was changed.

The study renderer temporarily replaces the bedroom room spec with a cloned composition for one synchronous render and restores the original in `finally`; this is report-only and is not a recommended production integration mechanism. A focused browser check compared the original room spec's identity and relevant data after a successful render and after an intentionally rejected render; both matched. A later production proposal should use an owned, pure composition input or immutable variant, not mutate the shared spec.

The first moved-shelf witness recorded Escape closing the decision but **not** restoring focus, despite the process exiting 0. Its trace is retained as `shelf-move-first-trace.json`. The witness then separated the composition helper from the UI entry module and added an explicit assertion; the suspected duplicate UI initialization was not independently proven. The farther-offset witness exited 0 in 3.34 seconds, sampled peak 322,969,600 bytes, no runtime exceptions, and confirmed owned Chrome tree and profile cleanup. It recorded programmatic bed focus, dispatched Enter to open the decision, and Escape to close it and return focus. Sampled desktop/narrow widths had no horizontal overflow. This is not full Tab, assistive-technology, hardware or production behavior evidence.
