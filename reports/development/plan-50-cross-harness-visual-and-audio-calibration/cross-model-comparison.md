## Review burden, resources and limits

Each model produced 24 numbered findings across the five successful reviews, plus some contextual commentary; each needed human disposition, and checking time was not measured. Flash's sanitized final-response text totals about 24,248 characters and Haiku's about 17,605; length is not a quality measure and redaction/formatting can affect it. Both produced speculative or subjective design advice. In particular, recommendations to hide Uninstall in Settings conflict with the selected immediate-exit direction; a reviewer needs the game contract and must reject unsupported consequences, invented causes and factual errors.

The five successful Haiku launches total 84.139 seconds and the five successful Flash launches 270.826 seconds, serial launcher elapsed time excluding setup, timeout, reading and scoring. Flash required an owner-authorized larger bounded retry after case 1 timed out without a review; that 48.078-second failed attempt is separate and is not a quality failure. These elapsed figures are observed for this run, not model speed estimates. Haiku CLI reported 20 uncached input, 8,334 cache-creation input, 36,242 cache-read input and 12,502 output tokens, including 5,759 thinking tokens. Flash CLI reported 78,103 input and 27,754 output tokens, including 21,859 thinking tokens. The providers' token/cache accounting and settings differ; do not interpret these numbers as directly comparable billing or quota costs. Haiku's reported list-basis estimate was $0.00828222 for its five successful calls, not verified billing; no comparable Flash charge was observed. Effective effort was not independently confirmed for either model.

Each CLI reported its requested model family, and each successful review trace recorded the intended image read. Haiku's read event exposed image content; Flash's neutral synthetic image challenge correctly identified unseen contents, but its traces did not expose the actual image payload. Sibling-file denials are finite checks, not exhaustive confinement. Flash advertised broad tools even though no unexpected tool executions appeared in these cases; startup knowledge, effective policy merging and some access paths were not fully audited. The owner amended the Flash gate for this staged comparison with those limitations disclosed. Full local receipts contain machine/session information and are hash-identified in sanitized derivatives, not committed. Source art and stills do not validate actual runtime interactions, accessibility, devices or owner experience.

## Recommendation for review

Keep this workflow **experimental**. These two models can offer supplementary prompts for a human reviewer to inspect, but this sample does not justify an automatic visual gate, a provider winner, or standing adoption. The common clipping miss and confident unsupported claims make independent image checks necessary. The initial visual calibration is sufficient to present for owner judgment; do not spend on an additional visual wave merely to force consensus. These reviews used neutral screenshot prompts. A future context-aware prompt that supplies the game contract might avoid advice to hide immediate exit, but that condition was not tested and there is no evidence it would fix the clipping miss or false assertions. A targeted follow-up may be proposed if the owner identifies a specific unanswered routing question and judges its time/quota worthwhile. Audio remains a distinct owner gate: this visual result neither authorizes audio calls nor supports claims about listening or music quality.
