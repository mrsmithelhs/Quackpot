## What is usable now

With the existing local Quackpot development server running, open `http://localhost:5173/Quackpot/reports/development/plan-53-generated-track-transcription-and-reconstruction/listening.html`. The page presents the owner-selected original M1 00:20–00:45, raw-time Medium notes rendered with pinned upstream TinySynth, and the identical playable notes using partial curated snare/hi-hat patches. Guitar and drums can be muted independently; raw events, playable events, timing, derived MIDI and a locally captured WAV can be downloaded. One raw zero-duration note is explicitly omitted from both audible variants. Dynamics are assigned, not recovered. The exported capture contains unmeasured start latency and is not sample-aligned to the original.

The source page, event conversion, check harness and documentation are report-local. The generated source crop, events, derived MIDI, captures and screenshots are under ignored `local/listening/`; they are private local artifacts and are not committed. The verified model files, isolated interpreter, venv, retained original model outputs and private diagnostics reside in the separately managed external Plan 53 workspace, outside the game and Git. The page depends on the local server and those ignored artifacts; a fresh checkout alone does not reproduce them. `prepare_listening.prepare(external_workspace, empty_listening_directory)` can recreate the ignored input bundle from the retained external M1 run after verifying its crop hash; review its no-overwrite rules first. The Medium weights are gated and must be supplied independently in a new environment.

The setup used CPython 3.12.13 in an isolated external venv, MuScriptor 0.4.0 and Torch/torchaudio 2.8.0+cu128, alongside owner-installed machine CUDA. Exact source, package and weight receipts and resource ceilings are recorded in `bootstrap-gate.md`, `model-and-first-inference-gate.md`, `first-run-implementation.md` and `progress.md`. ## Owner's first comparison feedback

The owner estimates that roughly 90% of the original melody's quality or recognizability comes through, while arrangement glitches sometimes interfere. This is their subjective listening impression, not a measured transcription score. They found the curated drums somewhat better. They judged M1 far more promising than the earlier agentic arrangements, still chiptune-like and less natural than the generated recording, but good enough as a working gacha-music direction with substantial improvement desirable. They noted that expressive note variations and the original instrument sounds are stronger, and that wrong instrument assignments can make it hard to judge pitch. They are interested in better source stems, improved instrument-to-MIDI mapping, a possible Large comparison, and identifying patterns that could eventually support music responding to the gacha/life balance. These are owner directions and follow-up interests, not approval to integrate a soundtrack or run another model.

Direct reinspection of the preserved raw M1 stream confirms 542 starts: 452 labeled `acoustic_guitar` and 90 `drums`. No other model-predicted instrument category was found. `prepare_listening.py` retains those exact two category names as the playable part labels, omitting one zero-duration guitar event with an explicit ledger entry. `listening.mjs` maps the model's guitar group to zero-based channel 0, GM program 24 (nylon guitar), and drums to zero-based channel 9; it assigns velocity 85 and the curated variant modifies snare/hi-hat timbre parameters. Thus the page does aggregate all 451 playable guitar-labeled notes into one instrument and mute control, but it did not combine several separately predicted pitched groups: none were present in this raw output. These two predicted groups are not source stems or proof of the original instrumentation.
