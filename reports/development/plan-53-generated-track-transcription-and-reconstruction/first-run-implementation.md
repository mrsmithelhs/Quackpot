## Proposed exact operational boundary

The one benign, root-cleared launch observation found the exact venv `Scripts\python.exe` launcher as parent and extracted base `python.exe` as the executing child; `sys.executable` reported the venv path. This establishes that benign two-process launch only, not all future model/helper behavior. A one-process job limit is unsuitable. Revalidate both executables' hashes immediately before any future run, and require a job that cannot break away. Restricting the active count to two is a possible guard, but cannot independently prove every descendant's identity or stop network access through OS services; polling can miss short-lived processes. Do not present it as a complete sandbox.

A concrete Windows Firewall candidate would create uniquely named, temporary **outbound block** rules for each verified venv/base executable at their exact paths, scoped to all profiles, and remove only those rules in `finally`. Rule creation/removal changes machine-wide policy and may require administrator privileges; no such action is authorized or implemented. Before deciding whether this is sufficient, a separately cleared no-model test must show the expected rules are active, direct-IP and hostname/DNS attempts from the actual launched tree fail, any subprocess/helper attempt cannot escape the policy, local file access still works, and the rules are removed even on failure/timeout. Windows DNS service delegation and unobserved executable paths remain unresolved; a process-count limit or sampled process list alone cannot close those gaps. If the candidate cannot enforce the whole tree and name-resolution boundary, request a stronger OS isolation approach or explicit root disposition; do not silently rely on `HF_HUB_OFFLINE`.
