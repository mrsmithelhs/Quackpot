# Plan 54: initial experience and writing consultation

You are a design/writing consultant for Quackpot, a fictional, offline browser gacha game for a high-school game-design course. Students experience a fictional duck-collecting game alongside apartment life, then receive a factual Receipt to reflect on commercial design. Your task is creative exploration, not implementation or approval. Return concise, concrete ideas and identify uncertainty; do not read files, use tools, delegate, or change anything.

## Grounding

The current source snapshot has six digital identities: three common (base speed 10), two rare (14) and one legendary (20). Identities are distinct from their cosmetic rig components and from the player's physical rubber-duck companion. A welcome ten-pull starts the flock; duplicates become Down (common 1, rare 5, legendary 20). One race uses a chosen owned duck and a steady/variable pace; completed race outcomes can award Gems. After racing, a duck's base rating is reduced by 50% until another race finishes, then 25% until two have finished; all remain selectable. A 3-Down one-race +5% speed upgrade is implemented. A 6-Down active-banner pull with ordinary odds and duplicate handling is owner-selected study direction, but not demonstrated as an implemented shop exchange. Singles cost 160 Gems, ten-pulls 1600; a current displayed bundle is 330 Gems for $4.99 fictional cash. Do not treat those prices, race payouts, or roster size as a balanced long-term economy.

The selected forward life design has five slots: breakfast, work, dinner, Evening leisure or early bed, and Late Evening optional play. Meals occupy slots; inspection, reading, browsing and re-entry are free. One baseline gacha session has one pull action (single or ten) and one race, with more successful play needing a separate shared commitment and its real life consequences. It must remain possible to decline spending or gacha. Existing production is in transition: use this as a design input, not a claim the port is complete. Results are seeded/committed once; shown actions and cues must be factually recorded, without inferring motives. Accessibility, free static/skip, necessary costs/odds and immediate exit remain available.

## Request

Compare three meaningfully different organizing structures: **collection-led**, **competition-led**, and **event-led**. For each provide the player's desire, a next-visit hook, a no-purchase route and an optional spending temptation, one matched early/return/later arc, and one lean version using mostly current systems. Suggest a few fictional rivals or events and short in-world promotional lines. Explain what choice matters after a duplicate-heavy pull, when a pull or race has already been used, and when rent or a social commitment makes playing again costly. Distinguish cosmetic interest from mechanical rewards; don't invent a set bonus, suitability statistic, new clock or free currency as an assumed rule. Mark proposed mechanics and any prerequisites explicitly.

Push back on your own ideas: describe failure modes, content-authoring/asset burden, potential life/economy or session conflicts, and observations that would make each hypothesis less plausible. Suggest one primary structure and a small first integration, with credible reasons to reject it. Avoid a mandatory chore checklist, large permanent reward tree, guaranteed daily attendance, real-world clocks, invented social proof, forced waiting, and unsupported claims about engagement, addiction, learning or balance. The tone should be inviting and satirical, with simple player-facing language. Provide coherent batches, not a long catalog.
