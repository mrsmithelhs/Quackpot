# Emerging Quackpot progression direction — for owner discussion

This is a synthesis of the owner's interview, not an adopted specification or a working simulation. It combines the owner's preferred directions with explicitly open mechanism choices. The existing report-local comparison tested framing and examples; it does not implement the system below.

## The experience across visits

**First visit:** The player receives the welcome flock, gets to know a duck, and races against a handful of fictional opponents from a larger local stable. A legible pre-race view can show who is present, what is known about them, and an uncertain indication of their tendencies. The player chooses an owned duck and a risk posture. The race remains automatic and accessible; no timing tap is needed. The player can lose and still make progress with that duck.

**A later visit:** The player recognizes some rivals, sees an opponent field, and considers whether to race a favorite or a different duck. A developed duck has small automatic improvements reflecting previous racing; sometimes it earns an ability point that the player may bank and assign without consuming a life slot. Once assigned, the point is permanent. A few improvable abilities, such as a seeded automatic speed burst, may be more legible than a long list of binary unlocks. Possible charms or skins could make a duck feel personal.

**A difficult decision:** A field of bolder opponents might make a cautious posture attractive if future tested rules make it protective. Under the owner's preferred injury direction an injured duck remains selectable with a penalty; racing it while injured is likely, not certain, to prolong injury. A low/medium/high indicator with optional exact conditional odds is the preferred presentation. A player should still have a meaningful no-purchase route. Whether these mechanics are worth adding, and how they interact with today's rest penalty and accepted race dynamics, requires a separate comparison.

**The wider server:** The fictional local stable can support separate performance and duck-development leaderboards. Reaching a board can be an attainable milestone; sustained top performance could be much harder. Events might give extra development opportunities or duck-specific cosmetic rewards. Celebrations can recognize actual new achievements and meaningful rank movement. Quackpot's promotional messages can portray an increasingly popular fictional game while the underlying local simulation, not real connected players, produces opponent behavior.

**Life still matters:** A race or additional play competes with work, sleep, social plans and financial obligations according to the selected shared schedule. The game may encourage extra play, but a warning, recorded outcome, and Receipt must reflect what actually happened. A due obligation cannot be bypassed by opening an event or leaderboard. No minimum real-world wait is introduced.

**Reflection and later runs:** Achievements can recognize gacha and life outcomes as well as less obvious play patterns. The Common-duck ten-race counter resets per run; each ending discovered may be recognized. The owner suggests 20–30 achievements as a long-term planning target, while cross-run achievements are deferred. A future manually downloaded profile export is intended to contain all runs recorded in that browser profile and their unlocked achievements; storage/export and teacher-analyzer design require a separate bounded packet. A browser profile does not establish student identity.

## What would have to be true for this to work

1. **Opponent information matters:** Two different fields can rationally lead to different duck or risk choices. Profiles are authored fiction; run-specific claims must come from actual observed results. Forecasts are uncertain, not promises. Today opponents do not independently choose player-like stances or spend Down.
2. **The player can read the trade-off:** Show likely risks and relevant costs before commitment, with accessible detail. Distinguish existing rest from proposed injury without a confusing stack of gauges. A collision or injury mentioned afterward must correspond to an actual event.
3. **Growth fits the run:** Every completed race contributes to the entered duck, better finishes and event goals potentially contribute more. Compare candidate geometric level requirements, growth budgets, soft caps and opportunity costs against an ordinary nonspending run. A losing favorite must still develop; a player should plausibly encounter an interesting ability choice without a long grind.
4. **Rarity is meaningful but testable:** A smaller starting speed gap plus more potential ability development for rarer ducks is an owner-suggested alternative. Compare it against current ratings and common-duck routes. Do not silently override the accepted no-per-identity-balancing constraint or assume more ability slots are balanced.
5. **Adaptation retains meaning:** Investment may combine time and spending, but exact inputs/weights are open; no direct penalty for not spending Gems is desired. Compare fixed fields, progression-matched fields and bounded adaptive fields. Stronger ducks must have a tangible benefit, and actual outcomes must match displayed rankings. Assess nonspending, fictional-cash spending and exceptionally lucky routes separately.
6. **Fiction and evidence stay distinct:** Authored marketing may claim the fictional game is popular, but it must not assert real connected players or falsely describe why the code adjusted a field. Receipt records actual shown claims, actions, adjustment and outcomes; it does not claim what the student felt or why they chose something.

## Next bounded design work to propose

An owner-reviewed follow-on study could compare the accepted current race as a control with a small recurring-opponent/forecast concept and a richer risk-and-injury alternative. Include a no-injury route, a small roster and losses; show one or two concrete seeded decision traces and a draft ability choice. Separately sketch the progression/leaderboard economy and event/customization options, then propose explicit candidate inputs, finite-run scenarios, measures, resource bounds and acceptance criteria before simulation. These are proposed next steps, not authorization to simulate, rewrite the race, alter the scheduler or adopt an economy. Export/storage work belongs to QP Orch's separately routed packet.

The owner subsequently confirmed that this synthesis captures the desired direction; see `owner-selections.md` for the actual response. Specific odds, permanent ability options, reward schedules, level multipliers, injury duration, adaptive policies, leaderboard formulas and achievement catalog remain open.
