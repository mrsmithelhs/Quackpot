# Owner selections — Plan 54

## 2026-11-02 — comparison scope

The owner approved “the lean controls plus the three richer traces” and authorized the report-local preview. The preview compares collection, competition and events across matched visits, with a lean control and an explicitly proposed richer choice for each. The candidate collection favorite/history, rotating field and no-reward occasion objective are not adopted production rules. Existing five-slot/session, rest, race and economic boundaries continue to apply. The owner has not yet selected a primary progression structure or approved production integration, rewards, new odds, or an event schedule.

## Collection interview — return visit

The owner prefers the richer favorite/history example to the lean missing-identity framing. They also ask to park a possible visual personalization direction for favorite digital ducks: charms or textured skins could build attachment through customization. The fictional gacha could offer a first customization free and occasionally offer limited free customizations. This is an idea to investigate, not a selected mechanic, price, cadence, availability rule, asset pipeline, reward or production authorization. Its effect on collection identity, accessibility, art burden, scarcity, economy and truthful Receipt evidence remains to be examined. The physical companion remains separate from digital ducks.

## Competition interview — return visit

The owner prefers the richer field view and wants opponents to feel as though they are strategizing; they need not be implied to be human. They propose a stable of opponents, signals about each opponent's demonstrated style, and simple graphical indicators of risk tendency and likelihood of choosing their top duck. They suggest authored fictional background details where actual run history is insufficient. Distinguish fictional profile claims from run-observed actions in any design and preserve factual Receipt evidence; no fabricated run events.

The owner suggests investigating more pre-race choices: a continuum between the current steady and variable/Sprint choices, with greater possible speed alongside increased wobble/contact risk or sudden fatigue; perhaps additional post-race fatigue and an injury status that sidelines a duck for several in-game days, especially at higher risk. The owner asks Codex and Sonnet to assess whether the resulting depth would let a player form a meaningful expectation of opponents' choices. These are proposals, not approved rules. A continuum, changed motion/contact probabilities, added fatigue, injury/unavailability and opponent upgrade behavior could materially change accepted race dynamics, no-reflex-input, rest/selectability, rewards, session/economy, save/replay and Receipt contracts; require a separate bounded investigation and owner/technical gate, not Plan 54 implementation. Whether opponents currently buy upgrades or choose equivalent stances has not yet been verified. No claim of measured engagement or fair balance follows from this interview.

The owner adds that an opponent's risky behavior could have spillover consequences: a major collision might injure multiple involved ducks, including the player's. A less-risky pre-race selection could offer some defense against injury, giving the player a reason to adjust their risk based on the field. This is a candidate interaction, not an adopted collision, injury or defense rule; probabilities, attribution, warning/feedback, fairness for small rosters and effects on existing actual race events require investigation.

## Event interview — return visit

The owner does not find an occasion plus the preview's optional no-reward goal appealing by itself. They propose investigating duck levels and a fictional local "server" leaderboard using the same stable of recurring opponents as races. The full opponent stable could be at least two to three times a race field, suggesting that not every character races when the player does. Events that offer opportunities to level up or improve leaderboard position could motivate participation; event rewards might include special charms or skins. These are exploratory ideas, not adopted level formulas, reward rates, server behavior, event cadence, rankings, scarcity or production scope. The game's no-network/no-accounts constraint remains: any server and opponents would be fictional and locally generated. A design must distinguish authored profiles and simulated results from actual player actions, show truthful recorded cues, assess nonspending and small-roster paths, progression/power balance, finite-session and life trade-offs, catalog/content burden, seed/save/replay, and ensure ranking calculations are coherent before claiming a functional leaderboard.

The owner suggests levels increase speed and develop capabilities in the way a particular duck is raced: repeated lower-risk play could build defensive bonuses for continuing that strategy; higher-risk play could build offensive bonuses such as higher top speed and increased chance of injuring opposing ducks. A middle-risk path might have its own identity instead of merely intermediate combat values—for example, a greater chance to earn customizations restricted to that duck. Duck-specific earned appearances could build attachment to the individual racer. This is a proposal, not a chosen formula, injury mechanic, reward, stat, randomness rule or balance conclusion. Investigate power feedback loops, readable choices, nonspending and losing routes, fairness to opponents and small rosters, transfer/identity rules, and interactions with accepted race dynamics, no per-identity balancing, rest and replay. Whether earned progression persists across strategy changes is unresolved.

The owner prefers every completed race to contribute to the entered duck's progress, with stronger finishes and event goals contributing more. This gives a favorite a development path even when it does not win. It is a selected design preference for exploration, not an adopted formula, event eligibility rule, implementation, or claim that an unresolved race counts as completed.
