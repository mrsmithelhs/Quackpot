# Owner selections — Plan 54

## 2026-11-02 — comparison scope

At this initial checkpoint the owner approved “the lean controls plus the three richer traces” and authorized the report-local preview. The preview compares collection, competition and events across matched visits, with a lean control and an explicitly proposed richer choice for each. At that time the owner had not selected a primary progression structure. The later experience-synthesis acceptance is recorded at the end of this document. The candidate collection favorite/history, rotating field and no-reward occasion objective are not adopted production rules; existing five-slot/session, rest, race and economic boundaries continue to apply.

## Collection interview — return visit

The owner prefers the richer favorite/history example to the lean missing-identity framing. They also ask to park a possible visual personalization direction for favorite digital ducks: charms or textured skins could build attachment through customization. The fictional gacha could offer a first customization free and occasionally offer limited free customizations. This is an idea to investigate, not a selected mechanic, price, cadence, availability rule, asset pipeline, reward or production authorization. Its effect on collection identity, accessibility, art burden, scarcity, economy and truthful Receipt evidence remains to be examined. The physical companion remains separate from digital ducks.

## Competition interview — return visit

The owner prefers the richer field view and wants opponents to feel as though they are strategizing; they need not be implied to be human. They propose a stable of opponents, signals about each opponent's demonstrated style, and simple graphical indicators of risk tendency and likelihood of choosing their top duck. They suggest authored fictional background details where actual run history is insufficient. Distinguish fictional profile claims from run-observed actions in any design and preserve factual Receipt evidence; no fabricated run events.

The owner initially suggested investigating more pre-race choices: a continuum between the current steady and variable/Sprint choices, with greater possible speed alongside increased wobble/contact risk or sudden fatigue; perhaps additional post-race fatigue and an injury status that sidelines a duck for several in-game days, especially at higher risk. The later preferred direction keeps injured ducks raceable, as recorded below. The owner asks Codex and Sonnet to assess whether the resulting depth would let a player form a meaningful expectation of opponents' choices. These are proposals, not approved rules. A continuum, changed motion/contact probabilities, added fatigue and opponent upgrade behavior could materially change accepted race dynamics, no-reflex-input, rest/selectability, rewards, session/economy, save/replay and Receipt contracts; require a separate bounded investigation and owner/technical gate, not Plan 54 implementation. At the interview the opponent mechanics had not yet been checked; the subsequent source finding in `current-state.md` identifies fixed opponent slots and player-only stance/upgrade inputs. No claim of measured engagement or fair balance follows from this interview.

The owner adds that an opponent's risky behavior could have spillover consequences: a major collision might injure multiple involved ducks, including the player's. A less-risky pre-race selection could offer some defense against injury, giving the player a reason to adjust their risk based on the field. This is a candidate interaction, not an adopted collision, injury or defense rule; probabilities, attribution, warning/feedback, fairness for small rosters and effects on existing actual race events require investigation.

When asked about a player with only one duck, the owner prefers an injured duck to remain raceable with a temporary penalty; racing it while injured is likely to prolong the injury. This is the owner's preferred direction for investigation, not yet a specified probability, penalty, recovery clock, persistence/transaction contract or production authorization. The possibility of extension is not a guaranteed extension. Preserve a no-injury control for comparison and distinguish this proposed injury from the accepted soft-rest rule. Future design must make the risk and actual outcome understandable, avoid a no-purchase lockout, and assess repeated injury/recovery feedback.

For the chance that racing while injured prolongs the injury, the owner prefers a simple low/medium/high risk indicator with optional access to exact odds. This is a presentation preference for the proposed mechanic; thresholds, calculation, timing, interaction with other risks, and UI are not selected. Any future implementation must keep the concise label consistent with the actual conditional odds, offer the detail accessibly before commitment, and record only what was actually displayed and happened.

## Event interview — return visit

The owner does not find an occasion plus the preview's optional no-reward goal appealing by itself. They propose investigating duck levels and a fictional local "server" leaderboard using the same stable of recurring opponents as races. The full opponent stable could be at least two to three times a race field, suggesting that not every character races when the player does. Events that offer opportunities to level up or improve leaderboard position could motivate participation; event rewards might include special charms or skins. These are exploratory ideas, not adopted level formulas, reward rates, server behavior, event cadence, rankings, scarcity or production scope. The game's no-network/no-accounts constraint remains: any server and opponents would be fictional and locally generated. A design must distinguish authored profiles and simulated results from actual player actions, show truthful recorded cues, assess nonspending and small-roster paths, progression/power balance, finite-session and life trade-offs, catalog/content burden, seed/save/replay, and ensure ranking calculations are coherent before claiming a functional leaderboard.

The owner proposes two distinct leaderboards, race performance and duck development, with celebrations for newly earned appearances or meaningful upward movements. They want a plausible path onto at least one board without too much difficulty, while reaching the top in the game's expected span could require significant competing life sacrifices for additional gacha play. They suggest a bounded rubber-band adjustment of fictional opponents when the player's performance is mediocre, supporting accessible progress and demonstrating manipulated perception and engagement pressure. These are design and calibration intentions, not a guarantee, selected adjustment formula or proof of dopamine release, retention or learning efficacy. The system would need a coherent locally generated field/ranking, explicit adjustment triggers/caps and seeded consequences, truthful distinctions between simulated opponents and player outcomes, factual evidence of displayed celebrations and actual adjustments, small-roster/nonspending and rapid/longer routes, and post-Receipt reflection without inferred player motives. Never fabricate actual race results or claim human competition. Separate normal satisfying feedback from manipulative amplification and study both rather than assume psychological effect.

The owner clarifies that rubber-band AI should not specifically penalize players for not spending Gems. They propose that balance could nonetheless make sustained leaderboard dominance almost impossible without Gems or statistically exceptional luck, and that Quackpot-related news/messages could increasingly market the fictional game as popular as the player invests more. This promotional framing could explain why more invested players encounter tougher competition while mediocre runs can still win sometimes. This is a candidate player-experience/economy direction, not selected triggers, a measured likelihood, a promise of any route, or authority to claim real player counts. Distinguish Gems earned in play, Gems spent, and fictional cash purchases; the exact investment measures and weights remain to be defined. The local adaptive mechanism must be evaluated independently of marketing claims, preserve truthful actual cues/actions/adjustments in the Receipt, avoid inferred motives and actual network/human-opponent claims, and be tested against nonspending and adverse outcomes. Promotional popularity claims and simulated opponents require clear fictional provenance in debrief; no post-Receipt retention technique follows.

The owner clarifies that “investment” can represent a **combination of time and spending**, and agrees the design can be tuned through algorithmic simulation and testing. The exact observable measures, relative weights, thresholds, reset/decay behavior, field-selection and promotional-cue policies remain unselected. Do not conflate time, Gems earned, Gems spent and fictional cash purchases, or infer motivation. This expresses support for simulation as a design method, not authorization for a particular new simulation runner, heavy execution turn, source change or proof of calibration. Define a bounded model, decision criteria and resource/owner gates first.

The owner also suggests achievements in both the fictional gacha and metagame, including collection/leaderboard success and less obvious behaviors such as ten races with Common ducks or five races with one duck, Ren friendship, bad workday streaks, missed shifts without firing, or loan repayment. They revised the earlier “only raced Common ducks” example to “10 races with Common ducks” so it can be earned during a run, and propose an achievement for each ending discovered. At that point exact triggers, eligibility, threshold scope, early exit, and presentation were not yet selected; the later Common-duck scope is recorded below. Achievement text must reflect actual recorded actions/outcomes, not infer a player's intent or emotional state.

The owner selects a forward export direction: **one student-initiated usage export per browser profile containing data from all runs recorded in that profile, including unlocked achievements**, to avoid multiple submission files per student. They accept that browser storage and export capability need upgrading. This is a direction for a future bounded design and integration, not an implemented export, schema, authorization for Plan 54 source writes, retroactive reconstruction of unavailable past runs, automatic upload, account, or proof that a browser profile equals one student. Define retention/clear behavior, profile identity limits, shared or reset lab devices, data minimization, evidence provenance and completeness, per-run versus profile-wide achievement scope, partial/unfinished runs, ending-event boundaries, storage failure/recovery, export versioning and teacher-analyzer behavior with the owner. Current selected implementation/contract is per-run; reconcile it explicitly before a separate integration packet. No post-Receipt retention techniques are authorized.

The owner clarified that the “10 races with Common ducks” counter **resets for each run**. They would consider a cross-run achievement later, after more of the core experience is in place, and suggest **20–30 achievements total** as a planning target, not an approved catalog or mandatory quota for this study. The distinction remains: an export can aggregate multiple run records and their earned achievements even when an achievement's counter is per-run. Ending-discovery achievement scope and other counters remain to be defined; do not silently treat every achievement as cross-run.

The owner suggests levels increase speed and develop capabilities in the way a particular duck is raced: repeated lower-risk play could build defensive bonuses for continuing that strategy; higher-risk play could build offensive bonuses such as higher top speed and increased chance of injuring opposing ducks. A middle-risk path might have its own identity instead of merely intermediate combat values—for example, a greater chance to earn customizations restricted to that duck. Duck-specific earned appearances could build attachment to the individual racer. This is a proposal, not a chosen formula, injury mechanic, reward, stat, randomness rule or balance conclusion. Investigate power feedback loops, readable choices, nonspending and losing routes, fairness to opponents and small rosters, transfer/identity rules, and interactions with accepted race dynamics, no per-identity balancing, rest and replay. The exact acquisition and activation rules remain unresolved.

When asked about changing styles, the owner says a duck could earn both defensive and offensive abilities over its career. They propose level requirements increasing at a small geometric rate, so building substantial bonuses of both types takes a long time. This is a direction for exploration, not a selected multiplier, level cap, reward curve or demonstrated balance. Assess the cumulative requirements against plausible races in the represented run, including loss/nonspending routes and the session/life budget; a geometric curve alone does not prove a duck cannot become overpowered or that the later levels are reachable. The ability acquisition and activation rules remain open.

The owner prefers a mixture of small automatic improvements reflecting how the duck has raced and occasional player-selected ability investments. They propose studying what an ability should be to keep complexity appropriate to the players' time budget. One example is a PRNG-triggered brief speed burst ("flap ahead") that can itself be improved, rather than a large catalog of binary unlocks. Ducks would determine activation through the seeded mechanism, with no player reflex/quick-time input. A point could be assigned among two or three selected specialization options; individual ducks might have variants, and higher rarities might have more potential abilities (e.g. one for Common, two for the next tier). Owner suggests comparing a smaller rarity-based initial speed gap if rarer ducks instead offer greater growth potential. These are investigation candidates, not selected ability counts, rarity rules, activation odds, level thresholds, speed changes or production authorization. A later bounded comparison must address readable choices, nonspending/common-duck viability, cumulative power and return-on-investment within the run, deterministic commit/replay and actual shown activation evidence. The accepted current roster ratings, shared race dynamics and no-per-identity-balancing constraint remain in force until a separately reviewed owner decision; identity-themed presentation does not itself authorize identity-specific balancing.

The owner prefers earned ability points to be saveable for later assignment; assigning a point should not consume a life time slot. Once assigned, a point cannot be reassigned: the owner wants the choice to be consequential. These are preferred interaction directions for the proposed system, not a selected save schema, upgrade transaction or production authorization. A future mechanism must preserve an unassigned point and any committed assignment through save/reload without double-awards or repeated spending, while keeping reading/navigation free and respecting due obligations and other established transaction boundaries. The player should be able to understand the permanence and the relevant effects before committing; no particular confirmation interface is selected.

The owner prefers every completed race to contribute to the entered duck's progress, with stronger finishes and event goals contributing more. This gives a favorite a development path even when it does not win. It is a selected design preference for exploration, not an adopted formula, event eligibility rule, implementation, or claim that an unresolved race counts as completed.
