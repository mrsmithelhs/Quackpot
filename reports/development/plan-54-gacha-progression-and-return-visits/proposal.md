# Plan 54 comparison proposal — owner prebuild decision

This proposal is for an isolated comparison, not an adopted game design. Baseline and source pins are in `current-state.md`. Three models are deliberately distinct; their examples and copy are illustrative and do not set prices, awards, banner policies, or new stats. The initial Sonnet batch and independent dispositions are in `consultation/initial-result.md`; owner selection is pending.

## Candidate structures

| Model | Player's next question and lean path | Optional spending temptation | Cost and weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Collection-led: **My flock** | “Who have I met, and which identity am I hoping to find?” Browse the existing Duck-Dex, enjoy known characters, and choose a missing identity as a personal aim. An ordinary race can earn Gems; a duplicate adds Down under current rules. No completion prize or set bonus is presumed. | A bundle could bring the next existing pull closer; no purchase is needed to browse or choose a racer. | Six identities can exhaust discovery quickly; a target may be inaccessible or frustrating, and a collection target alone may not make a return meaningful. More identities need writing/art and future odds/economy work. |
| Competition-led: **One more pond story** | “Which duck and pace will I try next?” Treat the next existing race as the central occasion; choosing among rested and tired owned ducks and watching actual results creates a small recurring story. Use existing race Gem awards and optional 3-Down upgrade. No invented league, rival rules or rewards are assumed. | Buy Gems toward an existing pull in hopes of another racer; the upgrade consumes earned duplicate Down, not cash directly. | A stronger rested duck can dominate choices; a small roster makes variation thinner. Copy can frame rivals but cannot claim a persistent rivalry or fair match without a mechanism. |
| Event-led: **Today's pond bulletin** | “What occasion is the pond celebrating, and how do I want to take part?” A proposed bulletin frames the same ordinary race as a themed gathering and invites a personal goal, such as entering a favorite duck. Reading and browsing remain free. The lean comparison uses editorial framing only; no tracked objective, extra race, reward or deadline. | A fictional promotion could draw attention to the existing offer, with actual costs and odds; no event purchase is required. | Even copy-only occasions need ongoing authorship and a truthful availability rule. A seeded rotation, exclusive rewards, expiry or altered fields is a separate mechanism with substantial economy, clock and evidence implications. |

Initial recommendation: test competition-led as primary, with collection as a personal attachment and occasional event flavor. It gives a repeatable decision using existing race and rest mechanics and does not depend on completing a six-item catalog. It may fail if the race choice is obvious, the presentation repeats, or the player prefers discovery to racing; the comparison must allow either alternative to win. Sonnet independently recommended competition, but also proposed adding a rest label the existing picker already has; agreement is not independent gameplay evidence. The smallest potential integration is a contextual next-race invitation based on real available state and recorded outcome, subject to a later source and evidence gate; it is not a commitment to create a named rival, league or rewards.

## Matched comparison situations

Each row uses the same illustrative balances, roster and allowance across all three models. Actual simulated outcomes must not be implied. These are scripted snapshots, not recorded saves.

| Situation | Collection-led question | Competition-led question | Event-led question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early: welcome ten-pull, two owned common identities, duplicates, no spare cash; pull used, race ready | Inspect new identities and pick a missing one to hope for; duplicates are Down, not a new identity. | Pick one of the owned ducks and pace for the remaining race; optional upgrade only if actual Down is sufficient. | Read a proposed pond bulletin and enter the same remaining race or leave; bulletin changes framing, not odds or prizes. |
| Return: three owned, previous racer tired, 150 Gems, rent approaching; pull and race ready | Browse collection and save Gems rather than promise an unaffordable single. | Compare a tired previous racer with a rested alternative; race can earn an actual placement award but no award is guaranteed. | Read an occasion and choose the same race or leave; no ticking deadline is implied. |
| Later: five owned including legendary, repeated duplicates, 3 Down, social visit competes with play; race already used | Enjoy existing collection or wait for an opportunity; a missing identity is not guaranteed. | Inspect rest and plan a future race; the consumed race cannot be replayed for another award. | Read the occasion, choose a personal goal for a future race, or defer it; it grants no extra session or social exemption. |
| Optional-spend contrast: same relevant state, 150 Gems and sufficient fictional cash | Compare keeping cash with the currently visible 330-Gem/$4.99 bundle and its implications; no forced transaction. | Same offer, same wallet, same race choices. | Same offer, contextual promotional copy only; no exclusivity or invented reward. |

Each presentation must show the actual remaining allowance and a free way to leave or inspect. If illustrative fictional cash is needed, label it as an example and show competing obligations; never suggest that 150 Gems buys a 160-Gem single. If a 6-Down action is shown at all, label it owner-selected but not implemented, and do not conflate it with the live baseline. Successful extra play must use the shared commitment and disclosed consequences; this preview does not implement that scheduler.

## Hypotheses and discriminating observations

| Hypothesis | Observation that would challenge it |
| --- | --- |
| Collection creates a personal next-visit goal with the current catalog. | A reviewer feels finished after seeing the small roster, or finds the missing duck only an abstract checklist with no interesting decision. |
| Rest and pace create an interesting recurring choice without added systems. | One duck/pace remains the obvious choice across the matched situations, or the same race framing feels repetitive despite the choices. |
| A bulletin adds variety at modest cost. | It takes several clocks or new rules to understand, requires exclusive rewards to matter, or looks interchangeable with cosmetic wording. |
| A free path remains meaningful under limited cash. | The player sees no desirable action after a duplicate or a used allowance except buying or sacrificing a life obligation. |

These are interview/clarity checks, not measures of population retention or educational effect. Record what the owner actually notices, competing explanations and untested alternatives; do not convert preference into a falsified universal claim.

## Optional richer comparisons — proposed mechanics, not selected rules

QP Orch observed that comparing only editorial framing could measure the appeal of a pitch while leaving the real progression question unanswered. Keep each lean treatment as a control, and optionally add one bounded decision trace per model. These traces are scripted illustrations, with no new game engine or actual awards.

| Model | Candidate to contrast with lean control | Changed choice and consequences | Content and integration implications |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Collection | **Choose a personal collection objective:** propose selecting an owned identity to feature in a non-reward personal collection page, such as a favorite with a factual race history. Selection can be changed freely; it does not affect draw odds, stats, inventory, or prizes. | After duplicates, a player can inspect and feature an existing duck instead of chasing a missing one. The decision is expressive rather than a mechanical advantage; it may feel too slight for sustained progression. Browsing/selection is free and does not grant an extra pull or race. | Needs a clear definition of recorded history, display and persistence if later adopted. Per-identity writing/art could grow with the catalog; no new asset or reward is required for this trace. |
| Competition | **Rotating opponent field:** propose a small pool of named fictional opponents with a deterministically chosen field for a future race. Show the field before the normal duck/pace choice. | The player chooses an owned duck and pace knowing which opponents are present. A field could change expected difficulty and existing placement-based Gem payouts, even without a new reward. It must use the same race, rest, one-session allowance and accepted physics; it cannot promise a win or imply unselected suitability stats. | Opponent identities, stats, selection/eligibility, seeded persistence, exposure and actual result evidence would need a later bounded design and calibration. Fictional names/copy create an ongoing authoring burden. No adjustment to accepted physics is proposed here. |
| Event | **Non-reward occasion objective:** propose an optional, clearly described personal challenge attached to an occasion, such as entering a particular owned duck in the ordinary race. It records whether the actual eligible action occurred but grants no currency, stat, item, or extra attempt. | The player decides whether to pursue that goal or race another duck; the ordinary race is still the single allowed race and the player can skip the objective. An objective requiring an unowned duck must never block play or be falsely offered as achievable. | Requires a factual eligibility, selection, completion and displayed-evidence contract; authoring and repeatability grow with the number of occasions. No expiry or hidden clock is assumed. Any later reward or deadline requires separate selection and economy/session review. |

These are intentionally different kinds of choice: expressive identity, changed competitive field, and a voluntary objective. The collection example is a proposed system for displaying and choosing a favorite, not an invented set bonus. The competition candidate can affect existing Gem outcomes and thus carries the greatest immediate calibration risk. The event objective adds tracking even without a reward. If any variant feels too slight, the comparison can reveal that rather than silently escalating it into a reward system. A future source implementation would need its own authorization and truthful Receipt treatment.

A student-facing phone composition shows only that scenario's in-world copy, factual illustrative balances, allowance and plausible choices. Adjacent reviewer notes identify what is proposed and what would change. Native buttons, visible focus, semantic headings, keyboard operation and narrow layout are required; static text is sufficient and there is no simulated engine, timer, purchase, persisted save or random roll.
