# Plan 54 recommendation — owner-reviewed direction

## A reason to return

Recommend **a recurring competitive pond as the primary structure**, supported by attachment to individual ducks and events that give the next visit a purpose. The owner accepted the combined direction in `experience-synthesis.md`: develop and personalize ducks, read a field of recurring fictional opponents, pursue leaderboard/event opportunities, and later reflect on actual promotions and actions. This is an experience direction, not adoption of every illustrated mechanic. The lean competition-first recommendation from the initial comparison has expanded: collection and event progression are substantive supporting systems, not merely cosmetic labels on a race.

The intended player question is: “Who should I race, how much risk should I accept against these opponents, and what am I willing to give up elsewhere?” A favorite can make progress after a completed race even if it loses. Ordinary play may build a history and eventually create understandable development choices; events or cosmetics can supply distinct goals. The fictional app can promote popularity and celebrate progress, while the apartment makes extra play a real opportunity cost. The Receipt must explain what was actually displayed and done, without claiming psychological effect or player motive.

## What the comparison and interview established

- The owner preferred the richer collection and competition examples over their lean controls. The no-reward event framing was not compelling by itself; levels, leaderboards and event rewards suggested a stronger event purpose. This is one owner's feedback on scripted views, not a measurement of student retention, ability comprehension or balance.
- Owner directions include bankable ability points assigned without a life slot and permanent once assigned; small automatic improvements plus occasional choices; duck-driven seeded ability activation without reflex input; a raceable injured duck with a temporary penalty and a chance racing prolongs injury; and low/medium/high risk with optional exact odds. Each needs a concrete mechanism and calibration before source implementation.
- A duck may develop both offensive and defensive capabilities. The owner suggests modest geometric increases to level requirements, with possible ability variants and more growth potential at higher rarity balanced against a smaller starting speed difference. Neither the multiplier nor the effect on actual run outcomes is established. A few improvable abilities may serve the time budget better than a large binary catalog; this is a hypothesis.
- Two fictional-server leaderboards, progress celebrations and bounded adaptation are candidates. The owner does not want AI explicitly penalizing nonspenders; economy and luck could nevertheless make sustained dominance very difficult without Gems. Investment may combine time and spending, with specific signals and weights open. Actual simulated fields, rankings, adaptation and displayed popularity claims must remain coherent and explainable.
- The owner selects one student-initiated profile-wide export containing recorded runs and unlocked achievements as future direction. Ten Common-duck races resets each run, ending achievements are proposed, cross-run achievements can wait, and 20–30 total achievements is a planning target. This export is not implemented; QP Orch owns its separate design route.

## Consequences and limits

Recurring rivals need authored profiles, deterministic policies, forecasts and actual run history; a credible 12–18-person stable is a substantial content and state commitment, not merely five labels. Ability variants and rarity potential add authoring, accessibility, replay and balance burden. Level growth, stronger competitors and rewards can create an arms race or make earned duck improvements feel meaningless; compare fixed, progression-matched and bounded adaptive opposition. If combining offense and defense eventually makes every duck converge on the same best build, test growth budgets, soft caps, opportunity costs and a no-specialization control. Do not silently select one.

The scripted preview, 18-cell browser check, one keyboard path, isolated desktop/narrow captures, two Sonnet consultations and owner's feedback do not establish a functional game economy, engagement, dopamine response, classroom effect, assistive-technology conformance or production lifecycle. Sonnet's preference to defer injury is a useful control, not a veto. Current accepted rest, race mapping and contact remain authoritative until explicitly reconsidered. No new prices, probabilities, ranking formula, injury duration, ability catalog or adaptation trigger is selected.
