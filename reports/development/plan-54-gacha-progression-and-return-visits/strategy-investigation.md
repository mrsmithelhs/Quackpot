# Strategy and return visits — working analysis

This is a report-local analysis of the owner's interview ideas, not selected game rules. The owner approved sending `consultation/strategy-brief.md` to Sonnet, and one fresh-context follow-up was completed. `consultation/strategy-result.md` records the result and independent corrections. Conclusions here are hypotheses to test and discuss, not measured results.

## The strategic question

Opponent profiles can support a meaningful choice only when the player can use them to make a different decision. A useful minimal loop would be: see a field with a legible tendency and uncertainty; choose a duck and risk posture; see a race whose recorded outcomes can sometimes confirm or surprise that expectation; and compare the actual result with what was shown. A named opponent or risk icon that never predicts anything would add character but little strategy. Conversely, a perfectly reliable signal could make every race a solved puzzle. Neither outcome has been tested.

Today the resolver has five fixed opponent slots, one shared field base and seeded individual performance variation. Player stance and upgrade are player inputs; the source does not establish opponents with independent strategic choices, inventories or persistent careers. `current-state.md` pins that inspection. A recurring stable, opponent policies and credible history would all be new design work. Authored profile copy may describe a fictional persona, but observations presented as having occurred in this run must derive from actual recorded events; a forecast must remain a forecast.

## Possible progression layers

1. **Attachment without a power curve:** an owned duck can accumulate factual race history and, if separately selected later, earn or receive duck-specific visual customization. This supports attachment without assuming a race advantage. Every completed race contributing to development is the owner's stated preference; exact earning, reward and presentation remain open.
2. **Recognizable opposition:** a bounded local stable has tendencies, selects racers and risk according to documented seeded policies, and exposes a deliberately limited forecast before a race. This could create a decision without yet adding injury or a full leaderboard. It requires policy, event-evidence, replay and authoring design; it is not a small copy-only change.
3. **Long-term growth, events and leaderboard:** levels, distinct defensive/offensive/decorative development, event rewards and a local fictional leaderboard could connect visits. A coherent local ranking and simulated opponents need explicit accounting and finite run pacing. Speed growth can alter race timing and Gem earnings, and participation/placing bonuses can compound advantages. The owner’s preference that every completed race contributes is a useful anti-stagnation input, not proof that the economy is fair.
4. **Collision spillover and injury:** a risky entrant could affect several racers and a cautious choice could provide defense. This potentially makes field-reading valuable, but crosses selected contact/no-additional-collision-penalty and all-owned-ducks-selectable boundaries. A multi-day exclusion can stall a small roster or require unwanted pulls. A future design needs a genuinely playable no-purchase path, understandable forecasts, honest actual-collision/injury evidence, and a no-injury comparison before selection. It must not imply that a duck was struck or injured without the corresponding actual outcome.

These layers are a decomposition for investigation, not a recommended implementation order selected by the owner. An alternate prototype might find that visible rival character and existing choices are insufficient, in which case more substantive mechanics may be needed.

Sonnet independently recommended a minimal opponent/forecast comparison using the current two pace choices before coupling in levels and injury. I agree this is a useful **control**, because it isolates whether opponents change a decision, but disagree that it should be the only path examined or that the richer ideas should be rejected. The owner is explicitly interested in collision spillover, a broader risk spectrum and duck development. A follow-on proposal should compare that richer direction with the lean control, including a no-injury alternative and actual decision traces, rather than silently choosing current mechanics as the end state. Neither approach has demonstrated sufficient depth or calibrated payoffs yet.

## Illustrative decision contrasts

These are design questions, not numerical balance claims or simulations:

| Situation | What could change a choice? | What would challenge the hypothesis? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A small flock faces a field described as mostly cautious. | A player may choose their favorite and a bolder posture if the expected collision exposure is relatively low. | The same strongest duck and setting is always best, or the forecast is too vague to affect the choice. |
| The same flock faces several opponents described as risk-seeking. | A player may choose a protective posture to preserve their favorite for later, accepting less potential speed. | Caution gives no meaningful protection, or outside collisions can disable every owned duck regardless of informed choice. |
| A favorite finishes outside the podium and the player has limited cash. | Participation can still advance that duck; the player can leave and attend to rent or a social plan. | Progress is too small to matter without repeat races or purchases, or an event requires sacrificing an obligation to stay viable. |

For a defensible comparison, both fields need declared generated tendencies, candidate player options, actual outcomes and consequences, including forecasts that sometimes fail. A statement such as “usually bold” needs a defined meaning, but does not promise this race's selection. No example demonstrates probability calibration or actual balance.

## Follow-up design boundaries

Before any production integration, reconcile owner selections against race/rest decisions, Plan 52's session/transaction authority and actual Receipt evidence. Test small rosters, losses, nonspending, uncertain forecasts, repeat exposure, no-injury and no-new-reward controls, local leaderboard reproducibility and power-curve feedback. The team should also consider whether deliberately increasing injuries to opponents fits the intended satire and classroom experience, and how to avoid visually implying that fictional rivals are real people. A later bounded calibration and owner decision are required for numbers, injury duration, rewards, availability and any change to accepted dynamics.
