| Axis | Player-facing question | Open design issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ability capacity | Which abilities can this individual duck have or acquire? | Whether rarity affects the number or selection; do not treat Plan54's illustrative counts as selected. |

These axes are independent questions. Equal starting base speed does not eliminate the value of a starting ability or wider risk choice. A higher ceiling needs a reachable benefit in a realistic nonspending run. Whether a low-risk option gives a higher or lower expected result is a mechanism question, not implied by a wider range.

## Illustrative experience traces — not proposed numerical rules

1. **Protective favorite:** a duck has a modest Guard ability and later develops an automatic Burst. The player chooses a cautious pace before a strong field. Guard may reduce injury from an actual collision; Burst may give occasional speed. Show what actually activates, and let the favorite sit out when tired or injured. A second ability creates an interaction without requiring the duck to be permanently raced while fatigued.
2. **Aggressive combination:** a duck develops an automatic speed move and an offensive ability that may affect an actual contact. The player chooses aggressive risk because the race matters. Higher chance of a good finish and injury consequences must both be modeled and disclosed; no forced collision or claim of a guaranteed result. An opponent's ability and injury state may matter independently.
3. **Common duck remains worthwhile:** a Common duck develops one or more useful abilities under rules still to be designed; a rarer duck may start with an ability or offer a different capacity/range. The Common path should support meaningful nonspending choices rather than merely waiting for a rare replacement. Exact counts, unlocks, timing and rarity boundaries remain open.

Combining abilities can create additive power (two benefits accumulating) or multiplicative feedback (winning earns faster growth, a stronger combination wins more, and the loop compounds). Also consider incompatible or situational abilities, a shared development budget, activation timing and a no-combination control; none is selected. A wider protective/aggressive range may add another interaction. Study both strong and unlucky/nonspending routes, practical run duration and clarity; do not use a numeric ceiling as proof that the ceiling is reachable or balanced.
