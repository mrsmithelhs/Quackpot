# Rarity and ability combinations — initial comparison proposal

2026-11-03. Owner paused Common-only Stage B, requested a rarity/combination comparison, reviewed the initial ideas and replaced Soft Landing with Bounce and Slipstream Release with independent crowd acceleration. Owner's positive reception of the other concepts is experience feedback, not approval of exact implementation, a catalog or numerical rarity rules. QP Orch must review any specific executable protocol and allocate resources before execution. This document is a proposal for review; no code or run follows from it.

## First question

Can one duck's combination create a recognizable choice and consequence while a Common duck retains a useful route? Consider **Bounce + crowd acceleration** as one small test combination: both can be independently meaningful in a crowded race, and neither requires the other. The duck might accelerate near traffic and, after a real collision, temporarily bounce over subsequent ducks. The originating collision remains real; a later contact avoided by height is not an injury event. This choice does not discard Wide Flap, Dive, Flap Ahead, Draft or the other owner-reviewed ideas. Its apparent synergy must be measured, not assumed.

## Two staged questions, not a full catalog

1. **Single-race interaction:** compare four fixed synthetic ability sets under the same seeds, field and starting duck rating: no new ability, Bounce only, crowd acceleration only, and both. Both current race/no-new-ability and no-injury controls remain visible. Assess actual opportunity and activation, path/collision counts, injury, finish place/time and both-duck outcomes. Independent trigger forks, actual recorded contacts and deterministic overlap rules are required. In this stage the ability set is already available: it says nothing about acquisition or rarity.
2. **Rarity reachability:** separately compare two labeled provisional development contracts through a finite represented run. One has equal starting base speed and no starting ability; the other has the same base speed plus a small starting ability and potentially higher ability capacity/ceiling. Use transparent fixture rosters and the same opportunity/life constraints, with losses and nonspending. Measure when a first ability and combination become available and usable, not merely the theoretical ceiling. This is a deliberately compound experience comparison: any difference cannot be attributed to one rarity feature without further ablation. Do not claim that wider risk choices or a complete rarity policy are tested unless explicitly added to a future reviewed protocol. The prior Common-only study remains paused rather than quietly folded into this one.

A starting sketch for orchestration review, not a request to execute: stage 1 might use eight fixed paired seeds and two predetermined field situations (one dispersed, one crowded) across four ability sets, with no seed selection: 64 race resolutions before any separate injury/control cases. Stage 2 should only be specified after the single-race contract and its evidence, because stacking, opportunity availability and life costs remain uncertain. Synthetic crowded starting conditions require an explicit adapter seam and must be compared against the unaltered field; never force an outcome or imply that those conditions occur in an ordinary production race. QP Orch should set exact initial conditions, conditional activation odds and independent draws, controls, shard/call ceiling, source pins, output and compute budget before any experiment. If substantial resolver changes are needed, begin with small forced-event correctness fixtures clearly labeled as tests, not balance evidence.

## Decisions that the simulation contract must settle

- Bounce activation occurs after a real duck collision. Specify whether its duck remains subject to the initiating collision and injury (the owner's description suggests yes), how long it remains above others, airborne contact categories, landing/overlap resolution and no retroactive change. The current one-contact-per-race production cap limits what a second avoided contact can demonstrate; no cap change is silently authorized. Compare with an unchanged-contact-cap control, and separately propose any altered cap if needed.
- Crowd acceleration needs a defined crowd using actual positions, activation timing and cooldown, final speed/acceleration limits, and whether closer proximity changes collision incidence. A crowd signal must not be generated from fictional copy.
- Two abilities may have additive or multiplicative effects, overlap, or interfere. Specify timing, priority and result/event evidence, plus a no-combination control and fixed seeded namespaces. Rarity-specific capacity, rank/ceiling, starting ability and per-race risk remain separate axes, not one implied parameter. A useful result may be that the combination does not work.
- One-off race outcomes are not evidence of lasting run balance, actual time-to-develop, accessibility, student engagement or production replay. A single contact-cap/no-injury treatment cannot settle systemic injury. Known limitations must be reported.

## Choice of first pair — engineering scope versus experience

Root notes that Bounce plus crowd acceleration is an informative long-term combination, but not necessarily the smallest first experiment. The previous 64-race sketch limits compute, not engineering complexity. Bounce requires a second duck-contact opportunity to demonstrate avoidance, vertical collision layers and a landing rule. Crowd acceleration requires a new proximity detector and acceleration behavior. These mechanisms could interact, making any observed effect harder to attribute. The accepted one-contact race must remain an unchanged control; an additional-contact model needs its own reviewed contract.

A smaller first candidate is **Steady Nerves + Quick Recovery**: the first modifies a seeded self-generated wobble, and the second adjusts the return after an actual duck collision. Both have recognizable seams in the current resolver and can be compared separately and together. They are both defensive: this tests whether two automatic abilities can combine and remain understandable, not an offensive/defensive combination or the full rarity vision. The existing resolver still needs a report-local adapter and actual control-parity checks; no behavior is already implemented. If an offensive/defensive contrast is essential in the first run, compare the earlier whole-race Burst proxy + Quick Recovery, clearly labeled as a speed proxy, not the owner's flight-based Flap Ahead. That cheaper model cannot answer whether airborne passing works or whether future visual effects feel right.

Proposed owner choice: prioritize (A) a focused first combination using existing wobble/recovery mechanics, preserving the possibility of later offensive abilities and rarity combinations, or (B) the more ambitious Bounce + crowd acceleration experience first, accepting that an explicit multi-contact/vertical/crowd model and correctness study precede balance comparisons. A third option is a small report-only side-by-side design of both routes before choosing implementation. Recommend A to establish combination behavior while separately designing B's needed contact model, unless seeing the more dynamic interaction is the owner's priority. No calculations, owner approval of numbers, production code or extra model calls are implied by any option.
