# Landing overlap: source findings and options for owner review

Status: investigation only. No landing policy, source change, or additional run is authorized by this note. In the diagnostic P became airborne and shared horizontal space with B, which the model intentionally permits while vertically separated. The same duck can still be underneath at the end of a fixed flight window; overlapping at the landing transition is therefore a foreseeable geometry case, not inherently a model failure. One diagnostic does not establish its frequency.

The current study updates Bounce to landing and checks for overlap **before** movement and normal contact resolution. If overlap exists, it aborts the tick with `landing_overlap_unresolved`. The ordinary solver is called later in the tick, searches swept horizontal closest approaches, and only emits an impulse/contact when relative normal velocity is closing. Its positional separation is part of that actual-contact response. An already-overlapping pair that is stationary, separating, or exactly coincident may not generate a normal contact, and an old airborne swept closest point may occur before the landing transition. Merely deleting the guard would therefore neither guarantee a contact nor guarantee safe separation; it could also misorder contact, phase transitions and genuine finishes.

Option A: treat a duck landing into an occupied space as a new actual ground contact at the landing transition, using the normal pair identity, rearm policy and independent injury opportunities, with Bounce protection determined by the landing phase. A closing contact may reuse the ordinary response where its assumptions hold, but onset while stationary/separating, coincident centers, multiple overlaps and finish ties need explicitly defined, deterministic handling; normal closing-only solver cannot simply be assumed to resolve them. If the earlier collision is relevant, retain its prior impulse and injury history rather than retroactively undoing them. A collision at landing makes the result visible and could hurt the other duck even when P has full protection.

Option B: keep the current guard pending a separately designed safe landing rule; the study remains unable to finish some natural races. Another possible experience is an explicit duck-chosen landing maneuver or earlier avoidance, but its movement, timing and potential failure need owner direction and bounded study. Silently hovering, extending the ability, teleporting, deleting a contact, or moving a duck without an explicit policy would invent game behavior and is not recommended.

Owner experience question for orchestration: if a bouncing duck descends onto an occupied space, should that be a genuine collision with the duck underneath, subject to each duck's own injury risk? If yes, full versus partial protection still applies only to the Bounce participant in its defined active window; it does not protect the other duck. Handling geometry and tie ordering remains a technical proposal, not an owner-approved numerical balance.

Before a revised run, specify landing transition and finish/contact fraction ordering; duplicate suppression and separation/rearm; closing, separating and zero-relative-speed overlaps; coincident centers; multiple targets; already-injured participants; full/partial independent keyed draws; airborne-to-ground and airborne-to-airborne cases; existing collision/recovery preservation; genuine finish precedence and rollback; no-ability/control parity; and bounded failure behavior when the geometry cannot be resolved. Pure and arranged integrated fixtures must verify these, followed by separately cleared finite natural samples. No production integration, tuning, held-out sample or success of the stopped screen follows.
