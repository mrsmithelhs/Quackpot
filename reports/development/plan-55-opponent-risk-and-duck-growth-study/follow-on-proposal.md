# Plan 55 follow-on proposal — condition-aware racing and collision consequences

2026-11-03. Owner correction conveyed by QP Orch: a favorite need not be entered while fatigued or injured; Guard is intended to prevent injury to a competitive, rested favorite. This is a report-local proposal, not authority to run or change production. The first study remains a narrow repeated-favorite stress test and cannot stand in for that sensible policy or prove Guard failed.

## What the first experiment did and did not model

- Actual production race resolution has duck-to-duck contacts and immediate mechanical deflection/recovery; it does not currently implement the proposed persistent injury state. The report-local candidate applied persistent injury only to the player's selected duck, and only when an actual completed-race contact involved it. Other contact participants could not receive persistent candidate injuries. The resolver currently permits at most one duck-contact event globally per race. The first study therefore did not test a collision injuring multiple participants.
- Rival fictional policies chose steady/variable performance spreads according to their forecast probabilities (0.2 or 0.8 variable, depending on field); actual choices were independently seeded and recorded. This is not a full opponent risk profile: rivals had no persistent injury, Guard, development or modeled choice responding to their condition. Player steady/variable used accepted performance spreads and candidate conditional injury chances after actual player contact (20%/40%); Guard halved new-injury chance only after assignment. Neither pace nor rival posture was changed to directly modify contact frequency. Existing movement could still produce different actual contacts as trajectories diverged. Injury extension was independently 50% and not reduced by Guard.
- Favorite-policy cells deliberately entered the same duck every race, despite the accepted immediate-repeat 0.5 rest factor and any injury; the owner has clarified that this is not the intended sensible favorite behavior. Rotation avoided that fatigue but, in six opportunities, each of three ducks ran only twice and no one earned a point. Neither result establishes the correct ability size or asks for an automatic rest-rule change.

## Proposed next comparison — contract before numbers

Keep the original fixed stress trace and current race/no-injury controls. Add a **condition-aware favorite**: enter the favorite when fully rested and uninjured; otherwise choose a rested, uninjured owned alternative. If none is available, use a predeclared policy to wait via a real available life opportunity or race the least-impaired duck, with no forced purchase or unavailable-only-duck trap. Report the frequency and life cost of the fallback separately. Do not silently assume ordinary sleep clears accepted race rest: existing rest requires intervening completed races by other ducks. An injured duck stays selectable. Optional early bed as a candidate injury recovery remains a new rule, not selected production behavior.

For ability headroom, compare Burst, Guard and no-new-ability with **the same competitive, rested favorite, same field and seeds**, including actual placement, contact, new injury, subsequent opportunities and recovery cost. Define the matchup and starting development state transparently as a controlled fixture; distinguish a fixture that starts with an earned point from a genuine engine route reaching it. A non-contact race provides no Guard injury opportunity; a large difference in placement is not a prerequisite for Guard's value if it reduces actual injury and lost opportunities. Conversely, if candidate effects do not change any meaningful outcome, report that. No selection of bigger numbers or changed rest is implied.

For rotation, extend the *represented opportunity budget* enough to test when each of three ducks can earn its first choice under a fixed rotation, while separately checking whether a genuine engine life route can reach that many races under real meals, work, rent, health and competing commitments. The longer horizon and number of seeds must be set with QP Orch before execution. Report earned-point timing per duck, losses, fallback, injuries and life trade-offs. An attainable theoretical opportunity is not proof that a student will choose it.

## Collision and rival-injury choices for owner discussion

Immediate collision deflection and recovery remain the accepted race mechanics. Proposed persistent injury is a separate seeded post-race consequence tied to the actual contact record. For an eligible completed-race contact, every participant can receive its own independently seeded injury roll, with its own pace/Guard conditions; neither a rival-only collision nor a player collision fabricates another participant. Keep the current one-contact-per-race limit unless a separate change is selected.

**Rival lifecycle options** (none selected):

1. **Carry injury for recurring rivals**: a rival can start a later race injured, subject to an explicit recovery clock and raceable penalty. It makes a remembered opponent's condition meaningful, but requires persistent state, actual opportunity/recovery history, forecasts that reflect current known condition, and careful small-field availability. Candidate policy: recover after one intervening scheduled rival appearance or a predeclared non-race recovery opportunity; exact semantics must be fixed and tested before execution, not inferred from the player's rest history.
2. **Race-local injury outcomes only**: report which participants were injured after the event, then reset rival condition before its next appearance. This is smaller but can undermine the credibility of a recurring opponent's injury history unless the reset is visible and honestly described; it cannot answer whether injury affects later rival choices.
3. **Keep rival injury out of the follow-on**: continue only the player's persistent consequence and label the asymmetry. This retains a simple control but cannot investigate a collision's consequences for both ducks, the concern that prompted this proposal.

Recommended investigation direction: option 1, with a small fixed roster and explicit rival recovery/choice rules; compare option 3 as a causal control if the resource budget permits. Before executable work specify whether a rival can race injured and whether racing prolongs its injury, when recovery occurs between appearances, what the player can actually know, and how simultaneous participant rolls and event evidence work. No invented run history or claim that rivals are real connected people. Each participant's roll uses a distinct seed key, so checking one duck cannot change another's result. If the resolver's one-contact limit or sparse contacts makes this uninformative, report the null rather than forcing collisions.
