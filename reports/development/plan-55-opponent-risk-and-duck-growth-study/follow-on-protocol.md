## Questions, fixtures, and fixed policies

1. In a competitive matchup with a rested, uninjured entrant and an already earned point, can Burst and Guard change a meaningful outcome? Compare no ability, Burst and Guard, with post-race-only and same-race onset. The point is a declared synthetic initial condition, not a claim that a life route earned it. Common input rating 10 against field rating 10, rare 14 against field 14. Each is a deliberately near-matched *candidate* fixture, not a revised production field or fairness proof. Opponents have no selected ability in the headroom stage. Same seed/field/entrant roster across paired arms. Pace is steady in this first headroom stage; do not claim both paces tested here.
2. Over 12 represented races, does a condition-aware favorite or fixed rotation reach a point and have opportunities to use it? Three controlled common ducks, same rating 10; first field rating 8 then 11.2, as current game. Favorite policy chooses the favorite when fully rested and uninjured; otherwise choose fully rested, uninjured alternative with lowest ID. If none exists, choose the non-injured duck with highest current rest factor, ties favorite then ID; if all injured, choose highest rest factor, same ties. All remain raceable, no forced purchase. Fixed rotation chooses 0,1,3 in order irrespective of condition; record if it selects injured/tired. These are deterministic models, not prescriptions for a human. No waiting/early-bed healing in the main model. Apply each duck's own current rest policy (never-raced 1, zero/one/two intervening other completed races 0.5/0.75/1), not elapsed-day recovery. A completed race grants one point after that duck's third completion, bankable until it is next selected, then permanently assigned per the arm; no more points. Compare no-ability, Burst and Guard, and include both injury modes. With three rotating ducks each should earn a point after its third personal completed race and can use it at its fourth if 12 completed opportunities occur; unresolved attempts invalidate that expectation and do not grant progress.
3. Preserve the original six-race losing-favorite results and unchanged race/no-injury control as recorded historical controls, not a rerun or proof of normal favorite behavior. In this follow-on include one no-injury/no-growth control per policy in the 12-opportunity stage to isolate new mechanisms. Life traces are separate: prior six-race baseline and three-race limited-cash boundary do not prove 12 starts; a later genuine public-API route and its time, rent, health and social costs require a separately bounded stage and root allocation. This matrix is a race-opportunity fixture, not a claim of a playable 12-race life path.

## Candidate consequences and field continuity

An actual eligible duck-contact record supplies its two active participants, and at most one globally per race remains the accepted cap. Wall impacts are **not injury-eligible** in this comparison; they retain existing collision/slowdown behavior and can be counted separately. No invented contact, forced contact, seeded filtering or silent contact-rate modification. At a genuine contact, each participant independently rolls new injury using stable `(seed, raceId, contactId, entrantId, injury)` keys: steady 0.20, variable 0.40, halved by that participant's already-assigned Guard. These rates are first-study candidates, not selected game odds. Existing injury and a new event never compound into two injury stacks.

- **Post-race-only:** make/record the participant-specific roll at the collision for pairing/audit, but do not alter the race's motion. Commit persistent injury only for a completed resolution; unresolved attempts keep the tentative trace and receive no persistent injury, growth or reward. This is an explicit difference from the first study's player-only candidate, so do not label it exact numerical replication.
- **Same-race:** after the genuine contact response, a successful roll adds one injury state immediately for each affected participant. Starting with the next motion tick, apply a 0.95 multiplier to that entrant's *uninjured race target-speed cap* for the remainder of this race; use existing acceleration/braking to approach the cap, without rewriting previous positions, contact response, trajectories, finish crossing or result. This new 0.95 physical-cap probe is not the 0.9 future input penalty. A duck entering already injured retains its already-applied persistent input penalty; do not additionally apply the same-race cap a second time on a subsequent injury event. Record tick, participant, draw key, before/after target cap and applicability. The cap changes subsequent physics only, never crossing order by fiat. A tentative same-race effect may affect an unresolved race's trace; no persistent event is committed after an unresolved attempt. Guard must be determined before rolling. Collision mechanical recovery remains independent of the injury cap.
- **Next races:** persistent injury multiplies that duck's current rest-adjusted rating input by 0.9 once; Burst's 50%-seeded +1 rating input is applied before rest and injury, as the first study. Each previously injured duck that races has an independently keyed 0.5 extension roll; otherwise its prior injury clears after the race. New contact injury is then applied, leaving one injury at most. A duck absent for one completed intervening race recovers before the next race. No passive clock or early bed is assumed in the main matrix.

Use a fixed six-rival fictional pool supplying five entrants each race. Each has a stable authored identity and independent seeded steady/variable policy; actual field order alternates probability 0.2/0.8 with seed parity counterbalancing. Prefer uninjured rivals, rotating a fixed order; if fewer than five uninjured are available, fill remaining places with injured rivals in fixed order—no injury-based unavailability trap. A rival absent for one completed race recovers before selection for the next; an injured rival racing uses the same 0.9 input penalty and 0.5 extension rule. This is a simple experimental lifecycle, not evidence that opponents strategically optimize. Opponent Guard/ability is none in main cells, so Guard's participant rule is validated synthetically but not an invented rival progression system. A rival known-condition forecast is the last actual completed-race state plus the deterministic absence/recovery rule; mark it as model-known information, not fabricated observations. Record forecast, actual field, pace choice, contact participants/rolls, injury onsets, extensions, recovery, results and missingness. Unresolved outcomes do not silently change known histories. A later UI must decide what the player sees; no marketing or real-player claims follow.

Do not interpret post-race placement equality as evidence against Guard. For same-race onset apply the 0.95 factor to the **final effective target-speed calculation**, consistently after any R2 assistance and leader effects, on ticks after onset. Condition-aware favorite first chooses the favorite if fully rested and healthy, then any fully rested healthy alternative by lowest ID. Otherwise maximize `authored rating × rest factor × (0.9 if injured else 1)`, ties favorite then ascending ID. This replaces the earlier uninjured-first fallback, which could choose a more impaired duck. No extra policy arm is added. Existing original repeated-favorite results remain historical controls.
