# Follow-on Stage A — rested headroom results

2026-11-03. Eight fixed seeds, two near-matched synthetic rating/field fixtures (10/10 common and 14/14 rare), three abilities and two injury-onset modes: 96 completed races, eight per cell. Entrants start rested and the point is assumed already earned; these are not engine life routes or production balance. Burst is the first study's whole-race rating proxy. The prior repeated-fatigued favorite remains a separate negative control, not a model of a sensible favorite policy.

All 96 races finished, with no sixth-place outcomes. The new fixture exposes a **near-first-place ceiling** instead: common and rare fixtures each averaged 1.125 places for Burst and 1.25 for Guard/no ability under post-race onset; all three averaged 1.375 under same-race onset. Lower placement is better. These are tiny, paired exploratory samples; identical averages or results do not establish ability equivalence. Post-race Guard cannot change the placement of that same race by design, and its future availability value is outside Stage A.

Every eight-race cell produced eight genuine duck contacts, but only one involved the player. In each Guard cell the player therefore had exactly one injury-prevention opportunity and was injured in that case; each cell had five injured rival participants. The single player exposure per rating/mode is too sparse to assess Guard's value. Counts repeat across paired modes/abilities and across the two synthetic ratings and must not be treated as independent student or player samples. Collision can affect two actual participants; wall impacts remain ineligible in this experiment.

For the common same-race condition, paired seed-level placements (none / Burst / Guard) were: `000: 1/1/1`, `001: 2/2/2`, `002: 1/1/1`, `003: 3/3/3`, `004: 1/1/1`, `005: 1/1/1`, `006: 1/1/1`, `007: 1/1/1`. Only seed 003 involved the player in contact for Guard. This small sample cannot establish a meaningful advantage, risk sensitivity, or absence of an effect in other fields.

The recorded same-race outputs contain 36 participant target witnesses across paired cells, six with assistance active and zero with leader slowdown active; these are repeated observations, not 36 independent races. A pure test checked counterfactual arithmetic, but a real leader-slowdown overlap remains unwitnessed. The candidate injury factor is applied to the final target from the tick after an actual contact, and target vs actual speed differ. Do not infer readability or production correctness from these model traces.
