## An honest first-threshold example

The selected free-path first point needs 4 progress; an entered duck earns 2 for any completed race plus 1 for finishing top three. A candidate effective 2-progress rate is equivalent, *for the first four-point threshold*, to multiplying newly earned progress by 2. A candidate effective 1-progress rate corresponds to multiplying new progress by 4. If a duck already has 3 of 4 when the boost is bought, neither proposal awards a point immediately. A later completed race outside the top three earns 2 ordinary progress; under either candidate it crosses the first threshold on that race. At zero prior progress, a single completed loss supplies the equivalent of 4 boosted progress under the 2-progress candidate and 8 under the 1-progress candidate. A top-three result supplies 6 or 12 respectively. These examples show credited progress, not actual repeatable points: only the first four-point threshold is selected, while later thresholds, overflow use, progression curves and repeat ability investment are undefined. A permanent point is not silently granted repeatedly until those rules are chosen. Because free progression already gets the first point after any two completed races, the immediate benefit here is chiefly reducing that wait to one; any longer-term value requires a separately specified progression model.
