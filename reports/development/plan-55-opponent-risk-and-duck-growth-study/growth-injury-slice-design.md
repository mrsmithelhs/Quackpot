# First duck-growth choice and injury lifecycle - owner interview draft

Status: report-local design, not implementation authorization or numerical balance. Owner selected in this chat on 2026-11-04: the first duck-earned, bankable, permanent point may be assigned to Bounce or crowd acceleration, without consuming a life slot; an injured duck remains raceable and recovers as in-game days pass, with no required purchase. Earlier owner preferences: every completed race advances the entered duck, stronger finishes and event goals can add progress, racing while injured may extend recovery, and accessible low/medium/high risk plus optional exact conditional odds are preferred. Full Bounce injury protection during its active window is selected for now; actual collision effects remain and the other participant's injury risk is independent (DECISION-084).

## First growth choice - selected working values

Each entered owned duck has its own progress and point bank. The owner selected the following tunable starting values in this chat on 2026-11-04. A completed race gives 2 progress even in last place; top-three finish adds 1, so two losses earn the first point at 4, and a first-place then a loss gives 5. No progress for a cancelled/unresolved race; an unrelated duck does not silently gain entered-duck progress. A point is not awarded twice for one committed race. The owner explicitly chose the four-point threshold over five: a top-three bonus does not accelerate the first point, though the recorded extra progress may matter once later progression is defined. The player can leave it banked or assign it permanently to Bounce or crowd acceleration without spending an additional life slot. Explain the choice and permanence before confirmation. This first slice needs only this first point; later thresholds, repeat investment, event-goal bonuses, starting abilities by rarity and eventual combinations remain open. The example does not override the preference for stronger finishes/goals to add progress and does not prove an ordinary actual run has enough races. A 3-duck player who rotates gets two races per duck after six races and could reach each first point; actual access and life costs still need a bounded real-path check.

Bounce automatically prepares when its candidate predictive conditions occur; full injury protection is active in the selected window. It can still have a real collision and can injure another duck. Crowd acceleration is a separate automatic ability whose temporary acceleration is subject to the existing target and opportunities; neither choice promises to activate, prevent a collision or win a race. Candidate exact probabilities/durations, animation and numerical effectiveness need a tested contract; do not advertise an unsupported speed or victory guarantee. For a duck that later already starts with one ability, whether a first investment improves an existing ability or unlocks the other remains a separate design question; do not silently assign a duplicate.

## Injury lifecycle - selected starting values and open boundaries

A new injury occurs on an actual independently evaluated contact, with participant-specific event/roll evidence and current Bounce protection. The duck remains selectable and receives the selected starting 10% race-rating penalty while injured, distinct from normal rest; if the player nevertheless races a duck already injured at commit, show a simple extension-risk label with accessible exact conditional odds before committing. A race entered healthy that later causes an injury is not also treated as entering injured. The owner selected that a new injury affects performance during the same race and that a duck already injured at entry has a 25% chance per completed race to add one day. Candidate ordering: record the actual collision and injury first, apply the altered race-rating input to the race target from the next simulation tick without retroactively moving or slowing the duck, and draw the extension once at completed-result commit, using a separately keyed deterministic roll. Do not draw the extension for a cancelled or unresolved race. These exact event/transaction boundaries are a technical proposal for QP Orch review, not an already-tested production rule. No new injury is stacked on an already injured duck unless separately designed. Explain any injury plus rest composition in the choice UI rather than presenting a falsely simple final speed.

The selected starting recovery duration is two in-game day changes. Illustration: injury during a race on day 2 starts with two day changes remaining. On day 3 it has one remaining; if it is not raced injured, the day-4 transition clears it for free. If the duck starts and completes a race on day 3 while still injured and the 25% extension roll succeeds, it has two day changes remaining again: day 4 reduces that to one and day 5 clears it if no further extension occurs. Precise sequencing at transition, ties between extension and recovery, and whether playing/racing during a day affects that day must be specified before production; do not silently heal via elapsed real-world time, waiting, paid item, or UI-only clock. A single-duck player can continue racing with the disclosed downside and cannot be trapped behind a purchase.

## Working event and rating contract - proposed implementation details

For an already-injured entrant, apply the injury factor to its authored rating separately from the accepted rest factor, before the existing rating-to-track-speed conversion. For example, with rating 10, rest factor 0.5 and injury factor 0.9, the combined rating input is 4.5; the accepted affine conversion then maps it to 10.5, before any other existing race effects. A 10% rating penalty is not a promise that visible track speed will fall by exactly 10%. A newly injured entrant follows the same input logic for its remaining race after the actual collision. Avoid replacing existing velocity outright; the resolver applies its normal acceleration/braking to the updated target. This mapping is an implementation proposal requiring source-pinned technical review and may require adaptation to the final resolver, not a simulated result.

One proposed deterministic ordering: capture injury state and remaining day changes at race commitment; resolve race/collisions; at successful completed-result commit, award the entered duck's progress once and, only if it entered injured, draw once at 25% for +1 remaining day. Persist actual roll/outcome. A duck newly injured during that race receives its two-day recovery state but no separate extension roll merely for that new injury. An unrelated duck's race or point assignment is not a day transition. Each actual in-game day change decrements remaining days once, clearing on reaching zero; ordinary elapsed real time and repeated reloads do not. If day advancement and a pending result can coincide, transaction order must be specified and checked rather than inferred. An unresolved race earns neither completed-race progress nor a completed-race extension draw. The owner selects that an injury actually shown to the player before a later unresolved race remains in force. Do not silently erase it or award a finish, reward, progress or completed-race extension. The engine resolves before playback, so making persistence depend on whether playback was watched would let skip, reload or reduced motion change a mechanical outcome. The owner confirms that any validated, retained injury from the unresolved attempt remains even when playback is skipped or reduced motion is used, with an accessible no-result explanation. The proposed technical implementation commits it exactly once as an atomic partial outcome, independent of view callbacks; an event discarded by guard rollback is not retained or applied. Do not falsely claim an unseen visual was shown; actual presentation and committed mechanics must be separately truthful. Exact save/replay transaction and nonfinish behavior remain root technical gates.

The top-three bonus gives extra accumulated progress even though it cannot accelerate the first point at a four-point threshold. Whether unused progress carries toward a later point, what later thresholds are, and how a rarity-granted starting ability relates to an investment stay open; the first slice must not imply those rules are settled.
