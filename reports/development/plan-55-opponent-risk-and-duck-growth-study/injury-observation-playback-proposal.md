# Injury-model observation playback — proposal for owner/root review

2026-11-03. Owner asks how the two injury rules might feel while watching races, especially repeated visually serious pileups without injuries. The existing susceptibility comparison observed 12 versus 27 newly injured entrant-races out of 48 under diagnostic first-contact versus per-distinct-contact rules, in eight paired races per arm. Entries are correlated, every duck initially healthy, and same-race injury alters future trajectories. These numbers do not establish what a viewer perceives or that either model is balanced. Production remains unchanged.

## What the existing evidence can support

The saved `injury-susceptibility-results/{first,contact}.json` files contain actual recorded contacts, tick, participant IDs, eligible rolls/outcomes, injury target witnesses and individual finish times. They do **not** retain full movement trajectories or images. A static event timeline can truthfully show those saved events without new race computation, but cannot demonstrate what the race looked like or whether contacts appeared large. Plan57 galleries contain separately authored synthetic events and cannot be relabeled as these races.

For true motion playback, a later explicitly approved report-local process would need to rerun the exact pinned resolver inputs and retain their full trajectories, then verify that regenerated compact contact/roll/crossing records match saved evidence before presenting them. If the historical dependency closure cannot be reproduced byte-for-byte or any comparison fails, stop and present the saved timeline only; do not construct plausible substitute motion or relabel a new simulation as old evidence. A replay costs new resolver calls and is subject to root's proposed scope/resource gate. Use a local isolated preview and preserve owner browser/server.

## Suggested bounded review and example selection

A first static, report-local paired timeline could show seed `p55m-000` under each injury rule: recorded first-contact arm has 11 contact events and zero newly injured entrants; the per-contact arm has seven contacts and three newly injured entrants. Select this seed explicitly because the already-read data show the contrast the owner asked about; it is an illustrative, **post-result-selected** example, not a representative or statistically independent sample. An optional second seed `p55m-001` has four contacts in each arm and four versus five newly injured entrants; it demonstrates that both rules can produce several injuries. Selection is disclosed in advance of any view or further call, no hidden search, and each arm's actual participants, tick, probability, roll eligibility, injury and finish order are shown. Don't call a contact a pileup or imply visual severity solely from count.

If owner wants motion, propose at most four explicitly named replay calls (two seeds × two arms), no alternate seeds and no odds changes. Show individual, genuinely recorded contact participants and timing, first injury onset and subsequent mechanical events, with equal presentation vocabulary in both arms. A visual burst communicates a contact; its shape/intensity should not encode invented mechanical severity. If a contact actually changes movement, use the actual trajectory. Transient steering recovery and persistent injury are distinct. Do not fabricate exact severity, near miss, unrecorded injury, a visible cue or a specific prevented collision. Label times, participating ducks and injury results factually, with static/reduced-motion equivalents.

## Interpretation and comparison

The question for a viewer is whether observed events and injuries feel coherent and varied, not whether one dramatic cue must injure a duck. Under first-contact policy, later collisions for a duck with its one roll already used have no new opportunity; that hidden rule may matter to interpretation. Under per-distinct-contact, new genuine eligible events can carry additional risk until injured; it may cause frequent injuries or visually busy races. Same seed does **not** guarantee the two versions show the same sequence: injury changes future motion and contact opportunities. Show each actual sequence rather than implying a controlled visual counterfactual. No comparative inference from two examples about typical frequency, learner perception or final balance.
