## Contract and study questions

A contact is a genuine recorded pair-contact episode involving active ducks. A pair can trigger again only after actual separation to at least 1.2 distance units and at least 10 ticks from the prior pair event, under the current candidate episode rules; continuous overlap alone never creates a fresh injury opportunity. Both actual uninjured participants can receive independently keyed draws; already newly injured ducks have no further new-injury rolls in that race, but their subsequent contacts are recorded. Contact effects, same-race injury effects and persistent state are separate; no stacks or retroactive effects. The same-race 0.95 target from the last diagnostic study is held solely as an experimental assumption, not selected balance. All entrants start uninjured; the screen has no Guard, Bounce or crowd ability. Existing injury, recovery, multi-race costs and small-roster consequences are outside this screen and require a separately reviewed model.

Does candidate predictive defensive advice actually reduce sustained crowding or repeated contacts while preserving a lively race? At plausible low conditional injury rates, what range of outcomes occurs given the resulting exposure? Which candidate merits a held-out check? Numbers cannot establish what a player considers a believable or satisfying event; root and owner visual inspection are separate.

## Measures and interpretation

Per race and arm retain ordered contact IDs, ticks, pairs, rearm evidence, each participant's risk/stance, actual eligibility, draw key/value/threshold and injury onset; player and rivals separately; total contacts and contacts by pair/duck, repeat-pair counts and concentration. Record fraction of races with zero/one/multiple injured ducks, per-duck exposed opportunities and injury incidence/timing, and all races with several contact episodes and no injury. Treat race as the primary independent unit and participant summaries as nested, not 96 independent races.

Measure local crowding from actual active grounded positions: at each recorded sample, a duck has a crowded sample if at least two other active grounded ducks are within 4 units and within 8 units along the course (shorter circular arc). Report sampled duration for each duck, longest continuous episode and a race-level duration distribution, using actual time intervals and disclosing sampling/interpolation gaps. This is a reproducible proximity proxy, not a definition of a visible pileup; also report quick contact clusters and repeated pairs without equating them to pileups. Pin the trajectory sampling interval and prevent gaps from being counted as sustained crowding. Track recovery interruptions and advice eligible/followed/cancelled, separately from outcomes. Review event-to-motion correspondence before claims of visual severity.

Report all actual individual crossings, winner and last crossing, rank order/churn and player-versus-field stat differences; note baseline player 10 versus field 8 and selected mapping, rather than attributing rank changes to injury without controls. Retain genuine finishes/unresolved statuses. Compare the winner distribution to accepted median 17–23 seconds and at most 5% strictly above 23 seconds, while recognizing this small sample is insufficient to validate the tail. Record full last-crossing/playback separately; the accepted two-second highlight is presentation overhead, and the study cannot assert actual browser playback from headless timestamps.

Predeclare that no single collision count, injury rate, proximity threshold or finish statistic automatically chooses a winner. A credible candidate should avoid persistent crowding and contact chatter, retain meaningful but not ubiquitous injury, respect actual race controls, and make risk effects intelligible. Report every arm including unfavorable results, uncertainty and observed tradeoffs. Owner and root must inspect representative and adverse examples identified by transparent criteria, not only highlights; no numerical proxy proves subjective believability.

## Root review refinements — 2026-11-03

Historical corrected reactive advice is a baseline for provenance, not the Stage 1 predictive candidate. Every 20 ticks (1.0 s at dt 0.05 s), it examines current relative positions and velocities; it does not predict a future collision over a time horizon. At least two nearby course-local neighbors and at least one currently closing neighbor are required. A seeded decision follows advice with probability 0.75 for steady or 0.35 for Sprint. It selects a feasible less-occupied side, targets a 0.4-unit lateral offset for at most 20 ticks, and uses existing recentering and turn/acceleration caps, with contact/recovery taking priority. Corridor half-width is 6; ordinary physics include 3 units/s² base acceleration, 6 braking, and 30–90 degrees/s speed-dependent turn caps, subject to existing R2 assistance. The correction relative to the earlier exploratory implementation cancels stale advice during an active steering episode and fixes assist attribution; it does not itself introduce a new anticipation horizon or prove lower crowding. The absence of predictive lookahead is a candidate limitation; if root/owner requires it, stop and specify a separate algorithm before running rather than claim the current heuristic meets that aim.

The model uses abstract course-coordinate distance units (the source's lookahead name says meters; no physical meter-scale claim follows). Duck radius is 0.5; the 1.2-unit rearm separation exceeds the 1.0-unit sum of radii, and ten ticks are 0.5 seconds. Both conditions are conservative candidate thresholds to separate episodes, not owner-selected realism. Tests and per-event evidence must show initial entry, continuous overlap without duplicate roll, separation and re-entry, two alternating pairs, contact revalidation after impulse, and wall-contact separation. ## Predictive defensive-driving candidate and source seam

Stage 1 proposes a deliberately modest prediction using only current position and velocity, not future random draws or knowledge of future steering. Every five ticks (0.25 seconds), for each active, grounded, non-recovering duck, consider active grounded neighbors within 8 units along the course and 6 units Euclidean distance. Let relative position r = neighbor position minus own position and relative velocity v = neighbor velocity minus own velocity. If v·v is nonzero and r·v < 0, compute time of closest approach t = -(r·v)/(v·v), and predicted separation |r + v t|. Threat exists only when 0 < t <= 1.0 second and projected separation is below 1.4 units. This linear estimate can be wrong, particularly around bends and when either duck steers; it is not a simulated future collision or perfect avoidance. Select minimum projected separation, tie by stable duck ID. No threat means no advice.

For a threat, use the same independently keyed follow probabilities 0.75 steady / 0.35 Sprint and the existing less-occupied feasible-side selector with 0.4-unit lateral target. Limit one active advice episode to 20 ticks, do not refresh while active, and cancel it when contact or recovery takes priority. Apply no separate acceleration or speed boost; preserve existing course, steering, R2 assist and acceleration caps. No ability proximity modifier, Bounce, or injury occurs in this traffic screen. These numbers are provisional study settings and must not be adopted because they sound plausible. A pure geometry test must cover closing vs diverging, zero relative speed, closest approach beyond the one-second horizon, projected near miss, curve-local exclusion, deterministic tie, and no future-state input. Additional tests cover risk-conditioned decisions with controlled draws, feasible steering/cap boundaries, active-advice cancellation, and no new effect when advice disabled.

Proposed seam: a new report-local pure prediction/decision module plus its pure tests; a separate report-local adapter copied from the corrected susceptibility adapter, changing only its advice sampling/selection wiring and required audit evidence while leaving the historical source untouched; a finite three-arm runner, analysis module and exclusive output directory. Before execution, root must review exact diff/source hashes, static import closure, source-specific guard and rollback behavior, arm commands, seed inputs, call counts and output ceilings. Prepare and test without production writes, with pure correctness calls and all race calls separately coordinated. No new traffic candidate or values may be silently substituted after results.
