# Plan 55 — preliminary owner comparison

These are report-local modeled results, not production behavior or evidence of student engagement. Owner selected the experience for investigation, not these numbers. Sixteen fixed seeds, six nominal races each, one- and three-common controlled rosters, nine fixed policies; 1,728 races. Two actual-engine life traces informed the six-opportunity scenario separately; the synthetic race rosters and candidate mechanics were not inserted into engine saves.

| Question | What this small comparison observed | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do rival forecasts change a modeled pace choice? | Steady versus variable yielded the same direction in both field types; steady averaged about 0.02 places better in each roster/field group. | No useful field-dependent pace reversal observed. Order was counterbalanced, but fields and earlier decisions still have carryover; this is not proof no design can work. |
| Burst or Guard? | Paired placement difference was zero for Burst versus Guard for both pace policies, field types and roster sizes. In each one-duck steady arm there were five new injuries and five extensions across 96 races; variable arms had eight and five. | This candidate did not produce a measurable choice advantage here. The two ability effects need not be equally valuable; the experiment failed to distinguish them. |
| Race the favorite or rotate? | In the three-duck scenario rotation improved average placement by about 2.25–2.44 places versus repeatedly racing a favorite, depending on field. | Existing soft rest is a much stronger lever here. With six races and three ducks, rotation gave each duck only two races, so none earned the proposed point. |
| Can a losing favorite reach the point? | In the controlled favorite policy every duck entered all six races and earned its one point after race three; the point was assigned before race four. | Reachable in this fixed opportunity budget, not proof that ordinary learners will take it. One actual-engine baseline reached six races and paid rent; another deliberately missed a shift and stopped after three with $164 against $170 due. |

One illustrative integration seed: a lone common duck placed first, then sixth in the next five races; its point was earned after race three, assigned before race four, and Burst activated on race six without changing that placement. This is one trace, not a typical player story. The 0.5 rest factor on immediate repeat applies before the accepted affine mapping; it does not make final track speed half.

Prespecified bootstrap intervals resample seeds, not individual races. They are descriptive for this small fixed set, not a guarantee of general balance. Identical outcomes produce [0,0] empirical intervals here; they do not establish the effects are universally identical. Forecast fields change rival performance spread but do not directly cause contact; counts are actual model outcomes, not assumed tactical behavior. Six races cannot measure longer-term runaway power, purchases, or full-life outcomes. The simulation does not establish comprehension, engagement, accessibility, production save/replay, or behavior on devices.

The recommended next question is whether Burst and Guard can affect outcomes in a suitable matchup or training state while retaining the current rest rule and this negative common-duck case as controls. A favorite and roster rotation need not be mutually exclusive. Do not change rest or numeric effects without a new bounded proposal and owner decision. Independent review findings and dispositions are recorded below; QP Orch and owner decisions remain pending.

## Independent audit and reviewer limitations

The trace above was selected before the matrix as the fixed integration-check seed `plan55-000`; it was not picked after inspecting outcomes. Reviewer requested model `gpt-6.1-terra`, effort `medium`, depth-one read-only by instruction. The reviewer could not attest its observed model or effort; the orchestration request specified them, but this is not independent runtime attestation. The reviewer did not execute simulations or edit files. The post-review Git status showed only the expected Plan55 report work plus Plan52's unrelated untracked startup artifact; this is a post-hoc check, not structural read-only enforcement.

The reviewer identified a decisive **placement floor**: after assignment every Burst and Guard favorite-policy race placed sixth. Independent aggregation verified 48/48 sixth places per cell and roster (C2/C3/C4/C5). Consequently equal placements and empirical [0,0] intervals cannot establish ability balance. Accepted. A second finding noted that the fixed analysis file does not itself summarize every planned contact, payout, point, loss, and stacking measure, and that growth comparisons blend pre- and post-assignment races. Accepted; the following read-only aggregation fills the named bounded measures without new simulation, while post-assignment headroom and longer horizons remain open.

- For one-common C2 and C3, each had 16/48 player-contact races in mostly-steady fields and 13/48 in mostly-variable; each earned 400 Gems per field. In the three-common favorite policy the same duck is selected throughout, so those results duplicate the one-common favorite policy; they are not independent evidence.
- Each steady favorite arm had 80 losses in 96 races, 800 total Gems, 16 points earned (one per seed), and eight races combining prior injury with a rest factor below 1 in the injury-on Burst arm. Rotation with three commons had 72 losses, 1,790 Gems, zero points and zero injured-plus-tired races in its 96 observations. These are outcomes for the frozen policies, not calibrated rewards.
- Post-assignment, each roster's C2 and C3 had 11 player-contact races and one new injury in 48 races. C4 had ten contacts and one new injury; C5 had eleven contacts and one new injury. Paired counts do not prove Guard is ineffective generally.
- All 288 run identities and 1,728 finished race records were present; an independent audit found no recorded eligibility without player contact, Burst before assignment, or assigned ability before three completed races. Reviewer separately checked injury-state continuity; that check did not independently reconstruct every draw.

No reviewer findings were rejected. Review cost: one depth-one read-only consultation and local verification, with no extra model or simulation runs.
