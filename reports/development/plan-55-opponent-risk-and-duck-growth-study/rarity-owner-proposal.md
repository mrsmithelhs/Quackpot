# Owner proposal — rarity through abilities and risk range

2026-11-03. Owner proposes investigating an alternative to a starting speed advantage for rarer ducks: equal initial base speed, a small starting amount of one special ability, a higher ability-development ceiling, and a wider risk spectrum at both protective and aggressive ends. This is an owner proposal for investigation, not an adopted production rule, numeric contract, code change or execution clearance. Existing production ratings and prior evidence remain unchanged. Clarification: these are three proposed characteristics.

A small starting ability may still create an immediate practical advantage despite equal base speed. A higher ceiling is meaningful only if a duck can approach useful development within realistic nonspending race opportunities; otherwise it is an inaccessible promise. Investigate cumulative power, first choice and opportunity cost, and preserve useful Common-duck paths. A broader risk spectrum should make both ends legible and consequential; wider variation alone need not increase average performance. Define injury, actual contact, effects, disclosures and accessible equivalents before execution, never assume time or spending produces a balanced outcome.

The completed Stage A rested fixtures used Common 10 versus field 10 and Rare 14 versus field 14, both with a synthetic already-earned ability point. They did not compare equal starting speed, starting ability grants, rarity ceilings or a wider risk spectrum. Prepared Stage B uses Common-only controlled rosters; it could supply a mechanistic one-point baseline, but cannot validate this rarity proposal. No Stage B run has been authorized. A separate bounded contract and owner/orchestration gate must decide whether to investigate the rarity proposal after that baseline or instead of it; avoid combining every axis into a large factorial. Preserve original results and selected production rest/race rules as controls, no silent rarity retune or new production implementation.

The owner adds that, with more abilities than Burst and Guard, rarer ducks could have interesting combinations of abilities. This extends the proposal beyond simply upgrading a single ability. Plan54's `owner-selections.md` records the owner's earlier ideas: a duck could earn both offensive and defensive abilities, individual ducks might have ability variants, and higher rarities might have more potential abilities (illustratively one for Common and two for the next tier). Those were exploration candidates, not fixed slot counts or an adopted catalog. The current proposal suggests combinations as a possible source of character and strategy; investigate interaction clarity, cumulative power, attainable combinations and meaningful Common choices before any design or execution gate.
