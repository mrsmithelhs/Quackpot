## Two injury-exposure policies to compare

Use an explicitly chosen *conditional* base probability p by actual participant's pace, with Guard's multiplier applied before its own roll if equipped/eligible. Numeric p remains unselected; the earlier 20%/40% example was once per duck per qualifying race and must not silently become a percentage for every contact. Injury is a status, not a stack of repeated speed penalties. Actual contact participants are independently keyed; a contact involving only rivals cannot injure the player. Current accepted collision recovery is distinct from candidate persistent injury.

- **One eligible roll per duck per completed race:** first genuine eligible duck-contact by that duck provides its one new-injury roll for that race; further contacts do not create another new-injury roll. Same contact may create independent rolls for both participants. A duck with an existing injury is not given a second persistent injury stack; define its separate extension once per completed race and report any contact involvement separately.
- **One eligible roll per duck per distinct contact episode:** each actual distinct contact involving a currently uninjured duck permits one independently keyed new-injury roll. Once newly injured, no additional new-injury rolls for that duck that race; later contacts are still recorded. Existing injury has the same single extension decision as the first policy, not an extension roll every frame/contact. Pair rearm/episode logic governs eligibility; mere overlap cannot repeatedly roll.

For post-race-only onset, candidate rolls may be recorded at actual contacts but persistent state commits only after a completed race; tentative decisions remain visible as tentative on unresolved races. For same-race onset, a successful first injury changes only subsequent simulated motion under an explicitly pinned candidate effect, never before the collision, and commits persistent state only on completion. Do not combine the same-race and future-race penalties into a double penalty. If the race cannot complete, report status and tentative events without awarding progress or silently converting it to a finished race. First compare one onset timing with a fixed traffic candidate; add another timing only as a separately approved comparison, not an unbounded cross-product.

## Illustrative exposure traces — arithmetic, not predicted rates

1. **One collision:** A and B have one actual eligible contact. Each uninjured duck has one independent opportunity under either policy. An injury is not guaranteed, and A's result does not dictate B's.
2. **Three separate collisions, no injury on first two:** A contacts B, then C, then B after that pair has truly separated/rearmed. Under the once-per-race rule A has one opportunity; under per-contact it can have up to three, stopping after its first new injury. If p were hypothetically 20% for each independent opportunity, the chance of at least one injury across three opportunities would be 48.8%, versus 20% for one opportunity. This arithmetic assumes all three contacts actually occur and eligibility/draws remain independent; altered same-race injury can change later paths, so it is not a predicted game frequency.
3. **Already injured duck:** A enters injured, experiences two collisions and remains raceable. The proposed model records both contacts and one separate post-race extension decision; it does not create two new injury stacks or two extension rolls. The severity/length and future action trade-off remain candidates.
