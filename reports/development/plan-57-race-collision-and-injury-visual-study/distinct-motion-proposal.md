## Comparison design

Use the same authored contact, two participants, starting pose and one-second white return in all three arms, so the effect's movement is the meaningful difference. Show a six-duck race-scale scene and enlarged comparison, with deliberate playback, pause, seek and useful stills. Keep prior A/B/C and their evidence as historical reference.

- **Angular pop:** a compact irregular paper shape rapidly expands from the contact point, then contracts and disappears. Its movement is radial and coherent; it does not travel across the scene.
- **Directional tear:** an irregular paper slash opens across the contact point in a fixed displayed direction, travels a short distance, then separates or retracts. Label the direction a synthetic presentation choice, not a physics vector or an injury.
- **Scattered fragments:** several small paper pieces spring outward along distinct paths, slow and settle/fade. This is distributed motion rather than a single expanding or travelling silhouette.

Initial proposed visual window is approximately 0.85 seconds, with a fast onset and a distinct later phase for each candidate; timings are provisional review settings. Static/reduced motion shows a single stable representative cue, participant symbol and text, without flash or looping. Injury status and the attached bandage remain independent of a collision. Preserve multiple contacts, cropped views, labels, resize handling, two independent instances and teardown.

For a first controlled comparison, compute all poses directly from the supplied presentation-clock age: pop grows from 0.35× to 1× over 0–0.12 s, holds briefly, then contracts/fades by 0.65 s; tear grows horizontally over 0–0.18 s, translates by roughly half a cue width by 0.50 s, then fades by 0.85 s; four chips move along fixed distinct outward vectors, decelerate and fade by 0.85 s. These are animation-study constants, not race velocities or selected production timings. One-second whitening remains the same in all three. No automatic repetitions, random variation or outcome-dependent changes.
