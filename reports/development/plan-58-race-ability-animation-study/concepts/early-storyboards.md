# Early checkpoint storyboards — draft, not selected artwork

Each panel uses an authored synthetic event, with labeled stages and a still/text equivalent. These sketches are prompts for creative consultation, not final treatments or evidence of mechanics.

## Bounce: two directions

A. Contact accent at actual impact; folded-paper spring below the affected duck opens as it rises above a later ground-level duck, then closes on a visible landing. The spring must not resemble an injury badge or imply the first contact was avoided.

B. Actual contact first; an offset paper arc traces the rising duck's path, with a separated water shadow beneath it. The arc ends on landing. At race size compare whether the arc remains distinct from Flap Ahead and track markings.

## Crowd acceleration: two directions

A. Nearby ducks establish the crowd; a short layered wake fans out behind the activated duck, then narrows and fades. Distinguish from Draft's leader-to-follower relationship.

B. The activated duck briefly compresses and extends its paper body while short directional water folds appear beside it, then returns to normal. Ensure motion does not suggest a contact or guaranteed passing.

## Combination and explanation contexts

Show a crowd opportunity and an independently authored surge, followed by a real collision and a separately authored Bounce activation. Show both overlap and a separated timing; label actual source and event stages. Include a no-activation collision control. Land without silently resolving the open physical overlap rule: the fixture authors a clear landing space.

Duck-Dex inspection: a substantially enlarged rig and local effect with a deliberately invoked replay, a useful still, and conditional wording such as “After a collision, this duck may bounce above other ducks for a short time.” Racer selection: same enlarged identity in a choice-context mock with a replay and short optional explanation. These are examples, not mounted UI or promises of final rules.

## Remaining candidate stills

Wide Flap: spread wings, outward directional folds and named affected neighbors; no impact burst. Dive: partially submerged duck and persistent underwater indicator. Flap Ahead: takeoff, aerial pass, landing, with flight clearly distinct from Bounce. Draft: marked follower, leader and directional wake; leader loss ends it. Straight Shooter: real impact then restored heading. Quick Recovery: real impact then continuous return toward lane. Steady Nerves: self wobble and restrained correction. Wake Break: source wake behind and explicit trailing participant. Guard: actual contact and provisional response marker separated from injury. Burst: speed-proxy text/control, no invented takeoff.

Review in the same six-duck arrangement at race size and at 390px, both facing directions, turn and normal/drama light. Static stills retain event and participant labels. The actual visual direction awaits consultant input and owner review.

## Current direction delta — 2026-11-03

Owner direction relayed by QP Orch supersedes the earlier mandatory post-impact trigger: Bounce may activate before a predicted imminent collision, with visible preparation and possibly changed temporary risk tolerance while active. Exact threat detection, timing, probability, air/landing and risk semantics remain Plan55 questions. Illustrate preparation → possible automatic lift → airborne → landing, without reflex input or a claim that a collision was certainly prevented. A contact cue appears only when a separately authored actual contact occurs; include a threat/no-activation/contact control. Compare with Flap Ahead using the context and preparation cue, not a newly invented rule.
