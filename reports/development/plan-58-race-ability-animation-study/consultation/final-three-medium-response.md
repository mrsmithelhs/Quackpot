# Consultation: Straight Shooter, Steady Nerves and Wake Break

## What the stills show

- **Quick Recovery:** a cream curved lane-return swoosh with a dark chevron at the tail, and a persistent injury band on A.
- **Draft:** three pale, straight, white-outlined streaks between the two ducks.
- **Crowd:** light-blue capsule currents plus a teal dome bow wave.
- **Bounce:** a brown pad with three red up-arrows.
- **Small size:** the ducks are tiny and the effects become loops, so only one bold shape survives.

To keep each ability distinct, each direction below uses one shape family that the accepted cues don't. I avoid cream sweeps, straight pale streaks, light-blue capsules and domes, and red arrows on brown. Color is never the only signal. Each cue also has a unique silhouette, a duck pose change and a caption line.

**Shared rules**
- Define every shape relative to heading. Mirror the whole duck and its effects together, including bill, hat and injury band. Labels stay upright and unmirrored.
- Do not draw the ability on top of the contact marker. The contact or injury marker is authored separately and stays visible and unchanged. The ability layer only starts after it.
- Static and reduced-motion versions use a 3-panel strip (before / event / end). Each panel gets a caption in the existing style, for example "A: bumped by B (separate event)" and then "A: Straight Shooter response (candidate)".

## 1. Straight Shooter

**Actual event:** B bumps A. The overlap and contact puff come from the contact system. The injury band, if any, stays. The ability never erases or shrinks the bump.

### A: "Heading corridor" (surroundings fixed to the heading)
- **Duck:** The bump knocks A's bill off-line. A drops into a head-down aerodynamic pose, then swings its bill back toward the original heading. Its feet kick and its tail feathers trail straight behind it.
- **Surround:** Two long dark-navy dashed rails lie on the water ahead of A along its pre-bump heading. They are anchored to the water, not to the duck. The far rail sits behind the duck and the near rail in front.
- **Motion:**
  - Start: the rails snap in from the bill outward.
  - Active: A's bill sweeps into alignment between the rails.
  - End: the rails break into dashes and fade, and A goes back to the normal pose.
- **Why it differs from Quick Recovery:** The duck pivots in place and does not slide sideways, so there is no lateral return.

### B: "Keel and counter-kick" (body-driven)
- **Duck:** A pushes against the knock. Its feet kick on the side opposite the bump and its chest and neck brace. A dark translucent keel-fin appears under the hull on the rear depth, and a small water wedge shows at the stern.
- **Surround:** A heading-tick fan (three short ticks) sits at the bill. It starts splayed and collapses to one bold tick.
- **Motion:**
  - Start: the fan splays with the knock.
  - Active: the keel bites in and the body yaws back.
  - End: the keel retracts and the fan clears.
- **Why it differs from Quick Recovery:** The cue is a vertical fin and a tick, not a sweeping curve.

**No-activation control:** Use the identical contact frames, injury marker and B position. A is knocked and has no rails or keel. Show a faint ghost of A's post-bump path and label it "illustrative". Do not draw a worse outcome, because effectiveness is open.

**At actual size:**
- A: keep only the near rail and the head-down pose.
- B: keep only the keel fin plus the single tick.

**Possible confusion:**
- A reads like Quick Recovery's lane line unless the rails are clearly straight and ahead of the duck.
- B's fan can read as a UI icon if it gets large.
- Neither should look like Bounce's pad or arrows.

## 2. Steady Nerves

**Actual event:** An independently authored self-generated wobble, a side-to-side rocking of the hull and head. There is no collision, no protection and no healing.

### A: "Settling rings" (a waterline gauge)
- **Duck:** The rocking amplitude visibly shrinks across the beats. The head and bill steady first, eyes calm and forward. The feet paddle in an even rhythm.
- **Surround:** Two elliptical rings sit at the waterline, one behind and one in front of the hull. They begin offset, jittery and dashed, and tighten into a steady solid ring as the wobble moderates.
- **Motion:**
  - Start: rings flutter with the wobble.
  - Active: they converge toward the hull.
  - End: they hold briefly, then relax into the normal shadow.

### B: "Plumb line and counterweight" (a world-vertical reference)
- **Duck:** The wing and tail swing opposite the lean as a counterweight. The swing shrinks as the wobble calms.
- **Surround:** A thick vertical dashed plumb line is fixed to the world and passes through the body. Early on the hull visibly tilts away from it, and later it aligns. Short water crescents lap the sides alternately and shrink.
- **Motion:**
  - Start: the tilt is clear against the line.
  - Active: the wing counterbalances.
  - End: the line fades.

**Same-wobble control:** Two parallel strips use the same authored wobble track and the same start frames.
- Strip 1: no activation. The wobble continues as authored.
- Strip 2: activation. The cue appears and the moderated rocking follows.
- In stills, overlay a faint ghost outline of the no-activation tilt behind the activated duck. Label both strips "same wobble".

**At actual size:**
- A: a single ring that tightens, plus body rocking.
- B: just the vertical line and the tilt.

**Possible confusion:**
- Rings could resemble Wake Break ripples. Keep them hull-anchored, closed and symmetric. Wake Break ripples sit off the hull at the stern.
- The plumb line could be mistaken for a tether. Keep it thin, vertical and ending at the duck.

## 3. Wake Break

**Actual event:** Source A is ahead and follower B is genuinely close behind. A disturbs its own wake and B's acceleration is proposed to be reduced. Any visible B response, such as harder paddling or lag, is a separate authored track. A does not push, injure or force B.

### A: "Foam churn" (a patch of turbulence)
- **Duck:** A paddles with a stirring shimmy, a tail flick and a wagging stern.
- **Surround:** A cluster of overlapping rounded foam lobes with dark-teal outlines and scalloped edges sits in the water behind A's stern. It is anchored to the water and drifts slowly. A visible gap separates it from B, so it neither touches nor connects to B. The foam sits on the back depth, with small froth in front of the near waterline.
- **Motion:**
  - Start: the lobes pop up in sequence at A's stern.
  - Active: they churn.
  - End (release): the lobes scatter into widening rings and fade, and A's tail returns to a normal thin wake.

### B: "Counter-eddies" (spiral swirls)
- **Duck:** A leans into a harder kick, with a quick tail waggle at the start and a settle at the end.
- **Surround:** Two small counter-rotating spiral eddies (no arrowheads) sit left and right of the wake line, one on the far side and one on the near side. They are dark blue-teal with foam dots, and the wake stream lines bend around them.
- **Motion:**
  - Start: the spirals coil open.
  - Active: they turn slowly.
  - End: they unwind into ring ripples and dissolve.

**Identifying B:** Use the existing label pill and put the same closeness in the framing. No line links A to B.

**No-activation control:** The same two ducks at the same distance. A has a plain narrow wake and no foam or eddies, and B's label is still present.

**At actual size:**
- A: a single foam cluster with a tail flick.
- B: one spiral, with the two-lobe version reserved for the enlarged view.

**Possible confusion:**
- **Draft:** the geometry is nearly identical, because the leader is ahead and the follower behind. Draft uses straight, pale, continuous streaks that reach the follower. Wake Break uses curled, broken, dispersed shapes with a gap and no streak reaching the follower.
- **Crowd acceleration:** that cue uses light-blue capsules flowing head to tail around the duck. Wake Break stays off the duck, at the stern.
- Do not draw a projectile, a backward tail arrowhead or a tether.

## Combined example (independent events only)

Duck A is yellow and carries the injury band. B is close behind.

3. **Beat 3:** D bumps A from the side, which is a separately authored contact. The contact puff and injury band stay. The Straight Shooter rails run ahead.
