## Numerical, serialization and replay decision

Current v1 requires exact replay of a serialized race; the recorded R01 witness matches Chromium's own saved result but differs in Node's floating metadata/digest. The v2 kernel is native-float experimental and its discrete substeps changed contact counts in the small half-step screen. Neither matching seed nor a JSON round trip establishes cross-runtime semantic agreement. Proposed options:

1. **Versioned deterministic decision/evolution contract (recommended before mounted cross-runtime replay):** explicitly define every decision-affecting representation, arithmetic/rounding and overflow behavior, contact/crossing/tie rules, and canonical serialization. Validate identical discrete events/places/injuries, not merely rounded display fields. Establish bounds and Node/Chromium witnesses around thresholds, tangencies, ties and negative/tamper cases. A fixed-point or controlled-rounded implementation requires its own feasibility and parity review; no specific scheme is selected here.
2. **Explicit supported-runtime-scoped v2 development contract:** preserve the experimental arithmetic, identify the runtime and reject unsupported replay without erasing the save; test same-runtime restoration and honestly disclose that it cannot solve cross-runtime validation. This may permit a bounded local preview if root accepts the narrower evidence and supported-environment limitation, but cannot be represented as a portable static-game replay contract.
3. **Digest-only quantization or global epsilon:** not recommended: it may conceal a serialized difference without preventing a branch, injury, contact or finish-order difference. It also risks silently weakening tamper detection. No such change is authorized.

Recommend root decide v2 numeric/serialization policy and v1 support before mounting. Version dispatch must never reinterpret an old v1 race under v2. Preserve supported v1 replay with its historical policy and a defined supported runtime, or identify it as unsupported development data and preserve it for explicit user choice; no silent reset or fabricated migration. Schema 6 is a candidate to distinguish the changed state and result contract, not an adopted requirement. Current five-slot development uses schema 5; storage's `CURRENT_SCHEMA_VERSION=4` is a legacy default with explicit schema-5 handling, so no single constant is the whole policy. Require corruption/unsupported distinction, same-runtime restore, duplicate callback, v1/v2 dispatch, negative seed/event/result/meaningful numeric tamper controls, and cross-runtime boundary witnesses appropriate to the selected contract. Do not weaken historical v1 validation or broaden global payload ceilings. Measure maximum serialized v2 envelopes, introduce only a bounded reviewed version-specific limit if needed, and preserve failure behavior.
