# Plan 60 creative consultation

Give a concise, skeptical creative critique of a fictional automatic duck-racing game's ability family and later growth. Work only from this brief; do not access other files or tools. Return text only. Identify yourself as reported by your runtime if available; do not guess. Do not treat your ideas as decisions or evidence.

The six-duck race uses seeded automatic choices, no reflex input, real individual crossings, a roughly 20-second winner target, an oval, lane recentering, weaving and real contact. Players choose a steady or sprint stance before racing. The game is a nonspending-accessible educational satire about gacha; a player may cherish a common duck, rotate ducks, lose repeatedly, or have only one duck.

Selected first point: each completed entered-duck race earns 2 progress plus 1 for top three; the first permanent, bankable point costs 4, preserves overflow, and is assigned without a life slot to Bounce or independently triggered crowd acceleration. Bounce protects its duck from injury throughout activation including landing; an occupied landing is real contact and the other duck has independent injury risk. Injury reduces race rating by 10% even during that race; ducks remain selectable, recover after two actual day changes, and each completed race entered already injured independently has a disclosed 25% chance to extend recovery a day. No paid healing or free-path lockout. Later costs and rarity bonuses are unsettled. Contact, momentary recovery, persistent injury, activation, and animation are distinct. There is no evidence that tiny previous samples established equal ability strength.

Nine concepts and selected animation references, not settled mechanics: Guard B follows real contact with an optional response; Quick Recovery A shortens return from contact deflection without healing; Dive submerges with a locator and later resurfaces, candidate slower and contact-immune; Wide Flap sweeps wings and may move a nearby duck, but the flap itself is not an injuring contact; Flap Ahead flies briefly and lands, candidate passing advantage and landing slowdown; Draft follows a named leader in its wake and ends when that relationship fails; Straight Shooter responds to contact with heading rails; Steady Nerves moderates a self-generated wobble rather than contact; Wake Break creates stern eddies affecting a real follower without pushing or injury. Bounce uses a rebound pad/flap; crowd acceleration uses a bow wave and head-to-tail airflow. All graphics are study references, not proof of physics. Burst is deferred.

For contact/protection/recovery, movement/positioning, and leader/follower/wake families: propose competing distinctive player purposes, automatic opportunities, likely costs, concise player copy, meaningful failure cases, interactions and potential mergers or deferrals. Explicitly compare a healthy favorite on steady versus sprint, and distinguish real injury prevention from avoiding a contact. Explain air/submerged/landing and individual-finish edge cases. Avoid recommending numbers as if established. Consider upgrades versus a second ability, loadout/caps, and rarity starting advantages; show how a common favorite, rotating roster, one-duck losing route and nonspender remain meaningful. Identify feedback loops and choices that look distinct visually but may feel redundant. Recommend a small next group and reasons against it; do not force all nine to survive. Keep answers compact and distinguish creative hypotheses from testable observations.
