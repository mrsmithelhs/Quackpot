# Later progression: illustrative traces for discussion

The owner wants later points to offer improvement **and** another ability, and favors exploring higher eventual potential for rarer ducks. No later threshold, rarity mapping, number of active slots, upgrade levels or increase in duration is selected. This draft supplies accounting examples, not a simulation of available game days, purchase needs, realistic race frequency or balance.

Only the selected first threshold is 4 cumulative progress. For comparison, assume a second point at **8** cumulative progress in alternative A or **12** in alternative B; both preserve overflow. These are rival examples, not repeated prices and not selections. Each completed race awards its entered duck 2 plus 1 for top three. `P` below is cumulative progress; `A/B` is total points earned under each illustrative alternative, whether banked or spent. Each trace begins at zero. No additional points beyond the second are calculated.

| Scenario | Race-by-race progress | A/B points after each race | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Favorite loses six completed races | 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 | 0/0, 1/1, 1/1, 2/1, 2/1, 2/2 | Losses still reach first point on race 2. Later choice arrives on race 4 under A, race 6 under B. |
| Favorite places top three six times | 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 | 0/0, 1/1, 2/1, 2/2, 2/2, 2/2 | First point still arrives on race 2; retained bonuses make the illustrative second point arrive earlier. |
| Three ducks rotate, each places top three on first entry and loses second | D1: 3 then 5; D2: 3 then 5; D3: 3 then 5 | Each duck 0/0 then 1/1 after its second entry | Each reaches a first point after six total races; none reaches the illustrative second. Rotation spreads development. |
| Four losses, first point banked until after race 4 | 2, 4, 6, 8 | 0/0, 1/1, 1/1, 2/1 | At race 2 one point is bankable. After race 4 A has two earned, B has one; assigning the first afterward does not consume a life slot. |
| Loss, unresolved attempt, loss | 2, 2, 4 | 0/0, 0/0, 1/1 | The unresolved attempt earns no completed-race progress. Valid retained injury may still exist separately. |
| Common favorite versus rare, both six losses | Both 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 | Both follow first row | Common obtains the same progress in this illustrative comparison; a rare's hypothetical higher cap need not provide an immediate extra point. Exact rarity grant and access remain open. |

For these examples, unless the row explicitly assigns a point, **assigned is zero** and banked equals the A/B earned sequence shown. In the delayed-assignment row, after the first point is assigned following race 4, A has one assigned and one banked; B has one assigned and zero banked. In the rotation row, each duck independently has one banked after its second entry. The unresolved attempt adds nothing and cannot redeem a new point. The examples stop after two earned points: later thresholds are unknown and no progress is discarded. Before a second point is earned, the hypothetical new-ability versus upgrade decision remains unavailable, even if a capacity ceiling would eventually allow it. A rare's hypothetical extra capacity or tier is a ceiling, not a free point or an already-active ability.

For the second point, compare learning a new ability against improving the first. For example, with an **illustrative** 0.50-second Guard activation, an upgrade to 0.65 seconds could protect a longer interval but still miss a late collision; learning Quick Recovery instead could provide a separate response after an actual collision while leaving Guard's original window. Neither effect guarantees a result. The details and actual useful increments need tests and owner review; a longer animation alone is not evidence of benefit.

Consider two separate hypothetical rarity axes: rare eventual active capacity 3 versus common 2; or rare maximum improvement tier 3 versus common 2. Those numbers are illustrations, and the two axes must not silently stack. The first could give a rare duck more simultaneous tools but make six-duck events harder to read; the second could make one tool stronger or longer-lived while keeping fewer concurrent effects. A lower common ceiling creates an enduring difference, so its early choices, reachable combinations and role alongside better ducks need particular scrutiny. An equal-ceiling/head-start alternative remains a useful comparison despite the owner's stated preference. One-duck viability and nonspending access must be checked against an actual future game route; this arithmetic cannot establish either.
