# Authored semantic phase: results and review disposition

The preregistered 36 named examples and six illustrative progression checks ran once, followed by a bounded corrected rerun after the read-only review below. Both runs passed their own assertions; **passing a named case does not mean every observation in its prose was modeled or checked**. This is an event-ordering study, not a race resolver, physical trajectory, injury-rate study, balance test or production binding. Source pins before execution are in `semantic-pins-before-execution.md`; the fixture table's statement that no executable existed describes its pre-execution state.

What was actually checked includes a predictive Guard activation before contact, an injury draw reduced only during its active window, independent other-participant injury, failed activation, window end/cooldown, finish-first synthetic ordering, selected layer separation, and assigned/banked illustrative progression totals. Under the explicitly authored .10/.20 risk and .5 Guard multiplier, the shared fixed draws produced the expected examples; no empirical probability or actual collision rate was estimated. Exact same-time order, crossing eligibility, risk, duration and cooldown remain proposals. No effect heals a prior injury in these examples. The 8/12 cumulative second thresholds and capacity/tier 2/3 examples are illustrations; no recurring redemption cost is selected. Guard's owner-selected pre-contact brace does not create or prove a collision.

**Coverage limits:** Dive, flight, flap, weave and Quick Recovery are sparse labels and do not implement a full finite ability lifecycle, realistic velocity, steering, genuine crossing, speed cap or injury-rating composition. A supplied crossing witness is accepted as an input, not proven physically. S1–S3's heading/obstacle effects are authored labels; actual path distinction and useful behavior are unobserved. Quick Recovery's .7-second value is supplied, not a modeled controller. F1 does not demonstrate landing slowdown, N2 does not measure the specified 16-degree contact deflection, X1 tests supplied contact while Bounce is active without a landing simulation, and X2 records a crowd opportunity, not a crowd activation or combined speed. Wake changes no follower acceleration in this model. Candidate outcomes not modeled must not be inferred from green case names. Full-air/submerged lifecycle, multiple-contact physiology, and any effectiveness/placement comparisons need a separately reviewed intervention seam or future production integration evidence.
