## Proposed small model and controls

Represent each duck as an identity, active ability and state (ground/air/submerged), injury flag, and an explicit finite ability window. Model a 0.5-second illustrative active window and 1-second illustrative cooldown, measured from activation and active-end respectively; these are only test settings, not candidate final tuning. Intervals are half-open: active `[start,end)`, cooldown `[end,cooldownEnd)`, then eligible. For this authored model only, a valid Guard prediction must occur at an earlier timestamp than the contact it protects; events at the same timestamp are ordered finish, previously scheduled expiration, contact and injury, then new activation. This is a conservative test convention, not an adopted production tie rule. A finished participant is ineligible for future ability effects or new contact; active effects end on its own genuine crossing. An actual finish at exact ability-end is a crossing, not an extension of ability time. Pure authored events and known inputs do not model trajectory, speed cap behavior, how realistic a trigger is, or hidden solver interactions.

For synthetic injury examples only, use shared explicit uniform draws rather than sampling or arbitrary paired seeds. Base per-contact risk illustrative 0.10 on steady and 0.20 on sprint, Guard multiplier 0.5 while active; paired control and candidate use identical duck-specific draws. Healthy duck A draw 0.075, B draw 0.15 in the standard steady case: A injured without Guard and not injured with Guard, B unaffected in both; at draw 0.025 A injured in both, at 0.15 neither. Sprint cases use the same draws and higher illustrative risk to exercise ordering, not to claim sprint actually produces more contacts. Already-injured entry has no new injury stack in this demonstration; it keeps injury/rating penalty, and this is not a calculation of its eventual recovery extension. Injury and draw consequences belong to the modeled actual contact only. Bounce's existing full protection is a separate active-window control, including occupied landing and independent other-duck risk.

## Proposed cases and discriminating observations

At most 36 cases, explicitly specified before running; the following names reserve 30 and describe intended discriminators. Each case will be paired against no-ability with the same authored event, states and draws, and preserve the exact observed event trace; add no extra cases opportunistically after results. Numerical equivalence here is exact equality of serialized semantic outputs, excluding labels; it says nothing about real race strength.

1. G1–G4: healthy steady real contact with draw 0.075, 0.025 and 0.15, plus already injured; test Guard vs control and independent other participant.
2. G5–G7: risky stance shared draws; contact at end boundary; contact during cooldown. The authored earlier-prediction convention above governs expected activation window and draw order; no retroactive removal of a recorded injury.
3. Q1–Q3: real contact while uninjured, real contact with already injured duck, and no contact; Quick Recovery must preserve injury, contact and speed penalty; scripted lane-recovery milestones may be compared only as model inputs/outputs, not real steering.
4. S1–S3: identical contact with heading correction versus Quick Recovery; second obstacle straight ahead and off-line; retain the same initial impulse/injury, and identify whether authored path assumptions—not solver evidence—drive a difference.
5. D1–D3: submerged passage, occupied resurfacing, individual finish while submerged; no grounded contact while submerged, explicit real contact on occupied resurfacing, and honest crossing under a stated authored crossing eligibility rule (not selected production policy).
6. F1–F3: grounded traffic passage while airborne, air-to-air contact, occupied landing; actual contact and independent injury only where layers overlap, and landing slowdown modeled without inheriting Bounce protection.
7. W1–W2: no eligible target versus named nearby target; a flap is not a contact, but a separate subsequent contact remains real and injury eligible.
8. DR1–DR3: eligible leader, no leader, leader finishes during active window; named relationship terminates on finish; no claim about gain in real placement or speed.
9. WB1–WB2: eligible follower and follower leaving or finishing; distinguish named source and target, no impulse/injury created by the wake.
10. N1–N2: self-weave and contact-generated deflection; only the former receives candidate wobble reduction, and injury/contact stay unchanged.
11. X1–X2: Bounce occupied landing with both participants' independent draws and crowd plus Draft opportunities; preserve full Bounce protection and independent crowd activation; simultaneous speed-combination policy remains unresolved and is not chosen through this fixture.

The case count is 30. Case-specific literal event times, opportunity flags, participant states, draw values, expected trace and comparison criteria must be frozen in an executable-free fixture table before root clears any run. If these cannot be specified without inventing an owner policy, keep that case unresolved rather than silently choose it.

## Progression trace protocol

Construct hand-checkable examples with actual known first threshold 4 and no claim of known later thresholds. Compare two illustrative **alternative** second-point thresholds, 8 total accumulated progress or 12 total accumulated progress, not repeated-point prices; overflow remains. Each event is +2 completed entered-duck races, +1 on top-three; point conversion after the first is illustrative conditional on the chosen curve, not selected. Six traces: one duck six losses; one duck six top-three; three ducks rotated twice each with mixed placements; one duck four races with banked first point then assignment; one duck interrupted by an unresolved race; and a common favorite versus an otherwise comparable rare with illustrative capacity 2 vs 3 abilities OR upgrade cap 2 vs 3, not both simultaneously. No rarity starting grant, gems, paid recovery, shared progress or lifetime promise is assumed. Each trace must show ledger after each race, available/banked points, what a hypothetical added point changes, and what remains inaccessible in the example. Compare adding an ability with extending a temporary active window only under explicit illustrative activation durations and caps; do not assert either creates a fair or realistic runtime experience.
