## Observations on the brief

- **The first point lands after two completed entered-duck races regardless of placement.** Two races at 2 progress each already reach 4. The top-three bonus only changes overflow and later pacing, so it won't feel like a reward at the first point. [T: check this against the real cost table.]
- **Bounce is more valuable to some players than others.** A healthy favorite on steady is rarely at risk, so Bounce looks like a premium insurance policy for them. It is more valuable to a one-duck player who must keep racing while injured. [H]
- **Crowd acceleration looks like a rubber band in the first slot.** It is a catch-up effect, so it must not read as "losing is the strategy." Its bow-wave and airflow graphics also imply physics the game doesn't simulate. [H]

## Family 1: contact / protection / recovery

Four distinct concepts hide inside the concepts listed:

1. **Prevent injury (Bounce).** Contact still happens, but the injury roll doesn't.
2. **Reduce the momentary consequence.** Guard B, Quick Recovery and Straight Shooter all do this in some form.
3. **Avoid contact altogether.** Dive does this by being absent from the pool.
4. **Create room for yourself.** Wide Flap.

**Competing purposes:**
- **Bounce:** insurance for the days after the race. Its failure case is that it spends its activation on a harmless bump and is unavailable when it matters. Its cost is a limited activation window.
- **Guard B** (optional response after real contact): purpose is "contact is a decision." Failure case: the response fires and still loses a lane or place. Cost is the response itself, such as lost speed.
- **Quick Recovery A and Straight Shooter:** these overlap heavily, since both say "I come back from contact faster or straighter." **Merge or defer** them into one "after-contact" slot. If kept, name Quick Recovery something other than "recovery," because players will conflate it with the multi-day injury recovery. Copy should say: "Gets back on course sooner. Does not heal."

**Real injury prevention vs. avoiding contact:**
- **Bounce** prevents injury but not contact. A bounced duck can still be pushed, and its landing on an occupied spot is real contact, with independent injury risk for the other duck.
- **Dive** avoids contact while submerged. That is not the same as prevention, because the resurfacing is still exposed.
- **Quick Recovery, Straight Shooter and Guard B** do not prevent injury. Copy must never imply they do.

**Edge cases:**
- **Landing:** a Bounce landing on an occupied space is real contact. The Bounce duck is protected and the other duck rolls independently. The result should be visible in the replay or recap, or it will feel unfair.
- **Dive and the finish:** I'd force an auto-resurface before the finish window, or a submerged crossing becomes a cheat. [H]
- **Individual finish while airborne or submerged:** decide which crossing rule applies, e.g. the body at the line at the surface. Ambiguity here will generate disputes.

## Family 2: movement / positioning

- **Flap Ahead:** purpose is a visible hop with a trade. Passing advantage is balanced by the landing slowdown and the occupied-landing contact risk.
  - **Failure case:** landing in traffic, or landing too early and gaining nothing.
  - **Cost:** the slowdown. It is the most legible of the movement ideas.
- **Dive:** purpose is a stealth route. It is candidate-slower and contact-immune, which overlaps Bounce's purpose (avoiding contact consequences). It has the largest edge-case surface: the locator, resurfacing, finish crossing, and anything landing on the resurfacing point. **Defer.**
- **Wide Flap:** purpose is to clear space. Its main risk is other ducks being moved. Even if the flap itself is not an injuring contact, a moved duck can end up in real contact, and players will blame the flapper. **Defer.**
- **Steady Nerves:** purpose is to moderate self-generated wobble. It is the only one that interacts directly with the player's one input, the stance choice. **Interaction hypothesis:** if sprint increases wobble, Steady Nerves is a sprint enabler; if it doesn't, the ability has no job.
  - It is also the least visible. [T: can a viewer tell a Steady Nerves duck from a plain one without a label?]

## Family 3: leader / follower / wake

- **Draft:** purpose is to be a good follower. Its failure case is that the leader finishes or changes lane and the relationship ends. It is a natural underdog ability and a strong visual story, because the named leader is the trade.
  - **Cost:** it is weaker when your duck is leading.
- **Wake Break:** purpose is to be a good leader, hurting a real follower without pushing or injuring. It is the mirror of Draft.
  - **Problem:** its benefit is invisible unless the follower is attributable. It is a tax on others.
  - **Defer** until Draft exists, then compare.
- **Crowd acceleration** is also a catch-up effect and belongs conceptually here, since it benefits trailing ducks. It should not stack with Draft as a second follower bonus.

**Edge cases:**
- **Individual finish:** Draft ends when the leader finishes, and the follower is still racing. Wake Break's target is gone once the leader finishes.
- **Air and submerged:** a Dive duck presumably leaves the wake relationship. A Flap Ahead duck in the air loses the draft, so landing re-forms it only if a new leader is named.

## Upgrades vs. a second ability; loadout and rarity

- **Upgrades** are number creep and easy to misread. **A second ability** creates combinations among six ducks, which is hard to read in 20 seconds.
- **Hypothesis:** later points should unlock sidegrades, meaning variants rather than strength, with a second slot much later. Since costs are unsettled, hold caps loosely.
- **Rarity** should be a **head start** (e.g. banked progress or an earlier unlock), not a higher ceiling. Otherwise the satire collapses into "pay for stronger ducks." Disclose the head start in copy. [T: compare a common duck after N races with a rare at race 0.]

**Player routes:**
- **Cherished common:** it earns points at the same rate and keeps its identity. A rare's head start must not make the common feel obsolete.
- **Rotating roster:** progress per duck is slow, so rotation sacrifices depth. Rotating may need some shared progress, or it feels punished. Unsettled.
- **One-duck loser:** earns 2 per race, so it still gets a point every two races. But a one-duck player who can't rest the duck hits the injury-extension loop. Bounce is the most valuable single thing for them, so that gap should be acknowledged.
- **Nonspender:** has no paid healing and no free-path lockout. That is good. Check that rarity doesn't become the only path to second slots.

## Feedback loops

- **Injury → -10% rating → worse placements → fewer bonuses → slower progress.** The bonus is small, but combined with the 25% extension it may trap a one-duck player. [T]
- **Crowd acceleration** lifts trailing ducks, which compresses the field. That can erase the value of choosing a stance.
- **Draft and Wake Break** could create a dependency where the leader is always the favorite.
