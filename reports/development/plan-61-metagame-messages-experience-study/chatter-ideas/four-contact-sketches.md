# Four contact sketches — owner review draft

Owner selected both Copy Counter and Lantern Laundry as candidates, and wants both an acquaintance and a family contact developed. Names and specifics below are proposals, not adopted canon. Historical texts are fixed earlier exchanges, initially read; optional later chatter is informational and need not arrive in any particular run. No sample creates a hidden reply, obligation, reward, transaction or relationship penalty. A follow-up is eligible only after its first message, and may never appear; the actual selection, spacing and replay mechanism is future work.

## The Copy Counter — cheerful precision, slightly crooked equipment

A small neighborhood print shop with a friendly shared business number. Its messages are concise and helpful first; the humor comes from the staff noticing that printers seem to have their own opinions, not from a sentient machine. The player is someone who has used the shop before, but the examples don't establish a job, specific order, money spent or a skill. Keep the business name; a named employee is optional, not required.

**Earlier exchange**

> Copy Counter: The sign came out a little darker than the preview. Want to see it before we finish?
>
> You: That looks good to me.
>
> Copy Counter: Great. We'll leave the tiny dinosaur in the corner, then.

The historical exchange implies a print interaction and a sign; if that feels too specific, use the alternate: “Your file came through clearly.” / “Good, thank you.” / “The printer and I are both relieved.” Neither establishes a real pending order at game start.

**Possible later single:** “The copier has produced one perfect blank page. We are framing it.”

**Possible pair:** “The window poster says GRAND OPE ING. Very exclusive opening.” → Sometimes later: “Found the N on the cutter. Opening restored.”

**Voice boundary:** It is a neighborhood business, not a marketing drip campaign. Avoid messages that claim an order is waiting, a payment is due or a task exists when no underlying system supports that claim. Print-shop weirdness should vary, or the shop becomes a one-joke machine.

## Lantern Laundry — matter-of-fact notices with an observant human behind them

A neighborhood laundry's shared number. The sender sounds practical and unhurried; the oddity usually belongs to a found object or a sign, followed by a small dry observation. This can be a different kind of commerce from the Copy Counter: fewer punchlines, more understated care. A named attendant is optional.

**Earlier exchange**

> Lantern Laundry: Your umbrella was left by the folding table.
>
> You: I'll pick it up next time.
>
> Lantern Laundry: It made it home yesterday, just in time for the sun.

This is a completed past interaction, not an item owed at game start. If it implies too much, use: “The machine nearest the window is working again.” / “Good to know.”

**Possible later single:** “Lost-and-found update: one glove has been given a name tag. The glove has not commented.”

**Possible pair:** “Someone put a tiny paper crown on the lost-and-found sock.” → Sometimes later: “The sock went home. The crown has joined the spare buttons.”

**Voice boundary:** No invented laundry fee, store credit, pickup deadline, errand or gameplay inventory. The glove joke describes a person projecting onto an object; it does not imply objects actually speak. Avoid repeating the crown motif if Harbor Court uses it in the same run.

## Acquaintance — working name Kit; the person who notices the wrong detail

**Proposed relationship:** a familiar person from the neighborhood who has crossed paths with the player often enough to trade little observations, but is not automatically a close friend, coworker, romantic interest or social-stat contact. “Kit” is only a candidate display name; their pronouns, job and biography are not selected. Their messages are compact, mildly sideways and comfortable with no immediate answer. This is deliberately a different relationship from Ren's introduced neighbor/maker role.

**Earlier exchange**

> Kit: The sign at the corner says OPEN, but only the O is lit.
>
> You: Maybe they're only a little open.
>
> Kit: Respectable boundary.

**Possible later single:** “The wind chimes on this block sound like an elevator thinking.”

**Possible pair:** “A folding chair has been sitting in the same spot all afternoon, facing the wall.” → Sometimes later: “It was holding the door open. Excellent work, chair.”

**Alternative relationship:** an old classmate or former coworker could supply a different voice, but either choice adds more backstory than this neighborhood acquaintance. Do not select one without the owner. No hidden strain or penalty follows silence.

## Family contact — proposed Mom, with a separate optional alternative

Quackpot already has an existing Call Mom activity and a visible reminder; a message thread from **Mom** could make use of an established relationship rather than inventing an additional relative. This is a proposal for *message voice only*: the existing call's availability, health effect, scheduling and replay are not changed, and receiving or ignoring an informational text must not start a call or grant health. Current scheduler/production seams must be checked again during integration.

**Voice:** warm, practical and lightly funny; the character is allowed to be a whole person with projects and interests beyond checking on the player. Avoid guilt, repeated “are you okay?” prompts, presumed money trouble or treating the player like a child.

**Earlier exchange**

> Mom: I finally got the kitchen timer to stop ringing.
>
> You: What was wrong with it?
>
> Mom: Nothing. I kept setting it again while trying to turn it off.

**Possible later single:** “The kitchen timer has been promoted to referee. It takes the job very seriously.”

**Possible pair:** “A very important family vote is happening about a bowl.” → Sometimes later: “The bowl won. It's staying on the table.” This implies family members and a shared setting without naming new relatives; even that implication is for owner review.

**Alternative:** a different family contact, with relationship/name defined by the owner, could have a more teasing or quieter tone. This would create a new relationship rather than reuse Mom; do not infer an aunt, cousin, sibling, name or pronouns automatically.

## Design comparisons and questions

- **History vs later chatter:** each earlier exchange reads as complete; later singles/pairs can be selected across runs. Do not deliver a follow-up before its parent or treat an unanswered informational text as a broken promise.
- **Curiosity:** the copier's blank page is a single, while the missing N is a pair. The chair can remain an odd image in one run and get a mundane payoff in another. These are authored options, not a promise that every player receives a payoff.
- **Saturation:** messages should not all arrive in a batch or establish a fixed cadence here. Quackpot may be more persistent than these voices by owner's direction, subject to a separate state-grounded design.
- **Continuity:** the current game already has Call Mom; decide whether Mom is the desired family contact before making the connection canon. No social rule, scheduling or production change follows from a writing preference.

Owner review: Does Kit as a light neighborhood acquaintance fit, and should the family thread be Mom or a different relative? Are the Copy Counter's brighter humor and Lantern Laundry's quieter tone sufficiently distinct? All example names and exchanges remain editable.
