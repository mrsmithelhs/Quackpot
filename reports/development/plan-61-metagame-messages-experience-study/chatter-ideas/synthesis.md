# Messages chatter — candidate cast and experience direction

This is a report-local addendum for owner selection and a later integration packet. The owner's new direction is a more lived-in inbox: some threads exist at game start, some established contacts such as Marisol also have real gameplay roles, and some later messages are informational texture. Nondecision chatter should vary quasi-randomly across runs, spark small curiosities that only sometimes receive a later payoff, and reveal quirky community, work and family details without becoming spam. Fictional Quackpot may be notably more persistent, including its Day 1 invitation, real event promotions and prompts when the app has not been opened that day. No numeric cadence, probabilities, new story canon, event, reward, schedule or production mechanism is selected by this addendum.

## Suggested first-wave mix

Start with **five or six distinct voices**, not every candidate below: Marisol; the existing property office; a neighborhood business; a community/public channel; one acquaintance or family contact whose relationship the owner defines; and Quackpot. Add Ren only when the established introduction permits. This is a proposed content mix, not a cap on later senders. Let some contacts have a brief read-only history, some send an occasional new message, and some do both. A contact with an actual gameplay role can also have ordinary nondecision messages, but the game must not confuse the two.

Avoid those as empty flavor unless the underlying world actually supports them.

## What makes the inbox feel alive without becoming a task list

- Let a single message land with a little texture even if nothing follows. Some pairs can pay off in a second message; the first must make sense if the payoff never comes. Don't make the player responsible for resolving every curiosity.
- Compare **a quiet run** with a more talkative one. Consider choosing among eligible authored candidates with a seeded local selection; gate a follow-up on its first message having actually been delivered, and never show it before that first message. Whether to gate it on having been *read*, when to choose it, whether to suppress late payoffs, and how much spacing is enough remain open experience and engineering questions.
- Avoid sending an irrelevant backlog all at once after a quiet period. Consider a saturation check and quiet gaps for nondecision chatter; these are proposals to test, not selected limits. Separate factual due/action state from unread and from a seller's self-created promotional pressure.
- Quackpot's promotional voice may be more persistent by design. Compare welcome, genuine available event, and not-opened-today examples against human chatter at the whole-inbox level. Avoid making every promotion animate strongly forever: the selected arrival cue is finite and settles to static. Whether a Quackpot return prompt repeats, stops after opening, or varies in tone needs explicit design and eventual calibration; the owner has not selected a numeric schedule.
- Use short, specific voices rather than a large list of interchangeable “thanks” messages. An acquaintance's small observation can have more personality than a pseudo-official notice; a real service notice can be dull by contrast. Don't add an action button or hidden relationship penalty to informational chatter.

## Quackpot examples for comparison, not adopted campaign copy

1. **Launch:** retain the actual invitation/reply/ack sequence; explore energetic advertising without changing installation, rewards or first-pull rules.
2. **Event:** “The pond is getting ready for [actual event].” Only when that event is actually available and the text accurately describes it; do not manufacture a reward or deadline.
3. **Not opened today:** “The pond is open when you are.” A softer control. A more insistent alternative could emphasize a genuinely available event, but should not claim the player is neglectful or invent a closing time.
4. **Progress celebration:** if a real progression fact is established, a sender-authored message can celebrate it and invite another visit. What facts exist and when they are eligible are future integration decisions.

These are fictional marketing, not OS notifications or real network messages. A later Receipt must distinguish what was actually displayed from what the player actually did, including a displayed cue even if the player ignores it.

## Questions for future integration—not current implementation

Pin current life/social/Quackpot state and launch chronology; decide eligible source identities, message/revision IDs, stable seed/PRNG isolation, actual delivery and display evidence, save/reload/replay, pair prerequisites, expiration and no-follow-up cases. Decide how unopened-today is defined around game-day boundaries, and ensure a prompt cannot imply an unavailable app/event or override due/recovery precedence. Test quiet/short and long routes, different seeds, no duplicate delivery, inbox saturation, first/second message ordering, interruption, reduced motion and actual Receipt evidence. These proposals do not authorize runtime generation, network calls, new schedules, social penalties or production changes.

## Owner choices for the first wave

1. Pick one **neighborhood business**: Copy Counter, Lantern Laundry, or another direction.
2. Pick a **personal voice**: an unspecified acquaintance, a family contact to define, or both as future candidates.
3. Pick a **community voice**: Harbor Court alone, or also a separate library/building bulletin.
4. For Quackpot, compare a friendly return prompt with a more insistent event-grounded one; the latter needs a real event before it can be factual.

Further examples can be authored after the cast is chosen, with actual relationship/launch gates reviewed. A choice of contact voice does not approve a notification schedule or production implementation.
